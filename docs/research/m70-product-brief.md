## Explicit initial profile

Proposed first supported profile: a U.S.-only, wholly owned office/distribution group with a California parent, straightforward operational-control consolidation, a single calendar reporting year, and no acquisitions, disposals, joint ventures or ownership changes during that year. The example distributes finished non-energy-consuming office supplies and operates offices, warehouses and a small vehicle fleet. This is a design choice for a first cohort, not an observed customer, demand claim or declaration that this profile is already supported in production.

Synthetic Juniper Office Supply Group has three expected entities and three facilities, in California and Nevada. The Nevada subsidiary starts missing from the working selection but remains visible in the independently supplied corporate roster. Reporting period: 2025-01-01 through 2025-12-31; this is an accounting exercise period, not a claimed legally required filing period. An optional challenge discovers a wholly owned Canadian subsidiary operating during the same period. It remains in the expected corporate boundary and unresolved coverage register; the U.S.-only implementation profile flags a support gap and cannot remove it simply because expansion is deferred.

Required context includes parent/entity relationships, country/state, effective dates, ownership/control evidence, selected consolidation policy and rationale, reporting-period alignment, facilities and source ownership. Out-of-state operations are included when within the declared boundary. More complex ownership, foreign operations, financial-control/equity-share approaches and fiscal/short years must be identified as unsupported until separately accepted, with affected records retained for resolution. Do not silently choose an alternative consolidation policy.

## First demonstration: five meaningful interactions

1. **Reconcile the company.** Compare the corporate roster with the working boundary. Add the omitted Nevada subsidiary and show its facility/source gaps. Toggle the discovered Canadian subsidiary challenge: keep it in expected coverage and flag unsupported geography. Entity counts are roster reconciliation, never an inventory-completeness score.
2. **Screen a Scope 3 category.** Select category 14, propose “not applicable,” and try to save without rationale or evidence. Keep the prior state and explain the missing fields. Saving a nonblank rationale and a matching synthetic evidence reference yields “proposed not applicable — review pending,” not accepted exclusion or zero emissions. Also show the distinction between estimated data, no data, proposed exclusion and unknown applicability. If the editor allows estimates/exclusions too, enforce the scenario's corresponding basis/uncertainty/rationale rules; otherwise restrict this first interaction to unknown/not applicable while keeping other dispositions visible in the fixture.
3. **Inspect a conflicting period.** Open the Nevada utility evidence. Its 2024 service dates conflict with the 2025 activity assertion. Both values remain visible; no automatic replacement, coverage resolution or calculation follows. Assign an owner/task to obtain period-matched evidence.
4. **Inspect an unsupported method.** Select purchased heat or the Scope 2 market-based view. Show the source/activity and method/evidence gap, with no invented emissions number. Electricity evidence does not stand in for heat, steam, cooling or market-based contractual evidence. Both Scope 2 view labels remain distinct without suggesting both are universally required for every operation; applicability awaits reviewed rules.
5. **Export the planning gap register.** In the inline preview, inspect/copy the JSON snapshot; the standalone local demonstration downloads the same snapshot. Download the current scenario, selected boundary, expected roster, coverage decisions, synthetic evidence references and unresolved tasks. It says “Nonproduction synthetic planning register — not an emissions inventory or assurance package.” Changes in this demonstration last only for its documented local lifetime; export is not a production save. A reset restores the initial scenario.

## Coverage and language contract

Keep four separate concepts: proposed disposition (`include`, `estimate`, `exclude`, `not_applicable`, `unknown`), activity-data state, evidence state and review state. “Proposed exclusion” is retained with rationale/owner and unresolved review; “not applicable” without support is invalid; “unknown” is not zero. An estimated row identifies its proposed method and uncertainty even when no amount is calculated. Completion of a form, review of a planning decision, data coverage and emissions inventory completeness are distinct.

The Scope 1 register must screen stationary combustion, mobile combustion, fugitive emissions and process sources for the selected profile. The Scope 2 register must explicitly consider purchased electricity, heat, steam and cooling plus applicable method/evidence needs. The Scope 3 checklist names every category 1–15. For real use, each corporate category aggregates underlying entity/activity coverage and cannot override an unresolved in-scope subsidiary. The first demo can use a corporate screening row per category, with that limitation visible.

No emissions total, percent complete, “SB 253 ready,” “assured,” “fully covered” or automatic compliance verdict appears in M70. A gap count may count explicit open tasks, provided its label and derivation are clear; it is not a completeness metric. All screenshots/exports retain the synthetic and nonproduction label.
