Research cutoff: September 8, 2026. This brief supports the first Neuvetra research and architecture milestone. It distinguishes enacted statutes, the status of implementing regulations, agency enforcement guidance, and proposed requirements. It is an evidence brief rather than a determination that a particular company complies.

The most consequential finding is that the repository's August 10, 2026 SB 253 deadline and “adopted regulation” descriptions are stale. ## 3. Entity scope and applicability

### Statutory starting point

Both statutes cover specified business forms formed under California law, another U.S. state's law, District of Columbia law, or an act of Congress. They cover public and private entities. Foreign formation and a U.S. subsidiary are distinct facts: an overseas parent does not become directly covered merely because a U.S. subsidiary is covered. CARB's FAQ permits a foreign parent to prepare a consolidated report on behalf of covered U.S. subsidiaries, subject to meeting the reporting requirements.[^38532][^38533][^faq]

The revenue comparisons are strictly “in excess of,” not greater than or equal to: $1 billion for SB 253 and $500 million for SB 261. A company exactly at the threshold does not exceed it. The statutes refer to prior-fiscal-year revenue. The proposed regulation supplies a more detailed two-year approach, discussed below. There is no statutory employee-count test.[^38532][^38533]

For SB 253, Scope 1 and 2 definitions expressly include covered emissions regardless of location. California presence determines whether the entity is covered; it does not restrict the inventory to California operations. Scope 3 extends to upstream and downstream sources outside ownership/control. A company-level reporting boundary must therefore be established before summing facilities or importing supplier data.[^38532]

SB 261 expressly excludes an entity regulated by California's Department of Insurance or engaged in the insurance business in another state. SB 253's statute does not contain that same express insurance exclusion. Its treatment in the initial proposal and future workshop must not be conflated with SB 261's statutory exception.[^38533][^15day-text][^july-slides]

The following are **proposed**, as of the latest text verified here. They should be stored as a draft rule profile, not silently promoted to effective eligibility rules.[^15day-text]

The FTB source currently provides a 2025 California-sales threshold of $757,070, with an alternative test of 25% of total sales. Its 2024 figure is $735,019. These are tax-year values, not interchangeable permanent constants. The proposed CARB test references the relevant tax-law definitions; the FTB's full tax nexus guidance should not be copied wholesale into a corporate-disclosure screen.[^ftb][^rtc23101][^15day-text]

The revised text proposes exclusions for tax-exempt nonprofit/charitable organizations; specified insurance entities; federal, state and local government entities and companies more than 50% government-owned; entities whose only California activity is interstate wholesale electricity transactions; and entities whose only California business is employee compensation/payroll, including telework. Their precise status must remain attached to the initial proposal. The July workshop separately proposes changing SB 253 insurance treatment beginning in 2027, so a permanent insurance exclusion would be particularly unsafe.[^15day-text][^july-slides]

The FAQ's two-year explanation directly contradicts an inherited knowledge-base statement: FAQ question 9 says that if either of the two previous complete fiscal years falls below the threshold, the entity is outside the proposed scope for the next deadline. The repository's claim that an entity must fall below the threshold for two consecutive years to exit does not follow from this rule. At equality, the strict statutory comparison also needs explicit handling.[^faq][^38532][^38533]

There is another version conflict. November FAQ question 15 describes subsidiary revenue counting toward a unitary parent's gross receipts. ## 4. SB 253 reporting, assurance and enforcement

### Enacted structure

HSC §38532 specifies annual Scope 1/2 reporting beginning in 2026 and annual Scope 3 reporting beginning in 2027, covering the applicable prior fiscal year. Measurement/reporting must conform to GHG Protocol standards and guidance, including the Corporate Standard and Corporate Value Chain Standard. The statute allows primary and secondary Scope 3 information, including industry-average, proxy and generic data. It does not mandate that every supplier provide measured data.[^38532]

Reports may be consolidated at parent level. Required disclosures include identifying names/trade names and the assurance provider's report. Acquisitions, divestments, mergers and other structural changes must be handled consistently with the applicable GHG Protocol guidance. Reports prepared for other national or international programs may be reused only if they satisfy all SB 253 requirements; reuse is not automatic equivalence. Mandatory industrial emissions data reported under HSC §38530 may be provided with the corporate disclosure.[^38532]

The statute requires independent third-party assurance: limited assurance for Scope 1/2 beginning in 2026 and reasonable assurance beginning in 2030. For Scope 3, CARB is directed to evaluate assurance during 2026 and may establish requirements by January 1, 2027, with limited assurance beginning in 2030 in the statutory framework. Scope 3 assurance must therefore be represented with its rulemaking condition, rather than as a fully specified current engagement obligation.[^38532]

Beginning in 2033, CARB may assess alternative recognized accounting standards and adopt a qualifying alternative after the statutory process. The current section does not state a recurring five-year review cadence. A separate provision calls for a 2029 disclosure-deadline review and necessary updates by January 1, 2030.[^38532]

### First-year operational guidance versus law

The December 5, 2024 enforcement notice permits first-year emissions data derived from information an entity already possessed or was collecting when the notice issued. The discretion is conditioned on good-faith efforts, including retention of relevant data. It explicitly describes enforcement discretion rather than an interpretation changing the statutory requirements. November FAQ question 20 says first-year data may be accepted whether or not it received limited assurance.[^253-notice][^faq]

The July 27 proposed text would make two specific 2026 alternatives explicit: submit Scope 1/2 information already possessed or being collected on or before December 5, 2024, or submit a company-letterhead statement that the entity did not possess and was not collecting that information by that date. These alternatives apply only to the 2026 report. The proposed text is not permission for all companies simply to skip the first year.[^15day-text]

For the proposed 2026 report, §96076(c) determines the applicable fiscal year as follows: a fiscal year ending on or before February 1 uses the fiscal year ending in the reporting calendar year; later fiscal-year endings generally use the fiscal year ending in the previous calendar year, with an option to use the most recent preceding fiscal year where data is available. For example, a December 31 calendar-year entity would ordinarily report the year ending December 31, 2025; an entity ending January 31 could use the year ending January 31, 2026. Revenue-testing periods and inventory-reporting periods should be separate inputs.[^15day-text]

The July workshop said further guidance, an instructional video, and a voluntary online intake platform were expected by September 1, 2026. The program, resources, news, and rulemaking pages retrieved here did not establish release of those materials. This is a source-verification gap, not proof that no portal exists. The draft October 2025 spreadsheet must not be advertised as a final mandated filing schema.[^july-slides][^resources][^program][^news]

### Fees and penalty boundaries

The statutes require annual fees for both programs, even though SB 261 risk reporting is biennial. The initial proposal calculates fees from program costs and the number of covered entities; this research does not establish a final dollar amount. July modifications propose fee determination notices by December 10 starting in calendar 2026, payment within 60 calendar days of the notice date, consolidated parent payment of assessed entity fees, and five-year retention of relevant California tax records. One consolidated report is not evidence of only one entity's fee.[^38532][^38533][^15day-text][^faq]

SB 253 directs CARB to establish administrative penalties, capped at $500,000 in a reporting year, with good-faith and other circumstances considered. Scope 3 has two distinct protections: good-faith disclosures with a reasonable basis are protected against penalties for misstatements; separately, penalties for Scope 3 during 2027–2030 are limited to nonfiling. The first protection is not textually limited to that four-year window. These provisions should not be generalized into immunity for all reporting failures.[^38532]

## 6. SB 261 requirements and litigation status

SB 261 requires a climate-related financial-risk report, not simply a carbon inventory. HSC §38533 covers material physical and transition risks to immediate and longer-term financial outcomes, and measures adopted to reduce/adapt to those risks. The statutory framework is TCFD's June 2017 recommendations or a qualifying successor/equivalent; the section expressly accommodates qualifying ISSB/IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards reporting. An existing report must satisfy the relevant content and public-access conditions; “we already report under another framework” is not a blanket exemption.[^38533]

The statutory first date is January 1, 2026, followed by biennial reports made publicly accessible on the company's own website. Consolidation is allowed. If disclosures are incomplete, the statute calls for best-ability disclosures, a detailed explanation of gaps and steps toward completion. It does not impose a general independent-assurance requirement on the whole SB 261 report: §38533(b)(4) addresses when CARB may consider independently verified emissions/voluntary-mitigation claims. Statutory administrative penalties can reach $50,000 per reporting year under implementing rules, with circumstances and good faith considered.[^38533]

The verified litigation sequence is narrower than a definitive merits conclusion:

- The district court denied a preliminary injunction on August 13, 2025 and an injunction pending appeal on September 11, 2025 in case 2:24-cv-00801-ODW-PVC. The orders concern preliminary relief; they do not establish an unappealable final resolution of every challenge.[^court-appendix]
- CARB's December 1 advisory identifies the Ninth Circuit's November 18, 2025 injunction in appeal 25-5327 against SB 261 enforcement during appellate proceedings. CARB says it will not enforce failure to post/submit by January 1, 2026 and will provide further information, including an alternative date as appropriate, after the appeal is resolved.[^261-advisory]
- Supreme Court application 25A561 was withdrawn on November 19, 2025. Its docket does not show a Supreme Court merits ruling validating or invalidating either statute.[^scotus]
- The CARB docket retrieved for this research still describes voluntary submissions and the injunction. Its February 10, 2026 update date and July 1 submission-window end must not be converted into a currently open filing deadline or proof of appellate disposition.[^261-docket]

The last authenticated agency position is therefore SB 261 non-enforcement pending the appeal, with the current full appellate docket still a required refresh before operational use. This research found no basis to extend that SB 261 injunction to SB 253. Neuvetra should not say either that both laws were struck down or that the litigation is over.

## 7. Relationship to U.S. EPA reporting and California MRR

Corporate GHG Protocol inventories, EPA Part 98 reports, and California Mandatory Reporting Regulation (MRR) reports use overlapping data but different legal boundaries, methods, reporting periods and verification requirements.

EPA GHGRP primarily covers U.S. facilities in specified source categories, fuel/industrial-gas suppliers, and CO2 injection activities. The familiar 25,000 metric-ton CO2e figure is not a universal screen: §98.2 has category-based triggers, threshold-based triggers, supplier rules, and an additional 30 mmBtu/hour capacity condition for one stationary-combustion applicability route. Category-specific definitions and thresholds must be evaluated. Certain categories report regardless of the general threshold. Existing reporters also have continuing obligations and specific exit rules; one low-emissions year does not automatically end reporting.[^epa982]

EPA explains that facility-level direct emissions data does not represent a complete parent-company inventory and does not cover purchased-electricity Scope 2. Supplier reports may represent potential downstream emissions. Adding facility and supplier totals together without accounting for their different boundaries can double count. EPA performs program verification; that is not automatically the independent assurance required by SB 253.[^epa-overview]

Current §98.3(b)(6) requires the 2025 annual report by October 30, 2026. The February 27 final rule is 91 FR 9712; the June 5 correction is 91 FR 34161. The normal March 31 recurring date remains relevant for other years, subject to changes. A July 2026 EPA overview still contains a generic March 31 statement, illustrating why a current general page can be insufficient for a particular reporting-year deadline.[^epa983][^epa-deadline][^epa-correction][^epa-overview]

EPA's September 2025 reconsideration proposed removing obligations for 46 categories and suspending remaining covered Subpart W reporting until 2034. Its status in the inspected EPA materials is proposed, while current Part 98 still contains reporting requirements. Changes to the Waste Emissions Charge, vehicle GHG rules, and Part 98 are separate legal actions. None should be used as a generic “U.S. reporting was repealed” shortcut.[^epa-proposal][^epa-rulemaking][^epa-current]

California MRR is a separate industrial/facility/supplier program under HSC §38530 and Title 17. SB 253 expressly permits submission of MRR data with the corporate disclosure; it does not make an MRR report sufficient for worldwide corporate Scope 1/2/3 accounting. CARB's August 31 notice confirms OAL approval of MRR amendments effective September 1, 2026. This is a different regulatory package from the still-pending corporate-disclosure initial rule. The parallel [calculation-source brief](<C:/Users/nimab/OneDrive/Documents/ChatGPT/Neuvetra/docs/research/calculation-sources.md>) validates the final MRR order's retention of the December 11, 2014 Table A-1 GWP reference for 2021 onward. A newer federal or IPCC table must not automatically replace a program's specified vintage.[^38532][^mrr]

For data reuse, retain the original program, facility/entity boundary, reporting period, gas masses, GWP set, method, factor edition, units, exclusions, and verification status. Reconcile these into a chosen corporate boundary before producing totals. Preserve the original filed values as an immutable record and make any corporate conversion separately traceable. This is an architectural inference from the different reporting systems, not a new legal mandate.

## 9. Recommended demo behavior and update monitoring

For the initial demo, the expert answer should say which jurisdiction, reporting program, entity/facility, fiscal period, and source version it used. It should distinguish “statutory requirement,” “effective implementing rule,” “enjoined/non-enforced,” “agency guidance,” “proposal,” and “not verified.” Missing revenue, formation, tax-nexus, ownership, or source-category evidence should produce an unresolved assessment with the exact missing facts, rather than a guessed exemption.

The deterministic accounting layer should calculate from typed activity data, explicit units, approved factors and a recorded GWP set. The research assistant can identify relevant requirements, explain source conflicts, and request evidence. Its prose should not silently change factors, units, boundaries, or totals. A report-ready result needs a trace from activity evidence through each conversion to gas-level and CO2e totals; an assurance-ready evidence package additionally needs independent review.

Recommended monitoring targets follow. This is a proposed operating process, not a scheduled automation or a subscription created in this task.
