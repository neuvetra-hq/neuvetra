## Purpose

This knowledge base covers Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions accounting and reporting for businesses operating under EU and California regulatory frameworks. It serves both sustainability professionals and business owners — from "what does this regulation require?" to "how do I calculate my Scope 3 Category 1 emissions?"

## Current Coverage

**Jurisdictions:** EU (CSRD, ESRS E1, EU ETS), California (SB 253, SB 261, CARB MRR), Global (GHG Protocol, IPCC, PCAF)
**Frameworks:** GHG Protocol Corporate Standard, GHG Protocol Scope 3 Standard, GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard v1.0 (eff. 2027-01-01), PCAF Financed Emissions Standard (Part A, Third Edition), IPCC 2006 Guidelines + 2019 Refinement
**Scopes:** 1, 2, 3 (all 15 Scope 3 categories) + CO2 removals + product carbon storage (LSRS)

## Thesis

The global GHG accounting system is architecturally unified around the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard. All major regulatory frameworks — EU CSRD/ESRS E1, California SB 253, CARB MRR, EU ETS — reference the Scope 1/2/3 taxonomy and the five accounting principles established by the GHG Protocol Initiative (WRI + WBCSD). Understanding the Corporate Standard is therefore the prerequisite for understanding every downstream regulation.

The key structural insight is the **two-boundary system**: companies first set an organizational boundary (which entities are in scope, using equity share, financial control, or operational control) and then set an operational boundary (which emission sources are Scope 1, 2, or 3). Every GHG calculation question ultimately reduces to one of these two boundary questions.

Scope 3 is the frontier: optional under the base standard, increasingly mandatory under new regulations (CSRD, SB 253), and representing the majority of most companies' footprints. The Scope 3 Standard and Calculation Guidance are now ingested and fully represented in the wiki through the scope-3-categories concept page and expanded methodology documentation.

Scope 2 has been materially deepened by the 2015 Scope 2 Guidance. The critical practical implication for businesses is **dual reporting** — most companies must now maintain two separate scope 2 totals. The market-based method creates significant complexity around certificate procurement (RECs, GOs) and residual mix factors that vary substantially by country, especially in the EU.

**California regulatory layer (now partially ingested):** The wiki can now answer California compliance questions for both the corporate and facility levels. A key structural insight from this layer: California operates a **two-track system** for GHG accountability — CARB MRR for verified facility-level Scope 1 data (linked to Cap-and-Trade enforcement), and SB 253 for corporate-level Scope 1/2/3 public disclosure (linked to investor transparency). The same California-operating company may face both obligations. The CARB MRR is the older, more deeply technical instrument; SB 253 is broader in corporate scope but less operationally prescriptive.

**The EU regulatory layer is now the deepest of any jurisdiction in this wiki.** With the full ESRS Set 1 ingested (2026-04-25) and the EFRAG May 2024 implementation guidance suite (IG 1 Materiality, IG 2 Value Chain, IG 3 Datapoints) added the same day, the wiki contains all 12 ESRS standards (cross-cutting + topical environmental + social + governance), the EU ETS legislation + MRR + AVR, CSRD itself, and the principal preparer-facing playbook. The defining structural insight is **double materiality** — the EU's deliberate departure from the financial-only materiality used by the SEC and ISSB. ESRS 1 codifies the methodology; ESRS 2 provides the disclosure mechanics; the 10 topical standards layer onto material topics; phase-in (size-conditional + first-year + 3-year qualitative-only) is the critical practical layer.

**EFRAG implementation guidance:** IG 1 + IG 2 together drive the new [[methodologies/double-materiality-assessment|Double Materiality Assessment]] methodology page — the wiki's first end-to-end practitioner playbook for the DMA (four steps; severity-likelihood threshold matrices; financial-control vs operational-control boundary distinction; tiered value-chain data approach; Seren Group worked example for Cat 15 investee mechanics). IG 3 quantifies the disclosure surface (≈1,144 datapoints across ESRS 2 + 11 topical standards: 161 always-mandatory, 622 materiality-subject, 269 voluntary) and provides a phase-in cross-walk. With IG 1/2/3, the wiki can now answer not just *what* CSRD requires but *how* a preparer actually does it.

**EU regulatory perimeter completed (2026-04-25):** Three further inbox files brought the EU stack to a working whole — **Directive (EU) 2023/959** (the "Fit for 55" ETS revision) materially reshapes [[regulations/eu-ets|EU ETS]] with a Phase 4 cap acceleration (LRF 4.3% → 4.4%, two one-time rebasings), maritime inclusion (40/70/100% phase-in 2025/2026/2027), and a brand-new **ETS2** parallel system covering buildings/road-transport/additional-sector fuel suppliers (permits 2025, trading 2027, first surrender 2028); **CBAM (Reg (EU) 2023/956)** as the carbon-leakage complement that mirrors EU ETS pricing on imports of cement, electricity, fertilisers, iron/steel, aluminium, hydrogen, with definitive obligations and first surrender 2027-09-30 against 2026 emissions, and a CBAM-factor phase-out of EU ETS free allocation 2026–2034 in the same six sectors; and the **EU Taxonomy Regulation (EU) 2020/852** plus its **Climate Delegated Act (Reg (EU) 2021/2139)** establishing the legal definition of "green" investment in the EU — six environmental objectives, four-part Article 3 alignment test (substantial contribution + DNSH + minimum safeguards + activity-specific TSCs), Article 8 turnover/CapEx/OpEx KPI disclosures (colocated with ESRS E1–E5 in the CSRD environmental information block), and operative thresholds including the universal <100 gCO2e/kWh life-cycle threshold for electricity/heat. The Climate DA is the de facto bridge between EU ETS verified emissions and EU sustainable-finance disclosures: per-product Taxonomy benchmarks for cement, iron and steel, aluminium, and chemicals are derived from EU ETS Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/447, making EU ETS-regulated installations the canonical data source for Article 8 alignment KPIs in those sectors.

**California regulatory perimeter extended (2026-04-25):** The **California Low Carbon Fuel Standard (Title 17 CCR §§95480–95503)**, OAL-approved 2025-06-27 and effective 2025-07-01, is now in the wiki as a CARB program adopted under AB 32 authority. It is distinct from and additive to Cap-and-Trade for refiners, gas utilities, hydrogen producers, biomethane producers, and electrical distribution utilities supplying transport electricity. Material caveat for the chatbot: LCFS-reported CI values are full fuel-cycle (well-to-wheels) and are NOT directly substitutable into corporate GHG Protocol Scope 1 or Scope 3 Cat 11 inventories without methodological reconciliation — flagged for any future Terrascope users serving California fuel-supplier clients.
