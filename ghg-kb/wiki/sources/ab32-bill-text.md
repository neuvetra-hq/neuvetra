## Key Takeaways

1. **Root enabling statute.** AB 32 is the statutory parent of CARB MRR (§38530) and Cap-and-Trade (§38570), and the institutional foundation for SB 253/SB 261. All California GHG compliance programs derive authority from or build on this law.
2. **1990-level-by-2020 target achieved; authority continues.** The 2020 GHG target has been met. AB 32's regulatory authority remains operative for ongoing reductions and new program development.
3. **"Market-based compliance mechanism" is a statutory term.** HSC §38570 defines it as either (1) a declining-cap allowance system or (2) GHG exchanges/credits meeting equivalency requirements. This statutory definition frames what Cap-and-Trade legally is and limits its scope.
4. **Environmental justice provisions are hard law, not policy.** The EJ advisory committee (§38591), leakage minimization (§38562), and community workshop requirements are statutory mandates that constrain how CARB may design implementing regulations.
5. **Cost-of-implementation fee (§38597) is separate from auction proceeds.** The §38597 fee on ~250 major GHG sources funds CARB's regulatory work. Auction proceeds go to the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund and fund climate investments — two distinct revenue streams.
