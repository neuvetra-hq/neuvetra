## Key Takeaways

### 1. Sets out the legally binding monitoring rules for ALL EU ETS emissions
The MRR is the technical implementing regulation that operationalises the high-level monitoring requirements in Directive 2003/87/EC. Every EU ETS operator's verified annual emissions report must be produced according to a monitoring plan compliant with this regulation. Coverage as of the consolidated 2025 version:
- **ETS1 stationary installations** (Annex I to Directive 2003/87/EC) — from 1 January 2021
- **Aviation** — including monitoring of **non-CO₂ aviation effects from 1 January 2025** (Article 1(ii))
- **ETS2 fuel suppliers** (Annex III activities) — released fuel amounts for buildings, road transport, and additional sectors

### 2. Tier-based methodology system
Operators must apply **tiers** — defined in Annexes II and V — to determine activity data and each calculation factor (net calorific value, emission factor, oxidation factor, conversion factor, carbon content, fossil fraction, biomass fraction, etc.). Higher tiers = higher accuracy = stricter data requirements.

| Tier rank | Method (illustrative) |
|---|---|
| Tier 4 | Continuous measurement / fuel-specific lab analyses |
| Tier 3 | Country-specific or installation-specific factors with prescribed analyses |
| Tier 2 | EU/IPCC type-specific defaults |
| Tier 1 | Generic IPCC defaults |

The required minimum tier depends on **installation category** AND **source-stream classification**.

### 3. Installation category and uncertainty thresholds (Article 19, Article 22)
Installations are classified by annual fossil CO₂ emissions:

| Category | Annual emissions | Required activity-data uncertainty (whole installation) |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | ≤ 50,000 t CO₂/year | ≤ 7.5% |
| **B** | 50,001–500,000 t CO₂/year | ≤ 5.0% |
| **C** | > 500,000 t CO₂/year | ≤ 2.5% |

Higher categories require higher tiers. Category C installations must apply the highest tier in Annex II for major source streams; lower tiers may be permitted only with competent-authority approval and an improvement plan.

### 4. Source-stream classification (Article 19)
Each source stream within an installation is classified to determine tier flexibility:
- **Major source streams** — must use the required highest tier (subject to category-based steps down)
- **Minor source streams** — collectively account for ≤ 5,000 t fossil CO₂/year or ≤ 10% of total emissions (max 100,000 t); lower tiers permitted with technical-feasibility justification
- **De minimis source streams** — collectively ≤ 1,000 t fossil CO₂/year or ≤ 2% of total (max 20,000 t); operators may use **conservative no-tier estimates** including the energy balance method

### 5. Two monitoring methodologies (Article 21)
- **Calculation-based** (default): `Emissions = Activity Data × Calculation Factor`. Includes the **standard methodology** (Articles 24, 26–35) and the **mass balance methodology** (Article 25, used for complex chemical processes — input C – output C – stored C = emitted C).
- **Measurement-based (CEMS)**: continuous emission measurement systems where the operator demonstrates equivalent or better accuracy. Subject to Article 41–46 requirements; results must be corroborated by supporting calculations.

### 6. Zero-rating mechanism — sustainability-linked
The 2024 and 2025 amendments introduced a structured "zero-rating" framework (Article 3(23c)) for the carbon content of fuels:
- **Biomass** — zero-rated if it meets sustainability + GHG savings criteria of Article 29(2)–(7) and (10) of the Renewable Energy Directive (EU) 2018/2001
- **RFNBO and RCF** — zero-rated if compliant with GHG savings criteria (RED II Article 29a)
- **Synthetic low-carbon fuels** — zero-rated if ≥ 70% GHG reduction vs fossil comparator AND prior surrender of allowances for any captured carbon used to produce them (Directive (EU) 2024/1788)

Without zero-rating, all carbon — including biomass-derived — is counted in the emission factor. This is a tightening of earlier MRR practice where biomass was automatically treated as zero-emission.

### 7. Annexes carry the technical detail
- **Annex II** — tier definitions for activity data and each calculation factor by source-stream type (combustion, mass balance, process emissions)
- **Annex III** — default factors (emission factors, NCVs) for standard commercial fuels (Table 1)
- **Annex IIIa / IIIb** — non-CO₂ aviation effects monitoring
- **Annex IV** — sector-specific monitoring rules
- **Annex V** — minimum tiers for category A installations and commercial standard fuels
- **Annex VI** — reference methods for analysis, sampling, calibration
- **Annex VII** — minimum frequency of analyses
- **Annex VIII** — measurement-based methodology

### 8. Monitoring plan as legally binding instrument (Article 12)
Every operator must have a **monitoring plan approved by the national competent authority** before reporting. The plan defines:
- All source streams and emission sources
- The tier applied to each parameter
- Sampling, analysis, and calibration procedures
- Data-flow activities and control system
- Uncertainty assessment

Changes to the plan must be notified to and (for significant changes) approved by the competent authority. Temporary deviations require applying the **highest achievable tier or a conservative no-tier approach** until the plan is restored (Article 23).
