## Key Takeaways

1. **Establishes the Scope 1 / 2 / 3 taxonomy.** The three-scope framework is defined here: Scope 1 (direct emissions from owned/controlled sources), Scope 2 (indirect from purchased electricity), Scope 3 (all other indirect). Every major downstream regulation references this taxonomy.

2. **Three organizational boundary approaches.** Companies choose equity share, financial control, or operational control to define what falls inside their inventory. The revised edition allows reporting under a single approach; the first edition required both equity and control approaches.

3. **Five accounting principles.** Relevance, Completeness, Consistency, Transparency, and Accuracy are the quality criteria all GHG inventories must satisfy. These principles are cited across virtually every other GHG standard and regulation.

4. **Scope 2 boundary revision from first edition.** Electricity purchased for resale was moved from Scope 2 to Scope 3 in this revised edition to prevent two companies from double-counting the same emissions in the same scope.

5. **Base year approach for tracking.** Fixed base year methodology established — including recalculation triggers (structural changes from acquisitions, divestitures, outsourcing) and a new Chapter 11 on setting GHG reduction targets (added in this revised edition).
