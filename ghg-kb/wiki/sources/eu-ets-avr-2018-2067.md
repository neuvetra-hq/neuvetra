## Key Takeaways

### 1. Companion to MRR — completes the EU ETS MRV cycle
The MRR (→ [[sources/eu-ets-mrr-2018-2066|MRR 2018/2066]]) tells operators **how to monitor and report**; the AVR tells operators and verifiers **how the report is verified and who is qualified to verify it**. The two regulations operate in lockstep — definitions in AVR Article 3 are layered on top of MRR Article 3.

### 2. Scope expanded 2018 → 2025
The current consolidated text covers verification of three reporting streams (Article 2):
- **(a)** ETS1 stationary installation and aircraft operator emissions (from 1 January 2019), plus baseline/new-entrant data and free-allocation activity level reports
- **(b)** **ETS2 regulated entities** — fuel suppliers reporting under Article 30f of the ETS Directive (from 1 January 2025)
- **(c)** **Non-CO₂ aviation effects** — verified separately by aircraft operators (from 1 January 2025)
- Climate-neutrality plan compliance verification was added by amendment 2024/1321

### 3. Reasonable assurance is the required standard
A verifier must conclude with **reasonable assurance** — defined as "a high but not absolute level of assurance, expressed positively in the verification opinion" — that the report is free from material misstatement. Verifiers must apply professional skepticism and document the basis for the opinion. The output is a binary verification opinion: **satisfactory** or **non-satisfactory** (with the latter triggering follow-up by the competent authority).

### 4. Materiality level — fixed thresholds (Article 23)
The AVR sets quantitative materiality thresholds rather than leaving them to professional judgement:

| Report type | Materiality level |
|---|---|
| ETS1 emissions report — Category A or B installation | **5%** of total reported emissions |
| ETS1 emissions report — Category C installation | **2%** of total reported emissions |
| Aviation emissions report — annual emissions ≤ 500 kt fossil CO₂ | 5% |
| Aviation emissions report — annual emissions > 500 kt fossil CO₂ | 2% |
| Non-CO₂ aviation effects report | 5% of total aggregated CO₂(e) reported |
| Baseline / new-entrant / annual activity level report | 5% of relevant data point |
| Climate-neutrality report | 5% of relevant target metric |

Note: misstatements *below* the materiality level may still be flagged as material based on size, nature, or circumstances (Article 22(3)) — materiality is not a strict floor.

### 5. Verifier accreditation — national bodies under Regulation (EC) 765/2008
Verifiers must be **accredited by a national accreditation body** (NAB) of a Member State under Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 and the AVR. Accreditation is granted per **scope of activities** (defined by Annex I to the AVR — covers different industrial sectors and aviation). Once accredited in any Member State, a verifier benefits from **mutual recognition** across the EU.

The AVR also lays down peer evaluation procedures for NABs themselves (Articles 49–63), ensuring consistent accreditation standards across Member States.

### 6. Verification process — structured 5-step engagement (Chapter II)
| Step | Article(s) | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-engagement | 8 | Pre-contractual obligations; allocation of resources and verification team |
| Strategic analysis | 9 | Understand the operator, its installation, its monitoring plan and methodology, prior history |
| Risk analysis | 10 | Identify and assess inherent risks, control risks, detection risks |
| Verification plan and site visit | 11–22 | Test data flow, control system, source data, sampling; on-site visit (waivers possible) |
| Verification report | 27 | Conclude verification opinion; statement of misstatements, irregularities, recommendations for improvement |

### 7. Independence and competence requirements (Chapter III)
Verifiers must demonstrate independence (no conflicts of interest with the verified operator), maintain a documented quality management system, and ensure verification team members hold the required competencies. The verifier may not have provided consultancy services to the operator that could affect the verifier's independence in the prior 2 years.

### 8. ETS2 verification — distinct from stationary installations
For Article 30f regulated entities (fuel suppliers under ETS2), the AVR (Articles 43a–43o) lays down a parallel but lighter verification regime. The reporting object is **released fuel amounts** rather than facility emissions; verification still requires reasonable assurance and a verification opinion, with materiality at 5% (regulated entity) or 2% above a threshold size.
