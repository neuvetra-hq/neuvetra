## Key Takeaways

1. **Double materiality is the foundation; impact perspective is the natural starting point** (IG 1 §2.1, Summary key points 1–2). A matter is material if either dimension (impact or financial) is material; the two are interconnected. ESRS 1 ¶43–51 codify the criteria. The undertaking's **entire value chain** (upstream + downstream) is in scope of the assessment, not only own operations (Summary key point 2).

2. **A four-step structured assessment process is illustrative, not mandated** (IG 1 §3, ¶63–99). The four steps are: **Step A — Understand context** (business model, value chain actors, affected stakeholders, regulatory landscape); **Step B — Identification** of long-list IROs using ESRS 1 AR 16 plus entity-specific matters; **Step C — Assessment and determination** applying severity/likelihood criteria for impacts and likelihood × magnitude for financial risks/opportunities, with thresholds; **Step D — Reporting** via ESRS 2 IRO-1, SBM-3, IRO-2. Undertakings design a fit-for-purpose process; no single methodology is prescribed.

3. **Impact-materiality severity criteria: scale, scope, irremediable character (+ likelihood for potentials)** (IG 1 §3.6, ¶113–159). For **actual** negative impacts, severity alone determines materiality. For **potential** negative impacts, severity AND likelihood. For **positive** impacts, scale and scope (+ likelihood if potential). Severity is assessed *from the perspective of the affected people / environment*, not the undertaking. For potential **human-rights** impacts, severity takes precedence over likelihood. IG 1 provides illustrative severity-likelihood matrices (Figures 4–5).

4. **Financial-materiality thresholds are entity-determined and may be qualitative or quantitative** (IG 1 §3.7, ¶126–137). ESRS does **not** prescribe a single quantitative threshold. Undertakings may use absolute monetary thresholds (EUR amounts) or relative thresholds (% of revenue, assets, costs, equity, cash flows). Assessment considers likelihood × magnitude over short / medium / long-term horizons. Critically, financial materiality includes effects **not yet reflected on the balance sheet** — dependencies on natural / human / social resources, reputational risks affecting cost of capital. Thresholds must be disclosed (under IRO-1) and applied consistently (ESRS 2 ¶53, ¶59).

5. **Stakeholder engagement informs both perspectives; disclosure of *whether and how* engagement occurred is required, but the method is open** (IG 1 §3.5, FAQ 15–17, ¶101–112). Affected stakeholders (workers, communities, suppliers in own operations and value chain) are central to **impact** materiality. Users of sustainability statements (investors, lenders, business partners) inform **financial** materiality. Engagement may occur at any step (A, B, C) and may piggy-back on existing business processes (HR surveys, supplier audits, customer NPS). Due-diligence frameworks — UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises — are explicitly named as informing both dimensions.
