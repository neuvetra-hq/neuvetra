## Key Takeaways

1. **Value chain reporting is concentration-driven, not actor-by-actor** (IG 2 §2.1–2.2, ¶28–35). ESRS 1 ¶63 defines the value chain as material IROs connected through direct/indirect business relationships upstream and downstream. The undertaking is **not** required to report on each tier-1, -2, or -N actor — it must report on **material concentrations and hotspots**: areas of severe actual / potential impacts on people or environment, and relationships critical to the undertaking's dependencies. Leverage and influence affect *data-collection ability*, **not** the materiality of an IRO (IG 2 ¶32). Business relationships are not limited to direct contractual ties; the chain extends through the entire upstream/downstream network.

2. **Reporting boundary = financial-statement consolidation perimeter; value chain extends *beyond* it** (IG 2 §2.3, ¶34–57). The "reporting undertaking" for ESRS purposes is the same parent + subsidiary group as the financial statements (full financial-control consolidation). Value chain disclosures extend to material IROs in business relationships **outside** that perimeter. **For environmental matters (E1, E2, E4)**, the undertaking additionally reports 100% of GHG / pollution from sites or entities under **operational control** as a *separate disclosure* (ESRS E1 ¶50(b)) — even where not financially controlled (associates, joint ventures, contractual arrangements where the undertaking directs operating activities). **For social standards (S1–S4)**, operational control is **not** the relevant trigger; social DRs follow contractual / stakeholder relationships. IG 2 provides a decision tree (¶59) and the **Seren Group worked example** (¶141–150) covering treatment of subsidiaries, joint arrangements, and associates under both control regimes.

3. **Cat 15 (Investments) treatment for financial institutions and corporate investors clarified** (IG 2 FAQ 5–7, ¶66–72, ¶148–150). Emissions of associates, joint ventures, and unconsolidated investments where the undertaking has **significant influence but not financial control** are reported as **Scope 3 Category 15 (Investments)**, not as Scope 1 or Scope 2. The reporting boundary follows the financial-statement consolidation perimeter; investees outside that perimeter flow into Cat 15. The IG 2 Company A / Company B fabric scenario (¶148–150) walks through the numerical mechanics for an associate (equity-method) investment.

4. **Tiered data approach with proportional "reasonable effort"** (IG 2 §3, FAQ 8–9, ¶152–162). When value-chain data is material, the order of preference is: (a) **direct data** from major tier-1 suppliers / customers (questionnaires, contractual data, audits); (b) **sector-average / regional / country benchmarks**; (c) **product-level proxies and estimates**. ESRS 1 ¶69 permits sector averages and proxies after "reasonable effort." IG 2 operationalises *reasonable effort* as **proportional**: there is no expectation to quantify impacts of thousands of tier-3 suppliers, but tier-1 major suppliers and customers should be engaged. The undertaking discloses basis, accuracy level, and planned improvements (ESRS 2 ¶10). Worked example: a beverage company estimates electricity consumed by customer-owned cooling fridges rather than measuring each unit (¶161).

5. **Three-year value-chain transitional provision: year 1 may be qualitative-only, with documented effort** (IG 2 §2.7–2.8, ¶22–24). ESRS 1 §10.2 permits an undertaking to **omit metric-level value-chain information not reasonably available** for the first 3 reporting years, provided it discloses the efforts made, why information could not be obtained, and plans for the future. Qualitative descriptions of value-chain IROs are required from year 1. Entity-specific disclosures have a separate first-year relief (ESRS 1 §10.1). **LSME cap:** an undertaking cannot require value-chain SMEs to provide data beyond what future LSME standards will require — limiting compliance push-down (IG 2 ¶94).
