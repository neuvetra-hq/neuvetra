## Key Takeaways

1. **Double counting within scope 3 is intentional and acceptable.** The same physical emission may appear in multiple companies' scope 3 inventories — for example, transportation emissions appearing in both the shipper's Category 4 and the receiver's Category 9. This is by design: each entity has different opportunities to reduce emissions. However, scope 3 inventories **must not be summed across companies** to estimate total regional or sectoral emissions, as this would double-count.

2. **Avoided emissions are outside scope 3 — always.** Category 11 (Use of sold products) captures the actual use-phase emissions of sold products over their lifetime. It does not capture the emissions that are *avoided* compared to an alternative product (e.g., a wind turbine manufacturer does not get credit for emissions avoided vs. fossil fuel generation). Avoided emissions claims require a separate project accounting methodology and must be reported outside the scopes. Companies may not deduct avoided emissions from their scope 3 total.

3. **Data collection is explicitly iterative.** In early years, companies should use secondary data (EEIO, industry averages) across most categories and progressively replace it with primary data for priority activities. Low data quality in the first years is acceptable and expected; the requirement is to describe data quality, not to achieve primary data across all categories immediately.

4. **Prioritization framework for data collection** — three legitimate bases: (a) estimated emissions magnitude — screen all categories with rough estimates, rank by size; (b) financial significance — rank by spend or revenue (with caution: financial value and emissions don't always correlate); (c) other criteria — stakeholder relevance, company influence, risk exposure.

5. **Double counting of reductions is also acceptable for reporting, but not for market instruments.** When two entities both claim credit for reducing the same scope 3 emission, this is acceptable as long as it is disclosed. However, when reductions take on monetary value (offset credits, market instruments), exclusive ownership must be established through contractual agreements to avoid double crediting.
