## Key Takeaways

1. **The Climate Delegated Act is the operative rulebook for EU Taxonomy mitigation and adaptation.** Where Regulation (EU) 2020/852 sets the framework — six objectives, four-part test, Article 8 disclosure — this Delegated Act fills in the activity-level **technical screening criteria (TSCs)** that determine whether a specific economic activity qualifies as substantially contributing to climate change mitigation (Annex I) or adaptation (Annex II), plus the activity-specific DNSH criteria for the other four environmental objectives. Without this Act, the parent Regulation has no operative content for objectives (a) and (b).

2. **Activities are NACE-coded and grouped into ~13 macro-sectors.** Annex I (mitigation) covers Forestry, Environmental protection, Manufacturing, Energy, Water/sewerage/waste, Transport, Construction and real estate, Information and communication, and Professional/scientific/technical activities — roughly 90 numbered activities. Annex II (adaptation) covers the same sectors plus Financial and insurance activities (Section 10 — non-life insurance and reinsurance), Education, Human health and social work, and Arts/entertainment/recreation — adaptation criteria reach a broader sectoral surface because almost any activity can implement adaptation solutions, while only some activities directly reduce GHGs at scale.

3. **TSCs are quantitative wherever possible — the headline thresholds for GHG accounting are:**
   - **Power generation** (Sections 4.1–4.8, 4.13, 4.17–4.24, 4.28, 4.29): life-cycle GHG emissions **below 100 g CO2e/kWh** is the universal substantial-contribution ceiling for electricity, co-generation, and most heat/cool generation. Renewables (solar PV, CSP, wind, ocean, hydropower, geothermal) qualify automatically subject to project-level life-cycle assessment; bioenergy, hydropower, geothermal, and renewable gaseous/liquid fuels must demonstrate the threshold per Recommendation 2013/179/EU (PEF), ISO 14067:2018, or ISO 14064-1:2018.
   - **Fossil gas power generation** (Section 4.29 — *transitional under Article 10(2), added by M1*): either life-cycle <100 g CO2e/kWh **or** for facilities permitted by 2030-12-31, direct emissions <270 g CO2e/kWh **or** average <550 kg CO2e/kW over 20 years, with seven additional conditions (no renewable alternative, replaces dirtier plant, ≤15% capacity uplift, switch to renewable/low-carbon gas by 2035-12-31, ≥55% lifetime emissions reduction, etc.). Compliance is independently third-party verified annually.
   - **Cement** (Section 3.7): grey clinker <0.722 tCO2e/t clinker; cement (or alternative binder) <0.469 tCO2e/t cement — derived from EU ETS benchmarks (10% best-performing installations 2016–2017) per Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/447, scaled by clinker-to-cement ratio of 0.65.
   - **Iron and steel** (Section 3.9): per-product thresholds — hot metal 1.331 tCO2e/t, sintered ore 0.163 tCO2e/t, coke 0.144 tCO2e/t, EAF high-alloy 0.266 tCO2e/t, EAF carbon steel 0.209 tCO2e/t — also EU ETS-benchmark derived.
   - **Aluminium** (Section 3.8): primary aluminium ≤1.484 tCO2e/t (until 2025) plus indirect emissions ≤100 g CO2e/kWh (electricity used); secondary aluminium qualifies categorically.
   - **Hydrogen** (Section 3.10): life-cycle GHG savings ≥73.4% vs fossil-fuel comparator (i.e., <3 tCO2e/tH2); for hydrogen-based synthetic fuels ≥70%.
   - **Light vehicles M1/N1** (Section 6.5): tailpipe CO2 <50 g CO2/km until 2025; **0 g CO2/km from 2026** (mirrors EU CO2 standards Regulation (EU) 2019/631).
   - **Buses, trains, ships, aircraft** (Sections 6.1–6.12): zero direct (tailpipe) CO2 emissions, with transitional rules for vessels capable of running on zero-emission fuels from 2026-01-01 (M2 amendment).
   - **Buildings** (Section 7.1): new buildings — net primary energy demand at least **10% below NZEB** national threshold; large new buildings (>5,000 m²) must publish life-cycle Global Warming Potential in kgCO2e/m² of useful internal floor area for each life-cycle stage.

4. **Three categories of contributing activity are operationalised activity-by-activity:**
   - **Own-performance** — default; activity directly meets the substantial-contribution criterion.
   - **Enabling activities (Article 16)** — flagged in the Annex per activity (e.g., manufacture of renewable energy technologies in Section 3.1; transmission and distribution of electricity in Section 4.9; data-driven solutions for GHG reductions in Section 8.2). For Section 4.9 (T&D) the enabling test includes a system-level "67% of newly enabled generation capacity below 100 g CO2e/kWh" rule on a five-year rolling average.
   - **Transitional activities (Article 10(2))** — flagged explicitly per activity (e.g., manufacture of cement, iron and steel, fossil gaseous fuel power generation, electricity from nuclear in existing installations). Transitional activities require best-in-sector emission performance and no carbon lock-in.

5. **DNSH is implemented through five Appendices to Annex I and four to Annex II** (Appendices A–E in Annex I; A–D in Annex II). Each in-scope activity references back to the relevant Appendix:
   - **Appendix A — Generic DNSH criteria for climate change adaptation:** every activity must perform a climate-risk and vulnerability assessment using best-available climate projections (RCP scenarios) and implement adaptation solutions for material physical climate risks identified.
   - **Appendix B — Water and marine resources DNSH:** water use efficiency thresholds (e.g., water appliance specifications in Appendix E for buildings); references to Water Framework Directive 2000/60/EC, Marine Strategy Framework Directive 2008/56/EC, Drinking Water Directive (EU) 2020/2184.
   - **Appendix C — Pollution prevention and control DNSH:** chemicals/substances restrictions referencing REACH (Regulation (EC) 1907/2006), POPs Regulation, Mercury Regulation, Stockholm Convention.
   - **Appendix D — Biodiversity and ecosystems DNSH:** EIA per Directive 2011/92/EU, Habitats Directive 92/43/EEC, Birds Directive 2009/147/EC; protected-area screens.
   - **Appendix E (Annex I only) — Technical specifications for water appliances:** specific litre/min and litre/flush thresholds for taps, showers, urinals, WCs.
   - On top of generic Appendix references, many activities carry **activity-specific DNSH overlays** (e.g., refrigerant GWP ≤675 for heat pumps in Section 4.16; outdoor sound power level limits; BAT-AEL ranges per Industrial Emissions Directive 2010/75/EU for combustion installations).

6. **Article 2a (added by M1) requires the Commission to review the dates in Sections 4.27–4.31** (the nuclear and fossil gas transitional activities) when conducting the Article 19(5) Taxonomy Regulation review. The 2030-12-31 fossil gas permitting deadline and the 2035-12-31 switch-to-renewable-gas deadline are accordingly subject to Commission re-examination.

7. **Independent third-party verification is mandated for the most contested transitional activities** (fossil gas Sections 4.29–4.31; nuclear Sections 4.27–4.28). Annual verifier reports must be published and transmitted to the Commission, certifying direct GHG emission levels and assessing trajectory compliance.
