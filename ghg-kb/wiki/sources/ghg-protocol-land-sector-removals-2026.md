## Metadata

- **Publisher:** GHG Protocol Initiative (WRI + WBCSD)
- **Approved:** 1 October 2025 (Independent Standards Board)
- **Published:** 30 January 2026
- **Effective date:** 1 January 2027
- **Review date:** 2030
- **Pages:** ~130
- **File:** `raw/standards/global-standard-ghg-protocol-land-sector-removals-2026.pdf`
- **Classification:** standards
- **Scope of v1.0:** Agriculture and CO2 removal technologies. **Forestry is NOT covered in v1.0** — pending a Request for Information in 2026 and a future version. Non-productive lands (forest or non-forest) on which Requirement 7 would have applied are likewise deferred.
- **Status:** In force; supersedes prior land-sector and removals-specific guidance (incl. the *Agricultural Guidance*). Used in combination with the Corporate Standard, Scope 3 Standard, and (optionally) the Product Standard and Project Protocol.

## Key Takeaways

1. **First binding GHG Protocol standard for land sector and CO2 removals.** Built through a 2020–2025 multi-stakeholder process under the GHG Protocol Independent Standards Board. The Standard contains **32 requirements**, organized into 4 parts and 20 chapters; the parallel *Guidance* document provides implementation examples and case studies.

2. **New required accounting categories beyond the standard scopes.** Companies meeting the applicability criterion (significant land-sector activities in operations or value chain) shall account for: **land use change emissions** (Ch. 7), **land use** and **land carbon leakage** (Ch. 8), **land management net biogenic CO2 emissions** (Ch. 9), **land management production emissions** (Ch. 10), and **biogenic product CH4/N2O emissions** (Ch. 11). Each is reported separately by accounting category and scope. (→ [[concepts/land-use-change-emissions|Land Use Change Emissions]])

3. **Optional categories: removals, gross CO2 fluxes, product carbon storage, reversals.** Companies choosing to report CO2 removals must apply Part 2.2 (Ch. 12–14) covering land management removals (LMR), CO2 capture with geologic storage (GS), and TCDR-based product carbon storage. **Reversals** track emissions from previously-reported removals when the carbon pool exits the reporting boundary — a permanence safeguard. (→ [[concepts/co2-removals|CO2 Removals]])

4. **Five-tier spatial-boundary framework tied to traceability.** From least to most precise: Global → Jurisdiction → Sourcing region → Land Management Unit (LMU) → Harvested area. The applicable scope-3 spatial boundary for a given product volume depends on the **physical traceability** the company can demonstrate. Sourcing region, LMU, and harvested-area boundaries require traceability systems (Requirement 8). The same spatial boundary must be applied to all accounting categories for a given product volume.

5. **Chain-of-custody models — book-and-claim is excluded.** To demonstrate physical traceability, only the following models are eligible: identity preservation, segregation, controlled blending, mass balance (subject to defined transfer boundaries and reconciliation periods within a single country and sourcing region), or mixes thereof. **Book-and-claim approaches and non-proportional mass-balance allocations cannot demonstrate physical traceability** — companies relying on these can only use coarser spatial boundaries (jurisdiction or global).

6. **GHG Protocol's seven principles — Permanence is added.** This Standard introduces **Permanence** as an additional accounting principle alongside the five Corporate-Standard principles (Relevance, Completeness, Consistency, Transparency, Accuracy) and Conservativeness (carried over from the Project Protocol). Permanence reflects the time-sensitive nature of biological and geological carbon stores. (→ [[concepts/ghg-accounting-principles|GHG Accounting and Reporting Principles]])

7. **Conformance is layered onto the Corporate + Scope 3 Standards.** Companies reporting under this Standard shall also meet the Corporate Standard and Scope 3 Standard requirements, including: organizational boundary consolidation; complete scope 1/2/3 reporting (including all 15 scope 3 categories); the seven required GHGs (CO2, CH4, N2O, HFCs, PFCs, SF6, NF3); 100-year GWP values from the most recent IPCC AR; reporting period disclosure; and minimum boundaries for each scope 3 category. For agricultural value chains, the scope-3 minimum boundary explicitly includes (a) life-cycle GHG emissions of agricultural inputs (feed, fertilizer, pesticides, herbicides) and (b) emissions from food loss and waste prior to purchase.

8. **Scope 2 is mostly N/A for land categories.** Land use change, land management net biogenic CO2, and land management production emissions have no scope-2 equivalent. Land emissions in the life cycle of purchased electricity, steam, heating, or cooling are reported in **Scope 3, Category 3** (Fuel- and energy-related activities) by the buyer, and in scope 1 or 3 by the energy provider depending on consolidation.

9. **Product-level applicability.** When applying the Product Standard, companies *shall* apply this Standard's accounting and reporting requirements for land emissions, removals, land use, land carbon leakage, gross CO2 fluxes, product carbon storage, and reversals.

10. **Carbon storage tracked, but with reversals.** Optional Part 2.3 lets companies report **product carbon storage** (carbon embodied in agricultural or TCDR-based products during the use phase). Any subsequent loss — recycling, end-of-life decomposition, geologic-reservoir leakage — must be reported as a **reversal**, preventing companies from recognizing storage benefits without later accounting for losses.
