## Key Takeaways

1. **Facility-level Scope 1 reporting, not corporate disclosure.** MRR targets individual facilities and fuel suppliers directly emitting GHGs or delivering fuels into California — a fundamentally different instrument from SB 253, which requires corporate-level Scope 1/2/3 disclosure. Both can apply simultaneously to the same company.

2. **Two-tier threshold system.** A 10,000 MT CO2e/year threshold triggers general reporting. Seven high-emitting categories (electricity generation via Part 75, cement, lime, nitric acid, petroleum refining, geologic CO2 sequestration, CO2 injection) report regardless of emissions level. A second threshold at 25,000 MT CO2e/year triggers mandatory third-party verification and creates a compliance obligation under the Cap-and-Trade Regulation.

3. **Three fixed annual deadlines.** April 10 (full emissions data reports for facilities and suppliers), June 1 (abbreviated reports; electric power entity reports), August 10 (third-party verification statements). These recur every year for the preceding data year.

4. **Calculation methodology is EPA 40 CFR Part 98, not GHG Protocol.** MRR incorporates the US EPA GHG Mandatory Reporting Rule by reference, substituting CARB authority for EPA. This creates a methodological split with SB 253: California-regulated facilities may calculate the same physical emissions two different ways depending on which obligation they are fulfilling.

5. **Enforcement is per-day and per-ton with statutory penalty amounts in H&S Code §38580.** Each day a required report remains late, incomplete, or inaccurate is a separate violation. Each metric ton of CO2e emitted but not reported is also a separate violation. The MRR itself does not specify dollar amounts — those are set by H&S Code §38580.
