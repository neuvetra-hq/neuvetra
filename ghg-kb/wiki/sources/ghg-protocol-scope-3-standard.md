## Key Takeaways

1. **15 standardized categories** for scope 3 emissions, organized into upstream (1–8) and downstream (9–15), replacing the informal category list in the Corporate Standard Appendix D with a formal, binding structure.

2. **All 15 categories are required** — companies must report scope 3 emissions by category, or disclose and justify any excluded categories. This raises the floor above the base Corporate Standard, which makes scope 3 optional overall.

3. **Minimum boundary per category** — the standard defines the minimum scope for each category to prevent cherry-picking. For goods-related categories (1–3), this means full cradle-to-gate upstream emissions. For activity-related categories (4–8, 9–15), this means scope 1 + scope 2 of the relevant value chain partner.

4. **Organizational boundary consistency** — whichever consolidation approach a company uses for scope 1/2 (equity share, financial control, operational control) must be applied consistently to scope 3.

5. **Reporting requirements** — for each category: total GHG in metric tons CO₂e, boundary description, data quality, methodology, and assumptions. The 6 required gases are CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs, PFCs, and SF₆. Biogenic CO₂ must be reported separately.
