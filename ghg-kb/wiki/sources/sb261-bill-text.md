## Key Takeaways

1. **New mandatory climate risk disclosure law.** SB 261 adds Health and Safety Code Section 38533, requiring covered entities to publicly disclose climate-related financial risks biennially using a TCFD-aligned or equivalent framework.

2. **Lower revenue threshold than SB 253.** Covered entities are businesses with >$500M annual revenue doing business in California — capturing a wider population than SB 253's >$1B threshold.

3. **This is financial risk disclosure, not emissions reporting.** SB 261 requires disclosure of physical and transition climate risks and the measures taken to address them — it does not mandate a GHG emissions inventory.

4. **TCFD or equivalent is the mandated framework.** Reports must follow the TCFD Final Report (June 2017) or a recognized equivalent such as IFRS S2/ISSB. Companies already reporting under a compliant law, regulation, or listing requirement satisfy the SB 261 requirement.

5. **Penalty ceiling is $50,000/year.** Much lower than SB 253's $500,000/year cap. Enforcement is via CARB administrative hearing. Health and Safety Code §38580 (standard GHG violation provision) is explicitly excluded — SB 261 has its own standalone enforcement mechanism.
