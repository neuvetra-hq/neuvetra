## Key Takeaways

### 1. Phase 4 cap radically tightened — 62% reduction by 2030 vs 2005

Article 9 of Directive 2003/87/EC is amended (Article 1(11) of this Directive) to:
- **Reduce the Union-wide cap by 90 million allowances in 2024** and **27 million allowances in 2026** (one-time rebasings).
- **Increase the cap by 78.4 million allowances in 2024** to admit maritime transport.
- **Linear reduction factor (LRF):** 4.3% from 2024 to 2027 and 4.4% from 2028 onward (up from 2.2% under the prior 2018/410 trajectory).
- The combined trajectory is calibrated to deliver a **62% emissions reduction in ETS-covered sectors by 2030 compared to 2005** (recital 39).
- Additional cap increases from 1 January 2026 (CH₄ and N₂O from maritime) and 1 January 2027 (offshore ships) — those incremental allowances flow to the Innovation Fund.

### 2. Maritime transport included in ETS1 from 2024

A new **Article 3ga–3gg** is inserted (Article 1(7)) extending Directive 2003/87/EC to maritime:
- **Geographic scope:** 100% of intra-EU voyages and 100% of emissions within EU ports; 50% of emissions on voyages with one EU and one non-EU endpoint.
- **Phase-in of surrender obligations (Article 3gb):**
  - 2024 verified emissions: **40%** surrender
  - 2025 verified emissions: **70%** surrender
  - 2026 onward: **100%** surrender
- **Gases:** CO₂ from 2024; **CH₄ and N₂O from 1 January 2026** (Annex I addition).
- **Regulated entity:** the **shipping company** (defined per ISM Code), with cost pass-through right under Article 3gc when fuel/operations are contracted to a charterer.
- **Coverage threshold:** ships ≥ 5,000 GT carrying cargo or passengers. Offshore ships from 2027.
- **Transhipment-port avoidance rule:** Article 3ga(2) requires a Commission-published list of "neighbouring container transhipment ports" outside the EU but <300 nautical miles from an EU port and >65% transhipment share — calls there are treated as EU port calls to prevent evasive routing.
- **Special provisions:** ice-class ship adjustment (until 2030), small-island connectivity derogations (<200,000 inhabitants), transnational public-service-contract derogations, outermost-region temporary derogation.

### 3. ETS2 — separate trading system for buildings, road transport, and additional sectors (Chapter IVa)

A new **Chapter IVa (Articles 30a–30k)** is inserted (Article 1(29)). This is a parallel but legally separate cap-and-trade system from the existing ETS1.

- **Scope (Annex III, new):** release for consumption of fuels used for combustion in:
  - Buildings — IPCC source category 1A4a (Commercial/Institutional) and 1A4b (Residential)
  - Road transport — IPCC source category 1A3b (excluding agricultural vehicles on paved roads)
  - Additional sectors — Energy Industries (1A1) and Manufacturing Industries & Construction (1A2) that fall outside ETS1 Annex I
  - CHP/heat plants (1A1aii/iii) where heat serves buildings or additional sectors
- **Excluded:** fuels already covered under ETS1 Annex I; fuels with zero emission factor; hazardous and municipal waste used as fuel.
- **Point of regulation: upstream — fuel suppliers / "regulated entities,"** defined by reference to excise-duty law (Council Directive (EU) 2020/262). Final consumers bear no direct compliance obligation.
- **Permits required from 1 January 2025** (Article 30b); historical-emissions reporting for 2024 commences then.
- **Trading begins 2027** with first surrender obligation 31 May 2028 (Article 30e(2)).
- **Allowed delay to 2028** (Article 30k) if exceptional gas/oil wholesale price triggers are met — automatic, one-year delay of cap and surrender obligations.
- **Auctioning only** — no free allocation (recital 81 / Article 30d).
- **Cap trajectory (Article 30c):**
  - 2027 cap derived from Effort Sharing Regulation 2024 limits, declining at LRF **5.10%** per year
  - From 2028 the cap is rebased on average 2024–2026 verified emissions and declines at LRF **5.38%** (or an Annex IIIa-adjusted LRF if 2024–2026 emissions overshoot the 2025 trajectory by >2%)
  - Auction front-loading: 2027 auctions = 130% of cap, with the extra 30% deducted from 2029–2031
- **Cost-efficient sectoral contribution:** 43% reduction by 2030 vs 2005 for buildings + road transport; 42% for the additional sectors (recital 80).
- **Price-stability safeguards:**
  - **Article 30h:** automatic MSR releases if average allowance price exceeds €45 in initial years (recital 91).
  - **Article 30k:** one-year delay trigger based on gas/oil wholesale price thresholds (recital 92).
- **Front-loaded MSR:** 600 million ETS2 allowances created in the MSR in 2027 (Article 30d(2)).
- **Material penalty:** standard ETS €100/t excess-emissions penalty applies via Article 30e (cross-reference to Article 16).

### 4. Social Climate Fund — funded directly from ETS auction revenue

A new **Social Climate Fund** (Regulation (EU) 2023/955) is established in parallel and funded by:
- **50 million ETS1 allowances** (Article 10a(8b))
- **150 million ETS2 allowances** (Article 30d(3))
- Additional ETS2 auction revenue up to a **maximum of €65 billion** through 2032 (Article 30d(4))

The fund channels revenue to vulnerable households and transport users to mitigate carbon-price impact. Annual caps range from €4 billion (2026) to €10.9 billion (2027).

### 5. Free-allocation phase-out for CBAM sectors (recital 46, Article 1(13))

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, Regulation (EU) 2023/956) replaces free allocation as the carbon-leakage prevention mechanism for covered sectors. A **CBAM factor** progressively reduces the free allowances available to those sectors:

| Year | CBAM factor (% of free allocation that remains) |
|---|---|
| 2025 and earlier | 100% |
| 2026 | 97.5% |
| 2027 | 95% |
| 2028 | 90% |
| 2029 | 77.5% |
| 2030 | 51.5% |
| 2031 | 39% |
| 2032 | 26.5% |
| 2033 | 14% |
| 2034 onward | 0% (no free allocation) |

Free allowances no longer issued to CBAM sectors are transferred to the Innovation Fund.

### 6. Conditionality on remaining free allocation from 2026 (recital 45, Article 1(14))

From 2026, free allocation to stationary installations is conditional on:
- Implementation of energy-efficiency / emissions-reduction techniques (Energy Efficiency Directive cross-reference) — applicable in particular to large energy users
- For the **20% installations with the highest emission intensity** under any product benchmark: a mandatory **climate-neutrality plan**

The minimum benchmark adjustment per year is increased from 0.2% to 0.3%; the maximum from 1.6% to 2.5% (recital 48).

### 7. Innovation Fund and Modernisation Fund expanded

- **Innovation Fund (Article 10a(8)):** scope expanded to support buildings/road transport/additional-sector decarbonisation, maritime decarbonisation (alternative fuels, electrification, wind propulsion, ice-class ships), aviation electrification. Adds support for **Carbon Contracts for Difference (CCDs), Contracts for Difference (CDs), and fixed-premium contracts**. An additional 20 M allowances from free-allocation pool + 5 M from auctioning pool top up the fund.
- **Modernisation Fund (Article 10d):** an **additional 2.5% of the Union-wide cap from 2024 to 2030** allocated to lower-income Member States (GDP per capita <75% of EU average). New Annex IIb Part B distribution. Fossil-fuel investments largely ineligible going forward.

### 8. Compliance-cycle deadline shifts (recital 66, Article 1(13))

Two material deadline changes for ETS1 stationary installations:
- **Deadline for competent authorities to grant free allocation:** moved from **28 February to 30 June**.
- **Deadline for operators to surrender allowances:** moved from **30 April to 30 September**.

The verified-emissions report deadline of **31 March** is unchanged.

### 9. Market Stability Reserve amendments (recital 86, Article 2)

Decision (EU) 2015/1814 is amended by Article 2 of this Directive:
- **Aviation allowances and emissions are now included in the Total Number of Allowances in Circulation (TNAC)** calculation from the year following entry into force (recital 86).
- A new automatic price-spike safeguard releases allowances from the MSR when the average allowance price triggers a defined threshold (recital 74).

### 10. Biomass — 95% threshold and tightened zero-rating

- New **95% biomass threshold** (recital 70, Article 1(28) amending Annex I): installations combusting >95% zero-rated biomass are excluded from ETS scope; installations between the threshold and 100% remain in scope to retain MRV oversight.
- Annex IV is amended (Article 1(28)) so that **the emission factor for biomass is zero only where it complies with RED II Article 29 sustainability + GHG-saving criteria** — codifying at directive level the zero-rating restriction already applied at MRR level (cross-references [[sources/eu-ets-mrr-2018-2066|MRR]]).
- New rules cover RFNBOs, recycled carbon fuels, and the accounting treatment of CO₂ captured and used in such fuels.

### 11. Annex I activity scope updates

- **Maritime transport** added as a new Annex I row (CO₂ from 2024; CH₄ and N₂O from 2026).
- **Hydrogen production threshold** lowered to >5 t/day (was 25 t/day under prior text) — captures more electrolytic and reformer-based H₂ producers.
- **Carbon black, gypsum products, primary aluminium** definitions revised.
- **Transport of greenhouse gases for geological storage** extended to all transport modes (ship, truck), not just pipelines.
- **Activity descriptions modified** so that installations meeting the capacity threshold are within scope **even if they emit no GHGs** (recital 10) — ensures equal treatment as installations decarbonise.
