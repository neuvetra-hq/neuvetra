> **Pre-rulemaking notice:** This FAQ does not have the force of law. It represents CARB's pre-regulatory thinking as of July 2025 and was intended to assist with initial planning. The adopted regulation (February 2026) supersedes any definitional proposals in this FAQ. Guidance on initial SB 261 reports (January 1, 2026 deadline) remains relevant as process guidance.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Confirms the SB 253 and SB 261 applicability thresholds.** H&S Code §38532 applies to companies with over $1 billion in total annual revenue doing business in California. H&S Code §38533 applies to companies with over $500 million in annual revenue doing business in California. Some companies are subject to both; others only one.

2. **"Doing business in California" proposed as RTC §23101 (FTB definition).** CARB's initial staff concept — later adopted in the final regulation — is that an entity does business in California if it (1) is actively engaging in any transaction for financial gain (RTC §23101(a)) AND (2) meets at least one of the criteria in §23101(b): organized/domiciled in CA, or California sales > $735,019 (2024 inflation-adjusted threshold). This FAQ presents the full §23101(b)(1)–(4) criteria; the final regulation adopted only (b)(1) and (b)(2), excluding property and payroll prongs.

3. **SB 253 verification timeline confirmed: limited assurance in 2026, reasonable assurance from 2030.** The statute requires Scope 1 and Scope 2 assurance at a "limited" level beginning in 2026 and at a "reasonable" level beginning in 2030. The timeline for verification will be specified in subsequent rulemaking; the 2026 assurance requirement is not expected to apply to the first-year good-faith report.

4. **First SB 261 climate risk reports due January 1, 2026 (biennial thereafter).** CARB opened a public docket on December 1, 2025 for covered entities to submit links to their publicly available reports. Docket remains open until July 1, 2026. Initial reports may cover FY 2023/2024 or FY 2024/2025 depending on organizational timelines. "Good faith" effort is a mitigation factor in penalty assessment.

5. **Streamlining intent confirmed.** CARB explicitly states its goal is to reduce confusion and simplify compliance, including aligning and streamlining reporting requirements where possible with existing programs (e.g., EU CSRD, SEC climate disclosure). Entities already reporting under other jurisdictions may be able to use existing reports if they satisfy SB 253's requirements.
