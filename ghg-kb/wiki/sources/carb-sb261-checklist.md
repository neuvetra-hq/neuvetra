## Key Takeaways

1. **Scope 1/2/3 emissions NOT required as minimum for the initial SB 261 report.** CARB explicitly excluded emissions data from the minimum requirements, citing feasibility concerns given the January 1, 2026 deadline and potential duplication with SB 253. Covered entities following full TCFD or IFRS S2 may still include emissions voluntarily; any GHG claims must be third-party verified before CARB will consider them.

2. **Four minimum disclosure sections with specific content requirements** (aligned to TCFD four pillars):
   - **Governance:** Describe the organization's governance structure — if any — for identifying, assessing, and managing climate-related financial risks, including board oversight (where applicable) and management oversight.
   - **Strategy:** Describe actual and potential impacts of climate risks and opportunities on operations, strategy, and financial planning (where material), including risks/opportunities across short, medium, and long-term horizons and the resilience of the strategy under various climate scenarios. Qualitative scenario-based assessment is acceptable and encouraged where feasible.
   - **Risk Management:** Describe how the reporting entity identifies, assesses, and manages climate-related risks, and how those processes integrate into the organization's overall risk management.
   - **Metrics and Targets:** Disclose metrics and targets used to assess and manage relevant climate-related risks and opportunities, where material.

3. **Three accepted frameworks:** TCFD Final Report (June 2017) or any successor; IFRS S2; any regulated exchange, national government, or other governmental standard including US government regulations. Reports must state which framework was used and explain any omissions with reasons and plans for future disclosure.

4. **Calendar vs. fiscal year:** HSC § 38533 does not specify; CARB guidance says use most recent/best available data for the first report.

5. **Third-party reports are usable** if they meet HSC § 38533 requirements; must be posted on the company's own website and registered in CARB's public docket. Parent-level consolidation allowed — subsidiaries need not file separately if covered by a parent's report.
