## Key Takeaways

1. **The LCFS is a fuel-pool carbon-intensity (CI) standard, not a cap-and-trade program in the traditional sense.** It sets a declining annual average CI benchmark (gCO2e/MJ) for the California transportation fuel pool. Fuels with a CI below the benchmark generate credits; fuels above generate deficits. Credits and deficits are denominated in **metric tons CO2e** and tracked in CARB's LRT-CBTS (LCFS Reporting Tool / Credit Bank and Transfer System). The 2025 amendment effective 2025-07-01 sharply increased stringency: the gasoline benchmark dropped from 87.01 gCO2e/MJ (2024) to 76.60 gCO2e/MJ for fuel transactions on/after 2025-07-01 — a 9 percentage-point step-up in the CI reduction target (22.75% vs 13.75% previously) — declining to 9.91 gCO2e/MJ by 2045. Diesel and jet benchmarks decline on parallel trajectories.

2. **First fuel reporting entity = the regulated party.** For liquid fuels (CARBOB, gasoline, diesel, ethanol, biomass-based diesel, alternative jet fuel) it is the **producer or importer**. For gaseous fuels (CNG, LNG, propane, hydrogen) it is the **owner of the fueling equipment** that dispenses fuel to vehicles. For electricity used as a transportation fuel, the credit generator depends on charging context — Electrical Distribution Utilities (EDUs) for residential charging, charging-equipment owners for non-residential, and OEMs/EV-Service-Provider arrangements for ZEV infrastructure capacity credits. Fossil jet fuel is **not** subject to deficits but alternative jet fuel can opt in to generate credits (Table 3 benchmark schedule).

3. **Credits never expire and are tradable, but are not securities and are subject to a price ceiling.** The Maximum Price for credit transfers was $200/MTCO2e in 2016, CPI-adjusted annually and republished each April for a June 1 effective date. A Credit Clearance Market operates June 1–August 30 each year as a backstop for entities that cannot internally retire enough credits. Carryback (Jan 1–Apr 30) and limited deferred-deficit-with-interest mechanisms exist; entities in deficit two consecutive Clearance Markets must file a five-year Compliance Plan with CARB. A novel **Automatic Acceleration Mechanism (AAM)** (effective May 15, 2027 onward) can advance benchmarks by one year if the credit-bank-to-deficit ratio exceeds 3 AND credit generation exceeds deficit generation for four trailing quarters — designed to prevent benchmark over-supply.

4. **Petroleum producers/importers carry both a base deficit and an incremental crude-CI deficit.** Base deficit = (CI_standard − CI_baseline) × Energy × 10^-6. The Incremental Deficit triggers if the volume-weighted three-year California Crude Average CI exceeds the California Baseline Crude Average CI (12.61 gCO2e/MJ for crude supplied in 2024+) by more than 0.10 gCO2e/MJ. Each crude type has a CI value listed in Table 9 (e.g., Albian Heavy Synthetic 24.45, California Cymric 31.27 range; Brazilian Buzios 7.08; Saudi Arab Light ~10–11). The OPGEE model is the methodology for adding new crudes to Table 9.

5. **Fuel pathway certification is the gateway to credit generation.** Three tiers: **Lookup Table** pathways (pre-certified default values), **Tier 1** (CA-GREET3.0 simplified model with prescribed inputs for most biofuels and biomethane), **Tier 2** (full process modeling for novel pathways). Each pathway gets a Fuel Pathway Code (FPC) and a CI value in gCO2e/MJ. The CA-GREET3.0 model and AEZ-EF Model (for indirect land use change, ILUC, applied to crop-based biofuels) are incorporated by reference. Annual Fuel Pathway Reports must be third-party verified (positive verification statement required to retain credits); discrepancies between reported and verified operational CI are reconciled through a CARB-controlled Buffer Account.

6. **Hard product exclusions and 2030/2040 sunset clauses.** Palm-oil-derived fuels are ineligible for credits (assigned ULSD CI). Fossil jet is excluded from deficits. Soy/canola/sunflower biomass-based diesel is capped at 20% of company-wide BBD volume from 2028. After 2030, hydrogen vehicle fuel must be ≥80% renewable or CCS-paired; from 2035 fossil-feedstock hydrogen is ineligible unless 100% biomethane-matched or CCS-paired. Bio-CNG/LNG/L-CNG projects breaking ground after 2029-12-31 lose credit eligibility for use in CNG vehicles after 2040-12-31.

7. **Penalties: $1,000 per uncleared deficit per year, plus per-day report violations.** Each day a required report remains unsubmitted, incomplete, or inaccurate is a separate violation. Each unresolved deficit at year-end is a separate day of violation, capped at $1,000 per deficit per day. CARB also has broad authority to suspend accounts, invalidate credits, modify Certified CIs, and recalculate deficits where information is incorrect or fuel pathway operating data deviates from the certified pathway (§95495). Material misstatement = ≥0.01 gCO2e/MJ change in CI or ≥1 whole credit/deficit change.

8. **Quarterly + annual reporting cadence.** Quarterly Fuel Transactions Reports are due 90 days after each quarter ends (45 days for upload + 45 days for reconciliation): Mar 31 (Q4 prior year), Jun 30 (Q1), Sep 30 (Q2), Dec 31 (Q3). Annual Compliance Reports are due **April 30** of the following year, with mandatory credit retirement to meet the prior-year compliance obligation. Liquid fuel volumes are temperature-corrected to 60°F using API/ASTM methods. Reports are submitted via LRT-CBTS with a digital signature meeting California Code of Regulations title 2 §§22000 et seq.
