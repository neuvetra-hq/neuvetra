## Definition

**Minimum safeguards** are the third of the four legs of the [[concepts/taxonomy-alignment|Taxonomy alignment]] test under Article 3 of the EU Taxonomy Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2020/852). **Article 18** defines them as procedures implemented by an undertaking carrying out an economic activity to ensure alignment with:

- **OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises**
- **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights** (UNGPs)
- **ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work** — including the principles and rights set out in the eight fundamental conventions
- **International Bill of Human Rights**

Article 18(2) further provides that, in implementing those procedures, undertakings must adhere to the **"do no significant harm" principle** as defined in point (17) of Article 2 of the SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088).

Where DNSH addresses environmental harm at the activity level, minimum safeguards address **social and governance harm at the undertaking level** — particularly human rights, labour rights, anti-corruption, fair taxation, and fair competition.

(→ [[sources/eu-taxonomy-2020-852|EU Taxonomy Regulation 2020/852]])

## Why It Matters

Minimum safeguards prevent the Taxonomy from labelling as "green" any activity carried out by an undertaking with materially deficient human-rights, labour, anti-corruption, taxation, or fair-competition practices. The leg makes alignment a **socio-environmental** test, not a purely environmental one — even an activity with perfect substantial-contribution and DNSH credentials cannot be aligned if the undertaking carrying it out fails the safeguards.

Operationally, minimum safeguards are typically the easiest leg to evaluate at the undertaking level (compliance is broadly company-wide), but require evidence of **due diligence processes**, not just statements of policy. The Platform on Sustainable Finance has issued guidance translating Article 18 into four substantive topics on which an undertaking must demonstrate procedures:

1. **Human rights** (including labour rights and consumer rights)
2. **Bribery / corruption**
3. **Taxation**
4. **Fair competition**

The first topic in particular requires due-diligence processes consistent with the UNGPs and OECD MNE Guidelines — covering policy commitment, human-rights impact assessment, integration and remediation, and stakeholder grievance mechanisms.

## Key Distinctions

### Minimum safeguards vs. DNSH

- **DNSH** (Article 17) → environmental harm prevention, assessed at the **activity level**
- **Minimum safeguards** (Article 18) → social and governance harm prevention, assessed at the **undertaking level**

Both are independent legs of Article 3 alignment — both must be satisfied. Failure of either prevents alignment regardless of how strong the substantial-contribution and TSC compliance are.

### Activity-level vs. undertaking-level

A subtle but important consequence: minimum safeguards apply to the undertaking carrying out the economic activity, not to the activity itself. This means an undertaking that fails minimum safeguards has **no aligned activities** for Article 8 KPI purposes — the failure is portfolio-wide, not activity-by-activity. By contrast, an undertaking can have a mix of DNSH-passing and DNSH-failing activities depending on each activity's environmental footprint.

### Reference to SFDR DNSH

Article 18(2) layers the SFDR Article 2(17) DNSH formulation on top of the four reference instruments. SFDR Article 2(17) defines DNSH for sustainable investments using the Principal Adverse Impact (PAI) indicator framework set in the SFDR RTS (Regulation (EU) 2022/1288). This linkage means minimum-safeguards compliance is not assessed in isolation from the broader SFDR sustainability disclosure regime — PAI indicators that flag adverse social impacts (e.g., violations of UNGPs, gender pay gap, board gender diversity) inform the safeguards assessment.

### What "procedures" means in practice

Article 18 requires undertakings to implement **procedures** to ensure alignment with the reference instruments — not merely to commit to them. The Platform on Sustainable Finance has emphasised that the test is one of **due-diligence process** (UNGPs Pillar II + OECD MNE Chapter II), encompassing:

- Policy commitment formally adopted at board / senior management level
- Periodic human-rights / corruption / taxation / fair-competition impact identification and assessment
- Integration of findings into business decisions
- Tracking and reporting of outcomes
- Cooperation in remediation of actual adverse impacts
- Operational-level grievance mechanisms

Documented findings in independent third-party assessments (e.g., adverse OECD National Contact Point determinations, court rulings on labour or human-rights violations, sanctions for corruption or tax evasion) are typically treated as prima facie evidence of procedure failure.

## Calculation Notes

Minimum safeguards is a **pass/fail compliance assessment at the undertaking level**, not a quantitative calculation. There are no thresholds or KPI formulae intrinsic to the safeguards leg.

In Article 8 disclosure practice, undertakings determine safeguards compliance once at the undertaking level and apply that determination uniformly across all otherwise-aligned activities for the reporting period. A failure of safeguards drives reported alignment KPIs to zero across the portfolio for that period.
