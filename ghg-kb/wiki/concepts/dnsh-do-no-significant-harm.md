## Definition

**Do No Significant Harm (DNSH)** is the second of the four legs of the [[concepts/taxonomy-alignment|Taxonomy alignment]] test under Article 3 of the EU Taxonomy Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2020/852). It requires that an economic activity — having been assessed as substantially contributing to one of the six environmental objectives — does **not significantly harm** any of the *other* objectives.

**Article 17** of the Regulation specifies what "significantly harms" means for each of the six objectives:

| Objective | Significant harm trigger |
|---|---|
| (a) Climate change mitigation | Activity leads to **significant GHG emissions** |
| (b) Climate change adaptation | Activity leads to an increased adverse impact of current and expected future climate on the activity itself or on people, nature, or assets |
| (c) Sustainable use and protection of water and marine resources | Activity is detrimental to the good status / good ecological potential of water bodies (surface, groundwater) or to the good environmental status of marine waters |
| (d) Transition to a circular economy | Activity (i) leads to significant inefficiencies in materials or resource use across the life cycle, (ii) significantly increases waste generation, incineration or disposal (with limited exceptions for non-recyclable hazardous waste), or (iii) causes long-term harm via long-term waste disposal |
| (e) Pollution prevention and control | Activity leads to a significant increase in pollutant emissions to air, water or land relative to the situation before the activity started |
| (f) Protection and restoration of biodiversity and ecosystems | Activity is (i) significantly detrimental to the good condition and resilience of ecosystems, or (ii) detrimental to the conservation status of habitats and species, including those of Union interest |

Article 17(2) requires that DNSH assessment consider both the **direct environmental impact** of the activity and the **environmental impact of the products and services** provided by the activity throughout their life cycle (production, use, end of life).

(→ [[sources/eu-taxonomy-2020-852|EU Taxonomy Regulation 2020/852]])

## Why It Matters

DNSH prevents the Taxonomy from rewarding "trade-off" activities — those that achieve substantial contribution to one environmental objective at the expense of significant harm to another. Without DNSH, an activity could be classified as green for mitigation despite, say, devastating biodiversity impact or severe water pollution. The DNSH leg makes alignment a holistic environmental test, not a single-axis one.

DNSH operates at two levels:

1. **Article 17 generic triggers** — the high-level definition of what "significantly harms" means per objective, set in the Regulation
2. **Activity-specific DNSH criteria** — concrete, sometimes quantitative requirements set in the Commission Delegated Acts for each in-scope activity, often referencing EU environmental legislation (Water Framework Directive, Industrial Emissions Directive, REACH, Habitats Directive, etc.)

The combination means an activity must clear Article 17 conceptually **and** satisfy the activity-specific DNSH provisions written into the relevant Climate or Environmental Delegated Act TSC.

DNSH also reaches beyond the Taxonomy itself into the **SFDR** ecosystem (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088). SFDR Article 2(17) defines "sustainable investment" using its own DNSH formulation, and Article 2a (inserted by Article 25 of the Taxonomy Regulation) tasks the European Supervisory Authorities with developing draft regulatory technical standards on the content and presentation of SFDR DNSH disclosures consistent with the Principal Adverse Impact (PAI) indicator framework.

## Key Distinctions

### DNSH vs. substantial contribution

- **Substantial contribution** is a positive criterion: did the activity actually advance an environmental objective?
- **DNSH** is a negative criterion: did the activity avoid harming the other five?

The two are tested in tandem under Article 3 — substantial contribution to one objective + DNSH against the other five — to produce the alignment determination.

### Taxonomy DNSH vs. SFDR DNSH

- **Taxonomy Article 17 DNSH** — applies at the **economic activity level**, with Article 17 triggers as elaborated by activity-specific TSCs in the Climate and Environmental Delegated Acts
- **SFDR Article 2(17) DNSH** — applies at the **investment level** for products marketed as having sustainable investment objectives; uses the PAI indicator framework and relies on quantitative thresholds set in the SFDR RTS (Regulation (EU) 2022/1288)

The two are linked but operate in different scopes (activity vs. investment) and use different operative metrics. Article 6 of the Taxonomy Regulation requires SFDR Article 8 products to disclose that the Taxonomy DNSH applies *only* to the Taxonomy-aligned portion of the underlying investments.

### DNSH vs. minimum safeguards

DNSH addresses **environmental harm**; [[concepts/minimum-safeguards|minimum safeguards]] address **social and governance harm** (human rights, labour, anti-corruption per OECD MNE / UNGP / ILO / International Bill of Human Rights). They are independent legs of the Article 3 alignment test — both must be satisfied.

### DNSH and life-cycle thinking

Article 17(2) explicitly mandates a life-cycle perspective for DNSH assessment, including evidence from existing life-cycle assessments. This is mirrored in Article 19(1)(g), which requires that **all TSCs** (substantial-contribution and DNSH alike) take into account the activity's life cycle. For GHG-intensive sectors, this means DNSH for mitigation cannot be evaluated only on direct emissions — upstream embedded emissions and downstream use-phase emissions must also be considered where material.

## Calculation Notes

DNSH itself is not a unitary calculation. In practice it compiles, per activity, into a checklist of activity-specific quantitative thresholds and qualitative compliance evidence:

- **Mitigation DNSH (a)** — typically expressed as an emission-intensity ceiling or a ban on activity types that have significant direct GHG emissions; for many activities, the substantial-contribution threshold and the mitigation DNSH ceiling are designed not to overlap such that a substantial-contribution determination implicitly clears mitigation DNSH for that activity
- **Adaptation DNSH (b)** — typically a requirement to perform a climate-risk assessment using best-available climate projections and to implement adaptation solutions for identified material risks
- **Water DNSH (c)** — typically references the EU Water Framework Directive, Marine Strategy Framework Directive, and water-stress / water-quality assessments
- **Circular economy DNSH (d)** — typically references waste hierarchy compliance, recyclability, and material-efficiency thresholds
- **Pollution DNSH (e)** — typically references the Industrial Emissions Directive, REACH (substance restrictions), and air/water/soil quality standards
- **Biodiversity DNSH (f)** — typically references the Habitats Directive, Birds Directive, environmental impact assessment requirements, and protected-area screens

The concrete numerical thresholds and references for each activity live in the **Climate Delegated Act (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2139)** for activities contributing to mitigation/adaptation, and in the **Environmental Delegated Act (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2486)** for activities contributing to the four other objectives.

### Worked example — activity-specific DNSH for "Installation and operation of electric heat pumps" (Climate Delegated Act Annex I Section 4.16)

The substantial-contribution test for this activity is straightforward: the activity is on the catalogue and the heat pump is installed and operated. Alignment then turns largely on the DNSH overlay, which combines generic Appendix references with activity-specific numerical limits (→ [[sources/eu-taxonomy-climate-delegated-act-2021-2139|Climate Delegated Act 2021/2139]]):

| Other objective | Generic source | Activity-specific overlay |
|---|---|---|
| (b) Adaptation | Appendix A — climate-risk & vulnerability assessment | Required: full physical-climate-risk assessment using best-available climate projections (RCP scenarios) over the heat pump's expected lifetime; adaptation solutions implemented for material identified risks. |
| (c) Water | Appendix B | None beyond generic. |
| (d) Circular economy | — | Designed for high durability and recyclability; recyclability of components considered. |
| (e) Pollution / chemicals | Appendix C — REACH / POPs / Hg / SVHC | **Refrigerant Global Warming Potential (GWP) ≤ 675**; outdoor sound power level limits per applicable Ecodesign regulations. |
| (f) Biodiversity | Appendix D — EIA / Habitats / Birds Directives | None beyond generic. |

This pattern is typical: each activity composes a DNSH "checklist" where generic Appendix compliance is mandatory and activity-specific numerical or substantive overlays sharpen the test. For combustion installations and heavy industry, the activity-specific overlay typically references the **BAT-AEL ranges** in BAT conclusions under Industrial Emissions Directive 2010/75/EU. For chemicals, REACH substance bans and SVHC limits sharpen Appendix C. For transport, noise limits under Regulation (EU) No 540/2014 sharpen Appendix C for air-quality-adjacent harm.

For the four other environmental objectives, the equivalent numerical thresholds live in the Environmental Delegated Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2486) *(separate ingest pending)*.
