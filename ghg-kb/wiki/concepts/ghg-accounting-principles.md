## Definition

The five GHG accounting and reporting principles are the quality criteria that underpin all GHG inventory work. They are derived in part from generally accepted financial accounting principles and were developed through a multi-stakeholder process by the GHG Protocol Initiative. All GHG inventories prepared under the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard — and by extension, the regulations that reference it — must satisfy these principles.

(→ [[sources/ghg-protocol-corporate-standard|GHG Protocol Corporate Standard, Chapter 1]])

## Why It Matters

These principles are the basis on which verifiers assess the credibility of a GHG inventory. When the application of specific accounting rules to a situation is ambiguous, the principles serve as the tiebreaker. Regulators (CSRD, CARB MRR, EU ETS) reference the GHG Protocol directly, so these principles effectively govern mandatory reporting as well as voluntary disclosure.

## Key Distinctions

**The five principles:**

### Relevance
The inventory must reflect the company's actual GHG emissions and serve the decision-making needs of both internal and external users. Inventory boundary selection should reflect the substance and economic reality of business relationships, not just legal form.

### Completeness
All emission sources and activities within the chosen inventory boundary must be accounted for. Exclusions must be disclosed and justified. A materiality threshold cannot substitute for completeness — once a source is quantified to check whether it is below threshold, most of the benefit of having a threshold is lost. Companies must make a good-faith effort to account for all sources.

### Consistency
The same methodologies must be used over time to enable meaningful comparisons. Any changes to data, boundary, methods, or other relevant factors must be transparently documented in the time series.

### Transparency
All processes, procedures, assumptions, and limitations must be disclosed in a clear, factual, neutral, and auditable manner. The information must be sufficient for a third party to reproduce the same results from the same source data. Independent external verification helps ensure transparency.

### Accuracy
Quantification must be neither systematically over nor under actual emissions, as far as can be judged. Uncertainties must be reduced as far as practicable. Accuracy does not require perfection — it requires that the methodology minimizes bias and that the degree of uncertainty is disclosed.

### Conservativeness *(project accounting and land sector / removals)*
Where data and assumptions are uncertain and where the cost of reducing that uncertainty is not justified, conservative values must be used — i.e., values that are more likely to underestimate than overestimate GHG reductions or removals. This sixth principle applies under the GHG Protocol for Project Accounting and is carried forward into the GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard for corporate-level removals reporting. It reflects the asymmetric integrity risk in project-based crediting and removals accounting: over-counting GHG reductions or removals is worse than under-counting.

(→ [[sources/ghg-protocol-project-accounting|GHG Protocol for Project Accounting, Chapter 4]] · [[sources/ghg-protocol-land-sector-removals-2026|Land Sector and Removals Standard, Chapter 3]])

### Permanence *(land sector / removals only)*
Reductions and removals reported under the Land Sector and Removals Standard must reflect the duration over which carbon is kept out of the atmosphere. Companies shall account for the risk of reversal of previously-reported removals (e.g., forest fire releasing biomass C, soil-carbon loss from re-tilling, geologic-reservoir leakage) and report any such reversals as a separate line item. Permanence was introduced as the seventh principle by the GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard (v1.0, January 2026, effective 1 January 2027). It is **not** part of the Corporate Standard's five-principle framework and applies specifically when a company elects to report removals or product carbon storage.

(→ [[sources/ghg-protocol-land-sector-removals-2026|Land Sector and Removals Standard, Chapters 3, 12]] · [[concepts/co2-removals|CO2 Removals]])

**Relationship between principles:** Completeness and accuracy can create practical tension — gathering data for every minor source may be costly. The standard expects good-faith effort and transparent disclosure of gaps, not perfection. In project accounting and land-sector accounting, Conservativeness provides a tie-breaking rule when uncertainty cannot be resolved economically. Permanence is the time dimension of accuracy: an accurate point estimate of removed CO2 is meaningless if the carbon pool subsequently reverses without being reported.

## Calculation Notes

These principles do not directly produce a calculation — they govern the quality of inputs, methodology choices, and outputs throughout the inventory process. They apply at every stage: boundary setting, data collection, emission factor selection, uncertainty assessment, and reporting.
