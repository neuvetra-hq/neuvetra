## Definition

A **CO2 removal** is the anthropogenic transfer of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere into a non-atmospheric carbon pool — biological (plants, soils, wood products), geological (saline aquifers, depleted reservoirs), or technological (mineralized carbonates, captured CO2 in cement). Removals are quantified and reported **separately** from gross emissions; they are never netted into a Scope 1, 2, or 3 emissions figure.

The GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard (v1.0, effective 2027-01-01) establishes the first binding accounting framework for removals in corporate inventories.

(→ [[ghg-protocol-land-sector-removals-2026|GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard]])

## Why It Matters

IPCC 1.5°C-consistent pathways require **100 billion to 1 trillion tonnes of additional CO2 removal over the 21st century** alongside deep emissions reductions across all sectors (IPCC AR6 WG3 Ch. 7). The economic potential of land-based and technological CDR by 2050 is significant — but only if accounting prevents:

- **Double counting** — a single removal claimed by multiple companies in the value chain or by a project and a corporate purchaser of credits.
- **Reversals** — silent loss of previously-reported carbon stock (forest fire, crop rotation back to tilled soil, geologic-reservoir leakage).
- **Confusion with avoided emissions** — a renewable energy project displaces fossil generation but does not *remove* CO2 from the atmosphere.

Permanence and additionality are the two principles that distinguish credible removals from accounting artefacts.

## Key Distinctions

**Removals vs avoided emissions vs reductions.** These three categories are conceptually distinct and never reported in the same line item:

| Category | What it is | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduction** | Lower gross emissions vs prior year (efficiency, fuel switch) | Scope 1/2/3 inventory line |
| **Removal** | CO2 transferred from atmosphere to a non-atmospheric pool | Separate "Removals" line; never netted |
| **Avoided emission** | Emissions from an alternative scenario that did not occur | Outside the scopes; supplementary disclosure only |

**Land management removals (LMR) vs technological CDR (TCDR).**
- LMR: photosynthesis-driven uptake into biomass and soils (afforestation, reforestation, agroforestry, cover-cropping, peatland rewetting, biochar application).
- TCDR: engineered processes — direct air capture (DAC), bioenergy with CCS (BECCS), enhanced rock weathering, mineralization, ocean alkalinity enhancement.

**Permanence is a spectrum, not a binary.** Geological storage (1,000+ years) is highest permanence; soil-organic-carbon and forest-biomass storage is lower (decades to centuries) and more vulnerable to reversal. The Land Sector Standard requires reporting reversals when previously-reported removals exit the company's operations or value chain — preventing companies from booking storage benefits without later booking losses.

**Captured CO2 ≠ removed CO2.** Capturing fossil CO2 at a power plant and storing it geologically (CCS) is a *fossil emission avoided*, not a *removal*. Capturing biogenic CO2 (BECCS) or direct-air CO2 (DACCS) and storing it geologically *is* a removal. The Land Sector Standard distinguishes "captured biogenic CO2 with geologic storage" from "captured fossil CO2" — only the former contributes to gross technological removals.

**Carbon credits are separate from inventory removals.** A company may *generate* credits from its own removals (and choose whether to retain them or sell them) and may *retire* credits purchased from third parties. Credits retired are typically used toward compensation targets (offsetting). The Land Sector Standard requires reporting credits retired and credits generated separately from physical inventory removals.

## Calculation Notes

**Scope mapping (under the Land Sector and Removals Standard):**
- Scope 1 removals: from the company's owned/controlled lands or operations
- Scope 3 removals: from the company's value chain (e.g., suppliers' agroforestry programs, downstream long-life bio-based products)
- Scope 2 is generally **not applicable** to removals — captured electricity-related removals route through the energy provider's scope 1 or 3, not the buyer's scope 2

**Required accounting categories (LSRS Part 2.2):**
- Land management CO2 removals (Ch. 13)
- CO2 removals with geologic storage — biogenic and TCDR (Ch. 14)
- Reversals — the quantitative loss line item (Ch. 12)

**Optional categories (LSRS Part 2.3):**
- Product carbon storage (biogenic and TCDR-based) — carbon embodied in products during the use phase (e.g., wood in buildings, CO2-cured cement)

**PCAF treatment.** Financial institutions financing companies that report removals attribute those removals using the same attribution factor as for gross emissions, and report the attributed removals as a **separate line** alongside attributed scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions. (→ [[concepts/financed-emissions|Financed Emissions]])

**IPCC AR6 mitigation context.** AR6 WG3 Ch. 7 estimates BECCS technical potential at 5.9 (0.5–11.3) GtCO2/yr by 2050, with 1.6 (0.8–3.5) GtCO2/yr economically feasible at <USD100/tCO2-eq. Forests + other natural ecosystems (protection, improved management, restoration) deliver 7.3 (3.9–13.1) GtCO2-eq/yr at <USD100. (→ [[ipcc-ar6-wg3-ch7-afolu-2022|IPCC AR6 WG3 Ch. 7]])
