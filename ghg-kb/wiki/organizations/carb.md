## Overview

The **California Air Resources Board (CARB)** — also referred to as ARB — is the state agency responsible for controlling air pollution and regulating greenhouse gas emissions in California. CARB operates under the California Environmental Protection Agency (CalEPA).

CARB's GHG authority derives primarily from the **California Global Warming Solutions Act of 2006 (AB 32)**, codified in Health and Safety Code sections 38500–38599. AB 32 directed CARB to develop and enforce regulations to reduce California's GHG emissions to 1990 levels by 2020 and further below by 2030.

(→ [[sources/carb-mrr-2018|CARB MRR 2018 Amendment Source]])

## Role in GHG Ecosystem

CARB is the central regulatory authority for California's GHG compliance programs. Its core roles:

**Rulemaking:** CARB promulgates the binding regulations that implement AB 32 and related statutes. Key GHG rules include the Mandatory Reporting Regulation (MRR) and the Cap-and-Trade Regulation (Title 17 CCR §95800 et seq.).

**Emissions reporting oversight:** Under the MRR (Title 17 CCR §95100–95163), CARB receives, processes, and maintains annual facility-level GHG emissions data from thousands of California facilities, fuel suppliers, and electric power entities. CARB assigns each reporting entity an ARB Facility ID.

**Cap-and-Trade administration:** CARB operates California's Cap-and-Trade Program, including administering the California Reporting Greenhouse Gas Reporting Tool (Cal e-GGRT) through which all MRR reports are submitted. CARB issues compliance instruments (allowances, offset credits) and sets compliance obligations based on verified MRR data.

**SB 253/SB 261 implementation:** CARB has dual implementation responsibilities under California's two-track corporate climate disclosure regime.

Under **SB 253 (CCDAA)**: CARB adopts implementing regulations for corporate Scope 1/2/3 GHG disclosure and contracts with a nonprofit emissions reporting organization to host the public digital disclosure platform.

Under **SB 261**: CARB contracts with a **climate reporting organization** — a nonprofit with existing U.S. operations and California experience — to biennially prepare a public report reviewing a subset of company disclosures, analyzing systemic climate-related financial risks to the state, and identifying inadequate reports. CARB also: collects annual administration fees from covered entities (deposited into the Climate-Related Financial Risk Disclosure Fund, continuously appropriated back to CARB for SB 261 costs); and enforces penalties of up to $50,000/year via administrative hearings against covered entities that fail to publish or publish an inadequate climate-related financial risk report. (→ [[regulations/sb261|SB 261]])

**Verification accreditation:** CARB accredits verification bodies and individual verifiers under the MRR. CARB sets standards for verification body qualification, conflict of interest rules, professional liability insurance requirements ($4M minimum), and the accreditation review process.

**Enforcement:** CARB enforces MRR obligations under Health and Safety Code section 38580, with penalties triggered per late report-day and per metric ton of CO2e unreported.

**GHG inventory:** CARB maintains California's statewide GHG emissions inventory, which uses MRR data as a primary input for the industrial and transportation sectors.

The Executive Officer of CARB is the individual with signatory authority under MRR. Where 40 CFR Part 98 (the federal EPA rule incorporated by reference into MRR) uses "Administrator" or "EPA," the MRR substitutes "Executive Officer of CARB" and "ARB" respectively.
