## Overview

The GHG Protocol Initiative is a multi-stakeholder partnership of businesses, NGOs, governments, and others convened jointly by the **World Resources Institute (WRI)** and the **World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD)**. It was launched in 1998 with the mission to develop internationally accepted GHG accounting and reporting standards for business and to promote their broad adoption.

The Initiative has produced the most widely used suite of GHG accounting standards in the world. These standards underpin virtually every mandatory and voluntary GHG reporting regime in existence.

(→ [[sources/ghg-protocol-corporate-standard|GHG Protocol Corporate Standard]])

## Role in GHG Ecosystem

The GHG Protocol Initiative is the **primary standard-setter** for corporate GHG accounting. Its standards:
- Define the Scope 1 / 2 / 3 taxonomy used globally
- Establish the organizational and operational boundary approaches
- Set the quality principles (Relevance, Completeness, Consistency, Transparency, Accuracy — plus Conservativeness for project and land-sector accounting and Permanence for land-sector and removals accounting) referenced by all downstream regulations
- Are compatible with and used as the basis for EU ETS, CSRD/ESRS E1, CARB MRR, SB 253, and most voluntary programs (CDP, SBTi, TCFD, PCAF)
- The **Land Sector and Removals Standard** (v1.0, Jan 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027) added the first binding corporate-level framework for land use change, agricultural land management, biogenic product emissions, and CO2 removals — supplementing the Corporate Standard and Scope 3 Standard for land-sector value chains

The Initiative does **not** require companies to report emissions to WRI or WBCSD — it provides the accounting framework only.
