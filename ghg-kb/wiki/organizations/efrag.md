## Overview

EFRAG (European Financial Reporting Advisory Group) is an independent European body that provides technical expertise and advice to the European Commission on financial and sustainability reporting standards. For sustainability reporting, EFRAG has been designated as the technical standard-setter responsible for developing and maintaining the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) under the CSRD.

EFRAG is headquartered in Brussels, Belgium. Its sustainability reporting work is overseen by a Sustainability Reporting Board (SRB), which includes representatives from European industry associations, civil society, investors, and accounting bodies.

## Role in GHG Ecosystem

EFRAG's primary function in the GHG emissions reporting ecosystem is developing and maintaining the ESRS:

- **ESRS development:** EFRAG drafts and publishes exposure drafts of all ESRS standards, conducts public consultations, and delivers final technical recommendations to the European Commission
- **ESRS adoption:** The Commission adopts ESRS as delegated regulations. EFRAG's recommendations form the basis but the Commission may modify them before adoption
- **Implementation guidance:** EFRAG publishes **non-authoritative** implementation guidance (IGs), FAQs, and educational materials for companies applying ESRS. IGs accompany ESRS but **do not form part of them** — where an IG appears to contradict an ESRS requirement, the ESRS takes precedence. The May 2024 IG suite (IG 1 Materiality, IG 2 Value Chain, IG 3 Datapoints) is the principal preparer-facing guidance for ESRS Set 1.
- **International coordination:** EFRAG engages with ISSB (International Sustainability Standards Board), GRI, and TCFD to promote convergence between ESRS and international standards

**First ESRS Set:**
Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772 (31 July 2023) contains the first 12 standards developed by EFRAG: 2 cross-cutting (ESRS 1, ESRS 2) + 5 environmental topical (E1–E5) + 4 social topical (S1–S4) + 1 governance topical (G1). ESRS E1 (Climate Change) is the GHG-specific standard within this set.

Per Recital 3 of the Delegated Regulation, the Commission introduced modifications to EFRAG's technical advice — particularly on the materiality approach, phasing-in of certain requirements, conversion of certain requirements into voluntary datapoints, introduction of flexibilities, technical modifications to ensure coherence with EU legal framework, and editorial modifications. EFRAG's underlying technical advice is confirmed by the Commission to meet the criteria of Article 49(3b) of Directive 2013/34/EU.

EFRAG is distinct from — but works closely with — the European Commission, which is the formal adopting body for ESRS via delegated act.

## Key Publications

- **ESRS Set 1 (2023):** 12 topical standards + ESRS 1 + ESRS 2 — adopted as Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772
- **ESRS E1 — Climate Change:** The GHG and climate standard within Set 1 (→ [[regulations/esrs-e1|ESRS E1]])
- **ESRS 1 — General Requirements:** Defines structure, materiality assessment, and quality requirements for all ESRS disclosures
- **ESRS 2 — General Disclosures:** Cross-cutting governance, strategy, and risk management disclosures applicable to all material topics
- **Implementation Guidance suite (May 2024):**
  - [[sources/efrag-ig1-materiality-2024|IG 1 — Materiality Assessment]] (55p) — operationalises ESRS 1 Chapter 3 with a four-step DMA process and severity / threshold mechanics
  - [[sources/efrag-ig2-value-chain-2024|IG 2 — Value Chain]] (48p) — operationalises ESRS 1 Chapter 5 and §10.2; financial-control vs operational-control boundary, tiered data approach, Seren Group worked example
  - [[sources/efrag-ig3-datapoints-2024|IG 3 — List of ESRS Datapoints]] (15p note + Excel workbook) — full datapoint inventory across ESRS 2 + 11 topical standards with phase-in cross-walk and SFDR/Taxonomy regulatory cross-references
