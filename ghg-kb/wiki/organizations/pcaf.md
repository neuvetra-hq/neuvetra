## Overview

The **Partnership for Carbon Accounting Financials (PCAF)** is an industry-led initiative that develops standardized GHG accounting and reporting methodologies for the financial sector. It is not a regulatory body — membership is voluntary. PCAF enables financial institutions to measure, disclose, and manage the GHG emissions associated with their loans, investments, and underwriting activities.

PCAF was founded in 2015 by a group of Dutch financial institutions, expanded to North America in 2018, and scaled globally in 2019. As of November 2025, it has more than 670 signatory financial institutions worldwide. The Secretariat is operated by Guidehouse, a global consultancy.

## Role in GHG Ecosystem

PCAF occupies a specific niche: it translates the GHG Protocol's Scope 3 Standard (which defines Category 15 — Investments) into practical, asset-class-specific calculation methods for financial institutions. Without PCAF, financial institutions would face an unbridged gap between "report Cat 15" and "here is how to actually calculate it for a mortgage portfolio or a corporate bond."

**Relationship to GHG Protocol:** PCAF's First Edition Financed Emissions Standard (November 2020) was reviewed by the GHG Protocol and carries the "Built on GHG Protocol" mark for six original asset classes. The GHG Protocol subsequently closed its review service. The Second Edition (December 2023) and Third Edition (December 2025) additions have not been reviewed by the GHG Protocol.

**Relationship to regulatory frameworks:** PCAF is referenced by:
- ESRS E1 (EU CSRD) — PCAF is the implied methodology for Cat 15 under ESRS E1 financed emissions disclosure
- SBTi Financial Institutions Net-Zero Standard — requires PCAF-aligned Cat 15 accounting
- CDP financial sector questionnaire — aligns disclosure requirements with PCAF
- TCFD supplemental guidance — cites PCAF for financed emissions

PCAF does not carry regulatory force on its own; it gains de facto mandatory status when regulations (CSRD, SB 253) require GHG Protocol-aligned Cat 15 accounting and no other detailed standard exists.

## Key Publications

PCAF's Global GHG Accounting and Reporting Standard for the Financial Industry has three parts:

| Part | Title | Current Edition | Covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Part A** | Financed Emissions | Third Edition — December 2025 | Loans and investments; Scope 3 Cat 15; 10 asset classes |
| **Part B** | Facilitated Emissions | First Edition | Capital markets facilitation (underwriting, syndication) |
| **Part C** | Insurance-Associated Emissions | Second Edition | Re/insurance underwriting attribution |

**Part A asset classes (Third Edition):**
1. Listed equity and corporate bonds
2. Business loans and unlisted equity
3. Project finance
4. Commercial real estate
5. Mortgages
6. Motor vehicle loans
7. Use of proceeds structures *(new — Third Edition)*
8. Securitizations and structured products *(new — Third Edition)*
9. Sovereign debt
10. Sub-sovereign debt *(new — Third Edition)*

Supplemental guidance published separately: Financed Avoided Emissions; Forward-Looking Metrics; Financed Emissions Inventory Fluctuation guidance.
