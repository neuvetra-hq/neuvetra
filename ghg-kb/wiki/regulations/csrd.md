## Overview

The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (Directive (EU) 2022/2464, "CSRD") is the EU's mandatory framework for corporate sustainability reporting. It replaces the Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD) and dramatically expands the scope, depth, and assurance requirements for sustainability disclosures across European companies.

CSRD does not set the disclosure requirements itself — it creates the legal mandate and delegates the technical content to the **European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)**, adopted by the Commission via delegated regulation. The first ESRS set was adopted as **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2772** on 31 July 2023, applicable to financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2024.

The defining principle of CSRD is **double materiality**: companies must report on both the financial risks and opportunities that sustainability issues create for the company, and on the company's impacts on people and the environment. Both directions of materiality must be assessed independently.

### ESRS sustainability statement structure (ESRS 1 Appendix D)

The CSRD sustainability statement is presented as a dedicated section of the management report, organized in four parts:
1. **General information** — ESRS 2 disclosures (cross-cutting)
2. **Environmental information** — Article 8 of EU Taxonomy Regulation 2020/852 + ESRS E1, E2, E3, E4, E5
3. **Social information** — ESRS S1, S2, S3, S4
4. **Governance information** — ESRS G1

## Who Must Comply

**Threshold for large undertakings:** Companies meeting at least 2 of 3 criteria:
- More than 250 average employees
- Net turnover exceeding €50 million
- Balance sheet total exceeding €25 million

**Four in-scope categories:**

| Category | Threshold | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Large EU undertakings | 2 of 3 thresholds above | Includes listed and non-listed |
| Listed SMEs | Securities admitted to EU regulated market | 2-year opt-out available |
| Third-country undertakings | Net turnover >€150M in EU + EU subsidiary or branch | Subsidiary/branch becomes the reporting entity |
| Credit institutions & insurance | Size criteria apply | Per sector-specific definitions |

**Exemptions:**
- Micro-undertakings (all three thresholds below €350K balance sheet, €700K turnover, 10 employees)
- Subsidiaries where the parent produces consolidated sustainability reporting that covers the subsidiary

## Reporting Requirements

Reports must cover **double materiality** across three ESG pillars. Cross-cutting requirements ([[regulations/esrs-1]] and [[regulations/esrs-2]]) apply in full regardless of materiality; topical requirements apply only to material topics, subject to phase-in (→ [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in and Transitional Provisions]]).

### Cross-cutting (always-mandatory)
- ESRS 1 — General Requirements: architecture, double-materiality methodology, value-chain rules, time horizons, transitional provisions
- ESRS 2 — General Disclosures: Basis for Preparation (BP-1, BP-2), Governance (GOV-1 through GOV-5), Strategy (SBM-1 through SBM-3), Impact/Risk/Opportunity Management (IRO-1, IRO-2), and the four Minimum Disclosure Requirements (MDR-P, MDR-A, MDR-M, MDR-T) that structure all topical policies/actions/metrics/targets

### Environmental (ESRS E1–E5)
- **E1 Climate Change** — GHG emissions: Scope 1, Scope 2 (location-based and market-based), Scope 3 (all material categories); transition plan (Paris Agreement / 1.5°C alignment); energy consumption and mix; carbon credits; internal carbon pricing
- **E2 Pollution** — air, water, soil, microplastics, substances of (very high) concern
- **E3 Water and Marine Resources** — water consumption, withdrawal, discharge; areas of high water stress
- **E4 Biodiversity and Ecosystems** — five direct drivers of biodiversity loss; transition plan; land-use change
- **E5 Resource Use and Circular Economy** — resource inflows, outflows, waste; secondary materials; circular design

The GHG-specific environmental standard is **ESRS E1 (Climate Change)** — see (→ [[regulations/esrs-e1|ESRS E1]]).

### Social (ESRS S1–S4)
- **S1 Own Workforce** — working conditions, equal treatment, work-related rights (17 DRs)
- **S2 Workers in the Value Chain** — upstream/downstream worker rights; supply-chain due diligence
- **S3 Affected Communities** — indigenous peoples, FPIC, land rights
- **S4 Consumers and End-Users** — privacy, safety, social inclusion

### Governance (ESRS G1)
- Business conduct policies and corporate culture
- Supplier relationships and payment practices
- Anti-corruption and anti-bribery
- Political influence and lobbying

### Format and Assurance Requirements
- Reports must be forward-looking and retrospective, qualitative and quantitative
- Must cover the full value chain where material
- Accompanied by a mandatory **assurance opinion** from an accredited auditor or assurance provider (limited assurance initially; Commission to assess move to reasonable assurance by 2028)
- Must be machine-readable (filed in iXBRL format using ESRS taxonomy)

## Deadlines

| Category | First Reporting Financial Year | Reports Published |
|---|---|---|
| Large listed companies previously subject to NFRD (>500 employees) | FY 2024 | 2025 |
| All other large undertakings | FY 2025 | 2026 |
| Listed SMEs | FY 2026 (2-year opt-out: can defer to FY 2028) | 2027 |
| Third-country undertakings (>€150M EU turnover) | FY 2028 | 2029 |

**Note:** The 2-year opt-out for listed SMEs means many listed SMEs will not face mandatory reporting until FY 2028.

**Disclosure-level phase-in:** Distinct from the company-scope phasing above, ESRS 1 Section 10 + Appendix C set staggered relief for *which disclosures within each report* are required immediately. Key reliefs include:
- **750-employee threshold:** Undertakings ≤ 750 employees may omit Scope 3 + Total GHG (E1-6) for year 1, and may omit ALL of E4 / S1 / S2 / S3 / S4 for the first 1–2 years
- **Anticipated financial effects** (E1-9, E2-6, E3-5, E4-6, E5-6, SBM-3 paragraph 48(e)): first-year omission permitted; first 3 years qualitative-only reporting permitted
- **Value chain transitional provision (ESRS 1 Section 10.2):** 3-year relief allowing exclusion of metric-level value-chain information not reasonably available
- **Comparative information:** first-year relief
- **Sector revenue breakdown (SBM-1 paragraphs 40(b)/(c)):** deferred until sector classification delegated act adopted

Full detail at [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in and Transitional Provisions]].

**Omnibus Package (2025):** The EU Commission proposed amendments in February 2025 to narrow CSRD scope and delay some deadlines for certain categories. As of 2026-04-24, these amendments remain under negotiation. Monitor EUR-Lex and EFRAG for updates. (→ flagged as pending resolution)

## Penalties

Specific penalty amounts are not set in the CSRD directive itself — they are delegated to Member State transposition into national law. Enforcement mechanisms include:

- Mandatory assurance opinion required for all sustainability reports (non-compliant reports can be flagged)
- National competent authorities responsible for compliance monitoring
- ESMA (European Securities and Markets Authority) coordinates supervisory convergence
- Third-country entities that fail to provide required information must publish a statement of reasons; their EU subsidiary or branch bears responsibility

Individual Member States set their own monetary penalties through national transposition.

## Calculation Requirements

CSRD mandates GHG emissions disclosure in accordance with the ESRS. The technical calculation requirements are in:

- **ESRS E1-6**: Scope 1, Scope 2 (location-based and market-based), Scope 3 (all 15 categories where material), biogenic CO₂ separate from total
- **Referenced methodology**: GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard; GHG Protocol Scope 2 Guidance
- **Scope 3**: Companies may use a Year 1 opt-out for the full 15-category breakdown; opt-out must be disclosed

See (→ [[regulations/esrs-e1|ESRS E1]]) and (→ [[methodologies/scope-2-location-based|Scope 2 Location-Based Method]]) and (→ [[methodologies/scope-2-market-based|Scope 2 Market-Based Method]]).
