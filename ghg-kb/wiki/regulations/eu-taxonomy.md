## Overview

The EU Taxonomy Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2020/852, "Taxonomy Regulation") establishes a Union-wide **classification system** for environmentally sustainable economic activities. It is the foundational instrument of the EU's Sustainable Finance framework — the legal definition of what counts as "green" investment. It does not itself prescribe emission factors, GHG calculation methodologies, or company-level reduction targets. Instead, it defines:

- **Six environmental objectives** (Article 9) that the framework targets
- The **four-part test** (Article 3) an economic activity must satisfy to qualify as environmentally sustainable
- The **delegation** to the European Commission to establish detailed **technical screening criteria** (TSCs) for each objective and each in-scope activity, via Commission Delegated Acts

The Taxonomy was published on 22 June 2020 (OJ L 198/13), entered into force on 12 July 2020, and applies in stages from 1 January 2022 (climate mitigation + adaptation) and 1 January 2023 (the four other environmental objectives) per Article 27(2). It amends the **Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR, Regulation (EU) 2019/2088)** to embed Taxonomy disclosures into financial-product transparency requirements.

The Taxonomy interlocks with the broader EU sustainability stack:
- **Article 8** of the Taxonomy Regulation requires undertakings subject to non-financial reporting (originally NFRD, now [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]]) to disclose the proportion of their **turnover, CapEx, and OpEx** associated with Taxonomy-aligned economic activities. The detailed methodology lives in the Article 8 Delegated Act, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2178.
- The **CSRD sustainability statement** (per [[regulations/esrs-1|ESRS 1]] Appendix D) presents Article 8 Taxonomy KPIs as part of the **environmental information block**, alongside [[regulations/esrs-e1|ESRS E1]] Climate Change disclosures.

(→ [[sources/eu-taxonomy-2020-852|EU Taxonomy Regulation 2020/852 — EUR-Lex]])

### The four-part test (Article 3)

An economic activity qualifies as **environmentally sustainable** ("Taxonomy-aligned") only if **all four** conditions are met:

1. **Substantial contribution** to one or more of the six environmental objectives in Article 9, in accordance with Articles 10–16
2. **Do No Significant Harm (DNSH)** to any of the other five environmental objectives, in accordance with Article 17 — see [[concepts/dnsh-do-no-significant-harm|DNSH]]
3. Carried out in compliance with the **minimum safeguards** of Article 18 — see [[concepts/minimum-safeguards|Minimum Safeguards]]
4. Compliance with the **technical screening criteria (TSCs)** established by the Commission under Articles 10(3), 11(3), 12(2), 13(2), 14(2) or 15(2)

The two related concepts that the Taxonomy disclosure regime turns on are [[concepts/taxonomy-eligibility|eligibility]] (the activity is on the list covered by an applicable Delegated Act) and [[concepts/taxonomy-alignment|alignment]] (the activity also passes the four-part test).

### The six environmental objectives (Article 9)

| # | Objective | Substantial-contribution article | TSC adoption deadline | Application from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Climate change mitigation | Article 10 | 31 December 2020 | 1 January 2022 |
| (b) | Climate change adaptation | Article 11 | 31 December 2020 | 1 January 2022 |
| (c) | Sustainable use and protection of water and marine resources | Article 12 | 31 December 2021 | 1 January 2023 |
| (d) | Transition to a circular economy | Article 13 | 31 December 2021 | 1 January 2023 |
| (e) | Pollution prevention and control | Article 14 | 31 December 2021 | 1 January 2023 |
| (f) | Protection and restoration of biodiversity and ecosystems | Article 15 | 31 December 2021 | 1 January 2023 |

Full coverage of each objective lives in [[concepts/six-environmental-objectives|Six Environmental Objectives]]. The mitigation + adaptation TSCs were adopted in the **Climate Delegated Act (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2139)**; the other four objectives' TSCs were adopted in the **Environmental Delegated Act**.

### Categories of contributing activities

The Regulation distinguishes three categories of activity that can substantially contribute:
- **Own-performance activities** — the activity itself directly contributes (default case under Articles 10(1), 11(1), 12(1), 13(1), 14(1), 15(1))
- **Transitional activities** (Article 10(2), mitigation only) — activities for which there is no technologically and economically feasible low-carbon alternative, with GHG emissions corresponding to **best-in-sector performance**, that do not lock in carbon-intensive assets and do not hamper the development and deployment of low-carbon alternatives
- **Enabling activities** (Article 16) — activities that directly enable other activities to make a substantial contribution to one or more objectives, provided they (a) do not lead to lock-in of assets that undermine long-term environmental goals and (b) have a substantial positive environmental impact on a life-cycle basis

## Who Must Comply

The Regulation applies to three categories of actors (Article 1(2)):

- **Member States and the Union** when adopting measures setting requirements for financial market participants or issuers in respect of financial products or corporate bonds marketed as environmentally sustainable
- **Financial market participants** (FMPs as defined in SFDR Article 2(1)) that make available financial products — Articles 5–7 set transparency requirements for pre-contractual disclosures and periodic reports
- **Undertakings subject to NFRD/CSRD non-financial reporting** under Article 19a or Article 29a of Directive 2013/34/EU — Article 8 requires Taxonomy KPI disclosure

In practice, this captures:
- All large undertakings in scope of [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]] (replaces the original NFRD scope, which Article 8 still references — CSRD amended that Directive and broadened the disclosure population)
- All financial-product manufacturers and distributors regulated under SFDR

Insurance, banking, and asset management firms have additional sector-specific KPI templates in the Article 8 Delegated Act (Regulation (EU) 2021/2178) — most notably the **Green Asset Ratio (GAR)** for credit institutions.

## Reporting Requirements

The Taxonomy Regulation itself sets disclosure *triggers*, with the detailed templates and KPI methodology delegated to the Article 8 Delegated Act. The high-level requirements:

### Article 5 — Financial products with sustainable investment objective
Where a product invests in an economic activity contributing to an environmental objective per SFDR Article 9, pre-contractual and periodic disclosures must include:
- The environmental objective(s) under Article 9 of the Taxonomy to which the underlying investment contributes
- A description of how and to what extent the underlying investments are in Taxonomy-aligned economic activities, including the proportion of enabling and transitional activities, expressed as a percentage of all investments in the product

### Article 6 — Financial products promoting environmental characteristics
SFDR Article 8 products: Article 5 applies *mutatis mutandis*, with a mandatory disclaimer that the DNSH principle applies only to the Taxonomy-aligned portion of the underlying investments.

### Article 7 — Other financial products
Products outside SFDR Articles 8 and 9 must disclose: *"The investments underlying this financial product do not take into account the EU criteria for environmentally sustainable economic activities."*

### Article 8 — Non-financial undertakings (Taxonomy KPIs)

Undertakings publishing a non-financial statement under Articles 19a or 29a of Directive 2013/34/EU must disclose **how and to what extent the undertaking's activities are associated with environmentally sustainable economic activities** under Articles 3 and 9.

Specifically, **non-financial undertakings** must disclose:
- The proportion of **turnover** derived from products or services associated with Taxonomy-aligned economic activities
- The proportion of **CapEx** and the proportion of **OpEx** related to assets or processes associated with Taxonomy-aligned economic activities

Detailed content, presentation, and methodology — including the templates for both eligibility and alignment KPIs and the bank/insurance-specific KPIs — are set in **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2178** (the "Article 8 Delegated Act"), adopted on 6 July 2021 per Article 8(4).

In the CSRD reporting context, Article 8 KPIs are presented as part of the **environmental information block** of the sustainability statement (per [[regulations/esrs-1|ESRS 1]] Appendix D), alongside ESRS E1–E5.

## Penalties

Article 22 leaves measures and penalties applicable to infringements of Articles 5, 6 and 7 (the financial-market-participant transparency provisions) to **Member State** rules. Member States must ensure that penalties are *effective, proportionate, and dissuasive*. Article 21 designates the SFDR competent authorities under Article 14(1) of Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 to monitor FMP compliance.

For **Article 8 disclosures by non-financial undertakings**, enforcement runs through the same channel as the underlying non-financial reporting obligation — via NFRD/CSRD national transposition. Member State penalties for CSRD breaches accordingly apply to Article 8 Taxonomy KPI disclosure failures.

The Regulation does not specify EU-level monetary penalty amounts.

## Calculation Requirements

The Taxonomy Regulation itself does **not** contain calculation procedures, emission factors, or quantitative thresholds. Its operative substance — the activity-by-activity rules that determine substantial contribution and DNSH — is delegated entirely to the Commission Delegated Acts adopted under Articles 10(3), 11(3), 12(2), 13(2), 14(2) and 15(2).

The **technical screening criteria (TSCs)** that determine alignment for each in-scope activity are set in:

- **Climate Delegated Act — Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2139** (consolidated 08.01.2025 incorporating amendments M1 (2022/1214), M2 (2023/2485), M3 (2024/3215)) — TSCs for substantial contribution to climate change **mitigation** (Annex I) and **adaptation** (Annex II), and the corresponding DNSH criteria for those activities. Sector coverage in Annex I (mitigation) includes forestry, environmental protection and restoration, manufacturing, energy, water/sewerage/waste/remediation, transport, construction and real estate, information and communication, and professional/scientific/technical activities. Annex II (adaptation) covers all of the above plus financial and insurance activities (non-life insurance, reinsurance), education, human health and social work activities, and arts/entertainment/recreation — adaptation criteria reach a broader sectoral surface than mitigation. (→ [[sources/eu-taxonomy-climate-delegated-act-2021-2139|Climate Delegated Act 2021/2139]])
- **Environmental Delegated Act — Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2486** — TSCs for the remaining four objectives (water/marine, circular economy, pollution, biodiversity).
- **Article 8 Delegated Act — Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2178** — defines the **KPI calculation methodology** (turnover / CapEx / OpEx numerators and denominators) used to compute and disclose the proportion of an undertaking's activities that are Taxonomy-aligned.

### Climate Delegated Act — Annex I and Annex II structure

Each Annex is organised as a numbered hierarchical catalogue of economic activities (e.g., 4.1 Electricity generation using solar PV, 4.29 Electricity generation from fossil gaseous fuels, 6.5 Transport by motorbikes, passenger cars and light commercial vehicles), each cross-referenced to a NACE Rev. 2 code per Regulation (EC) No 1893/2006. For every activity, the Annex specifies in a uniform structure:

1. **Description of the activity** with NACE code mapping
2. **Substantial-contribution criteria** to the relevant objective (mitigation in Annex I, adaptation in Annex II)
3. **DNSH criteria** for each of the other five environmental objectives — typically by reference to the generic Appendices plus activity-specific overlays
4. Where applicable, a flag classifying the activity as **enabling** (per Article 16 of the Taxonomy Regulation) or **transitional** (per Article 10(2), mitigation only)

### Headline TSC thresholds invoked by the Climate Delegated Act

The substantive thresholds most relevant to GHG accounting practitioners — these are regulatory thresholds for Taxonomy alignment, not emission factors:

- **Power generation, co-generation, and most heat/cool generation** (Sections 4.1–4.8, 4.13, 4.17–4.24, 4.28, 4.29): **life-cycle GHG emissions <100 g CO2e/kWh** is the universal substantial-contribution ceiling. Renewables (solar PV, CSP, wind, ocean, hydropower, geothermal) qualify subject to project-level life-cycle assessment; bioenergy, hydropower (additional eligibility tests), geothermal, hydrogen, and renewable gaseous/liquid fuels must affirmatively demonstrate the threshold using **Recommendation 2013/179/EU (Product Environmental Footprint)** or, alternatively, **ISO 14067:2018** or **ISO 14064-1:2018**.
- **Fossil gas power generation (Section 4.29)** — *transitional under Article 10(2), added by M1 amendment 2022/1214*: meets the criterion if life-cycle <100 g CO2e/kWh **or** for facilities permitted by 2030-12-31 the activity satisfies seven cumulative tests, including direct GHG emissions <270 g CO2e/kWh **or** annual average <550 kg CO2e/kW over 20 years; switch to renewable/low-carbon gases by 2035-12-31 with management-approved plan; ≥55% lifetime GHG reduction; replaces existing solid- or liquid-fossil-fuel generation with ≤15% capacity uplift; and Member-State coal phase-out commitment in the relevant integrated NECP. **Annual independent third-party verification** of compliance is mandatory. Parallel transitional rules exist for gas-fired co-generation (Section 4.30) and gas-fired district heat (Section 4.31).
- **Nuclear (Sections 4.26, 4.27, 4.28)** — *transitional, added by M1*: pre-commercial advanced nuclear, new plants using best-available technologies (construction permit by 2045), and existing installations with modifications permitted by 2040 — all subject to life-cycle GHG <100 g CO2e/kWh and accident-tolerant fuel deployment timelines.
- **Cement (Section 3.7)** — *transitional*: grey cement clinker **<0.722 tCO2e/t clinker**; cement (or alternative binder) **<0.469 tCO2e/t cement** (clinker-to-cement ratio assumption 0.65). Both thresholds derive from the EU ETS benchmark for the 10% best-performing installations 2016–2017 per Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/447, creating a direct linkage between Taxonomy alignment and EU ETS benchmark methodology.
- **Iron and steel (Section 3.9)** — *transitional*: per-product thresholds derived from EU ETS product benchmarks: hot metal 1.331 tCO2e/t, sintered ore 0.163 tCO2e/t, coke (excl. lignite coke) 0.144 tCO2e/t, iron casting 0.299 tCO2e/t, EAF high-alloy steel 0.266 tCO2e/t, EAF carbon steel 0.209 tCO2e/t.
- **Aluminium (Section 3.8)** — *transitional*: primary aluminium ≤1.484 tCO2e/t until 2025 (derived from EU ETS benchmark) **and** indirect emissions intensity of grid electricity ≤100 g CO2e/kWh (mirroring the power-generation threshold); secondary aluminium qualifies categorically.
- **Hydrogen (Section 3.10)**: life-cycle GHG savings ≥73.4% vs the 94 g CO2e/MJ fossil-fuel comparator (i.e., <3 tCO2e/tH2); hydrogen-based synthetic fuels ≥70% savings.
- **Other manufacturing thresholds** (per-product, Sections 3.11–3.17): carbon black, soda ash, chlorine, organic basic chemicals (HVC 0.693 tCO2e/t HVC, aromatics, vinyl chloride, styrene, ethylene oxide/glycols), anhydrous ammonia, nitric acid, plastics in primary form — each with its own EU ETS-aligned numerical threshold.
- **Light vehicles M1/N1 (Section 6.5)**: tailpipe CO2 <50 g CO2/km until 2025; **0 g CO2/km from 2026-01-01** (mirrors EU passenger-car CO2 standards Regulation (EU) 2019/631).
- **Heavy-duty vehicles, buses, trains, ships, aircraft (Sections 6.1–6.12)**: in general, **zero direct (tailpipe) CO2 emissions**; transitional rules for inland and maritime vessels capable of running on zero-emission fuels (M2 amendment tightened from 2026-01-01); freight road transport thresholds expressed in g CO2/tkm with a transitional pathway to zero. Freight rail must meet zero-direct-tailpipe CO2 (or zero on electrified track).
- **Buildings (Section 7.1, 7.2)**: new buildings — net primary energy demand at least **10% lower than the NZEB national threshold** under EPBD; for buildings >5,000 m², the embodied **Global Warming Potential** must be calculated and published for each life-cycle stage in **kg CO2e/m²** of useful internal floor area. Renovation must achieve at least 30% primary energy reduction (or "major renovation" under EPBD).

### Cross-cutting requirements for all TSCs (Article 19 of Regulation (EU) 2020/852)

The Commission must ensure all TSCs:
- Identify the most relevant potential contributions to the given objective while respecting **technological neutrality**
- Specify minimum requirements to avoid significant harm to any other relevant objective
- Be **quantitative with thresholds** to the extent possible, otherwise qualitative
- Be based on **conclusive scientific evidence** and the **precautionary principle** (Article 191 TFEU)
- Take into account the **life cycle**, including existing life-cycle assessments
- Take into account the **nature and scale** of the activity, and whether it is enabling (Article 16) or transitional (Article 10(2))
- Cover all relevant economic activities within a sector, treating them equally where they contribute equally
- Be easy to use and verifiable
- **Exclude solid fossil fuel power generation** from qualifying as environmentally sustainable (Article 19(3))

The Climate Delegated Act operationalises every one of these constraints — the EU ETS-benchmark linkage embodies "best-in-sector" performance for transitional activities; the PEF/ISO 14067/ISO 14064-1 invocation embodies "life cycle"; the Appendix A climate-risk-assessment requirement embodies "scale and adaptation context"; the third-party verifier regime for fossil gas and nuclear embodies "verifiable."

### DNSH structure under the Climate Delegated Act

DNSH is implemented through five Appendices to Annex I (A–E) and four to Annex II (A–D). Each in-scope activity's DNSH section references the relevant Appendix, then layers activity-specific requirements on top:

- **Appendix A — Generic DNSH criteria for climate change adaptation:** required across virtually every activity in Annex I. The undertaking must perform a robust **climate-risk and vulnerability assessment** using best-available climate projections (RCP scenarios per IPCC), identify material physical climate risks over the activity's expected lifetime, and implement **adaptation solutions** for those risks that do not increase risk elsewhere. Annex II expands this into the substantial-contribution test itself (since adaptation *is* the objective there).
- **Appendix B — Generic DNSH criteria for sustainable use and protection of water and marine resources:** environmental degradation risk identification per EU Water Framework Directive 2000/60/EC, Marine Strategy Framework Directive 2008/56/EC; for water-using activities, water appliance specifications referencing Appendix E (taps, showers, urinals, WCs by litres/min and litres/flush); references to Drinking Water Directive (EU) 2020/2184.
- **Appendix C — Generic DNSH criteria for pollution prevention and control regarding use and presence of chemicals:** restrictions referencing REACH (Regulation (EC) 1907/2006), POPs Regulation (EU) 2019/1021, Mercury Regulation (EU) 2017/852, the Stockholm Convention, and substance-specific bans (e.g., ozone-depleting substances under Regulation (EC) 1005/2009 / (EU) 2024/590).
- **Appendix D — Generic DNSH criteria for protection and restoration of biodiversity and ecosystems:** Environmental Impact Assessment under Directive 2011/92/EU (and Strategic EIA under Directive 2001/42/EC); compliance with Habitats Directive 92/43/EEC and Birds Directive 2009/147/EC including Article 6 appropriate assessment for Natura 2000 sites; primary-forest and high-biodiversity-value protections.
- **Appendix E (Annex I only) — Technical specifications for water appliances** referenced from buildings activities.

On top of generic Appendix references, many activities carry **activity-specific DNSH overlays**. Examples:
- **Heat pumps (Section 4.16)**: refrigerant Global Warming Potential **≤675**; outdoor sound power level limits.
- **Combustion installations**: emissions within the BAT-AEL ranges per Industrial Emissions Directive 2010/75/EU BAT conclusions for large combustion plants.
- **Cement, steel, aluminium**: BAT conclusions for the relevant industrial sector under IED.
- **Transport**: noise limits under Regulation (EU) No 540/2014.

### Linkage to GHG Protocol and EU ETS measurement methodologies

The Climate Delegated Act does not redefine measurement — it invokes existing methodologies:
- **EU ETS verified emissions** (per [[sources/eu-ets-mrr-2018-2066|MRR 2018/2066]] and [[sources/eu-ets-avr-2018-2067|AVR 2018/2067]]) underpin the per-product benchmarks in cement, steel, aluminium, and chemicals — making EU ETS-regulated installations the de facto data source for those activities' Taxonomy alignment determinations.
- **PEF Recommendation 2013/179/EU** is the default life-cycle GHG methodology invoked for power, hydrogen, and fuels; **ISO 14067:2018** (carbon footprint of products) and **ISO 14064-1:2018** (organisation-level GHG quantification) are accepted alternatives.
- **CO2 fleet emissions standards** (Regulation (EU) 2019/631 for cars/vans; Regulation (EU) 2019/1242 for HDVs) provide the measurement basis for vehicle TSCs.
- The corporate-inventory GHG Protocol Scope 1/2/3 framework is *not* directly invoked for activity-level alignment determinations, but underpins the [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]] [[regulations/esrs-e1|E1]] disclosures with which Taxonomy KPIs are reported alongside.

### Article 8 KPI computation

The **Article 8 Delegated Act (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2178)** defines the KPI calculation methodology — turnover, CapEx, and OpEx numerators and denominators, presentation templates, and sector-specific KPIs (Green Asset Ratio for credit institutions; insurance underwriting KPI from Annex II Section 10 of the Climate DA). Climate DA TSC compliance is the input that determines whether a given revenue stream or asset enters the *aligned* numerator.

The **Platform on Sustainable Finance** (Article 20) advises the Commission on TSC development, review, usability, and the Article 18 minimum safeguards. The **Member State Expert Group on Sustainable Finance** (Article 24) provides Member State input. Article 2a (added by M1) requires the Commission to review the dates in Annex I Sections 4.27, 4.28, 4.29(1)(b), 4.30(1)(b) and 4.31(1)(b) — i.e., the nuclear and fossil gas transitional permit and switch deadlines — when conducting the periodic Article 19(5) Taxonomy Regulation review.
