## Overview

California SB 253, the **Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act (CCDAA)**, was signed into law on October 7, 2023. It adds Health and Safety Code Section 38532 and requires large companies doing business in California to publicly disclose their Scope 1, 2, and 3 greenhouse gas emissions annually, in conformance with the GHG Protocol.

SB 253 is the broadest corporate GHG disclosure mandate in U.S. history by applicability. It covers any qualifying company doing business in California — not just California-based companies.

(→ [[sources/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253 Source]])

## Who Must Comply

**Reporting entities** — any partnership, corporation, LLC, or other business entity that:
- Has annual revenues **exceeding $1,000,000,000** (determined by prior fiscal year), AND
- Does **business in California**

There is no requirement to be headquartered in California or incorporated in California. Any company with revenue above the threshold that conducts any business activity in California qualifies.

The University of California applies only if "the Regents of the University of California, by resolution, make any of these provisions applicable."

**Revenue definition (adopted — Title 17 CCR §96072(a)(13)):** Revenue means **gross receipts** as defined in California Revenue and Taxation Code §25120(f)(2) — the sum of money and fair market value of property/services received from sales, services, or use of property in transactions producing business income. Revenue is **not** reduced by cost of goods sold or operating costs. This catches high-volume/low-margin businesses (retailers, distributors) that might otherwise appear small on a net-revenue basis. Revenue is determined using the lesser of the entity's two prior fiscal years to prevent one-off asset sales from cycling companies in and out of compliance. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation]])

**"Doing business in California" (adopted — Title 17 CCR §96072(a)(7)–(8)):** An entity does business in California if it is (a) actively engaging in any transaction for financial gain [RTC §23101(a)] AND meets at least one of: (b)(1) organized or commercially domiciled in California, OR (b)(2) California sales exceeding $735,019 (2024 inflation-adjusted threshold). The property-holding [§23101(b)(3)] and payroll [§23101(b)(4)] prongs are explicitly excluded — these lower thresholds ($73,502) would otherwise catch companies whose only California presence is a small number of teleworking employees. Wholesale electricity sales do not count toward California sales under §23101(b)(2). (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation]])

**Exemptions (codified — Title 17 CCR §96071):**
- Non-profit or charitable organizations that are tax-exempt under the Internal Revenue Code
- Business entities subject to regulation by the California Department of Insurance, or in the insurance business in any other state
- Federal, state, and local government entities, and companies majority-owned (>50%) by government entities
- Entities whose **only** California activity consists of wholesale electricity transactions
- Entities whose **only** California presence is teleworking employees

**Parent-level consolidation (added by SB 219):** A parent company may file a single consolidated report covering qualifying subsidiaries. Subsidiaries covered by a parent report need not file separately. (→ [[regulations/sb219|SB 219]])

## Reporting Requirements

**Accounting standard (mandatory):** Reporting must conform to:
- GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard
- GHG Protocol Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Accounting and Reporting Standard

Entities may submit reports already prepared for other national or international requirements (e.g., EU CSRD, SEC climate disclosure) if those reports satisfy SB 253's requirements — avoiding duplicative reporting where possible.

Existing CARB MRR reporters (under Health and Safety Code Section 38530) may use that data as part of their SB 253 disclosure. Note that MRR uses EPA 40 CFR Part 98 calculation methods, while SB 253 requires GHG Protocol methods — the same physical emissions may produce different calculated totals depending on which framework is applied. (→ [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]])

**What is disclosed:**
- Scope 1 emissions (direct, owned/controlled sources)
- Scope 2 emissions (purchased electricity, steam, heat, cooling)
- Scope 3 emissions (all other value chain indirect emissions)
- Entity name, trade names, and logos
- Structural changes (acquisitions, divestments, mergers)

**Platform:** CARB contracts with an emissions reporting organization (nonprofit) to host a public digital platform. Disclosures posted within 30 days of receipt. Data downloadable in electronic format; multi-year aggregated views required.

**Draft Scope 1/2 Reporting Template (voluntary for 2026):** CARB released a draft reporting template on 2025-10-10 to streamline disclosure, especially for first-time reporters. Use is voluntary for the 2026 reporting cycle; CARB will address mandatory template requirements through subsequent rulemaking.

Template sections: (1) Organization Information, (2) Third-Party Verification, (3) Inventory Boundary, (4) Scope 1 and Scope 2 Disclosure — by emission source (electricity, fuel use, refrigerants, etc.) with optional breakdown by GHG type, (5) Methodology — including GWP source and year, emission factor sources, and calculation approach, (6) De Minimis/Minor Sources, (7) California MRR Fields — MRR facility ID numbers to cross-reference the CARB MRR database, (8) Emission Reductions — direct contracts for renewable electricity or renewable gas (optional).

Required decision-useful fields: at least one **intensity metric** (e.g., MT CO2e per $M revenue); **NAICS 2-digit sector** classification.

**Open policy question — organizational boundary approach:** As of October 2025, CARB was evaluating whether to mandate one boundary approach (equity share, financial control, or operational control) for all reporters to support comparability, or retain all three options. This question was not resolved by the February 2026 Initial Regulation and may be addressed in subsequent rulemaking. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-template-memo|CARB Template Memo Oct 2025]])

## Deadlines

| Date | Obligation |
|---|---|
| 2025-01-01 | CARB must adopt implementing regulations (extended to July 1, 2025 by SB 219; Initial Regulation finally adopted February 26, 2026) |
| **2026-08-10** | First Scope 1 & 2 disclosure (annual thereafter) — official deadline set by Title 17 CCR §96076; aligns with MRR verification deadline. First-year report may be based on best-available data; entities not collecting data at the time of the December 2024 Enforcement Notice are not expected to submit for 2026. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation]]) |
| 2027 (schedule set by CARB) | First Scope 3 disclosure (annual thereafter) — per SB 219, the schedule is set by CARB regulation, no longer fixed at 180 days after Scope 1/2 |
| 2027-01-01 | CARB determines whether Scope 3 assurance will be required |
| 2029 | CARB reviews and updates Scope 3 disclosure deadlines |
| 2030-01-01 | Scope 1 & 2 escalates to reasonable assurance |
| 2030-01-01 | Scope 3 limited assurance begins (if CARB required it by Jan 1, 2027) |
| 2033 and every 5 years | CARB reviews GHG Protocol standard; may adopt alternatives |

## Penalties

| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Nonfiling, late filing, or other noncompliance | Up to **$500,000** per reporting year |
| Scope 3 misstatement (2027–2030) | **No penalty** if made with reasonable basis and disclosed in good faith |
| Scope 3 nonfiling (2027–2030) | **Penalizable** — only nonfiling triggers penalties during this phase-in period |

Penalties imposed through CARB administrative hearing process (Title 17, California Code of Regulations). Good faith measures to comply are a mitigating factor.

## Calculation Requirements

Reporting must follow the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard (Scope 1/2) and Scope 3 Standard (Scope 3). Starting 2033, CARB reviews accounting standards every five years and may adopt alternatives.

**Assurance standards under consideration:** CARB identified four candidate standards: ISSA 5000 (IAASB), AA1000, ISO 14060 family, and AICPA. Final assurance standard selection is deferred to the subsequent rulemaking. (→ [[sources/carb-workshop-aug-2025|CARB Workshop Aug 2025]])

**Assurance timeline (confirmed by statute and FAQ):** Limited assurance required beginning 2026 (statutory); reasonable assurance required beginning 2030 (statutory). The 2026 assurance requirement is not expected to apply to the first-year good-faith report — specific assurance requirements for 2026 reporting will be addressed in the subsequent rulemaking. (→ [[sources/carb-faq-july2025|CARB FAQ July 2025]])

**Limited assurance defined by CARB:** The initial assurance level is "limited" — a negative-form statement ("nothing has come to our attention that causes us to believe the report is not materially correct"). It involves sampling plans, limited data checks, limited conformance checks, process documentation, and a log of found-and-corrected errors. Material misstatement threshold: 5% of covered emissions.

**Annual implementation fee (adopted):** Flat-rate fee per entity, calculated annually as Total Required Revenue ÷ number of entities (separately for SB 253 and SB 261 programs). CPI-adjusted annually from FY 2027-28 onward. Fee notices issued by September 10 each year; payment due within 60 days. Exact dollar amounts set annually; pre-adoption estimates (~$3,106 SB 253 fee; ~$1,403 SB 261 fee) were workshop projections, not binding figures. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation §§96073–96074]])
