## Overview

ESRS 1 (General Requirements) is the architectural cross-cutting standard that establishes the framework for all other ESRS. It contains no Disclosure Requirements of its own; instead it codifies the rules that govern *how* every topical standard is applied — what counts as material, how to assess materiality, what time horizons to use, how to handle the value chain, and which transitional reliefs apply.

ESRS 1 is one of two cross-cutting standards in the first ESRS set (the other is [[regulations/esrs-2|ESRS 2]]). Together, ESRS 1 and ESRS 2 apply to the sustainability matters covered by every topical and (future) sector-specific standard. ESRS 1 is the rules-of-the-road; ESRS 2 is the cross-cutting reporting checklist.

The standard introduces three categories of ESRS:
1. **Cross-cutting standards** — ESRS 1 and ESRS 2; apply to all sustainability matters
2. **Topical standards** — Environmental (E1–E5), Social (S1–S4), Governance (G1); sector-agnostic
3. **Sector-specific standards** — apply to all undertakings within a sector; not yet adopted as of 2026-04-24 (deferred to a future Commission Delegated Act)

(→ [[sources/esrs-set1-2023-2772|ESRS Set 1 — Commission Delegated Regulation 2023/2772]])

## Who Must Comply

ESRS 1 applies to every undertaking in scope of CSRD (→ [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]]). Because ESRS 1 sets the rules under which all other standards operate, no in-scope undertaking can apply ESRS without applying ESRS 1 — there is no materiality assessment that can switch off ESRS 1 itself.

ESRS 1 has no Disclosure Requirements, so the question "does ESRS 1 apply" is functionally "do you have to do a Double Materiality Assessment, follow value-chain rules, and use the ESRS time-horizon definitions?" — yes, always, for every CSRD reporter.

## Reporting Requirements

ESRS 1 does not contain Disclosure Requirements. It contains **rules and definitions** that the undertaking must apply when preparing every other part of its sustainability statement. The major rule areas are:

### Categories and architecture (Chapter 1)
- The 3-tier ESRS hierarchy (cross-cutting, topical, sector-specific)
- The 4 reporting areas: **Governance (GOV), Strategy (SBM), Impact/Risk/Opportunity Management (IRO), Metrics & Targets (MT)**
- The 4 Minimum Disclosure Requirements: **MDR-P** (policies), **MDR-A** (actions), **MDR-M** (metrics), **MDR-T** (targets) — applied jointly with topical disclosure requirements
- Drafting conventions: "shall disclose" = mandatory, "may disclose" = voluntary, "shall consider" = required input where applicable

### Qualitative characteristics (Chapter 2 + Appendix B)
- Fundamental: **relevance** and **faithful representation**
- Enhancing: **comparability**, **verifiability**, **understandability**

### Double materiality (Chapter 3) — see [[concepts/double-materiality|Double Materiality]]
- **Impact materiality** — material when the undertaking has actual or potential impacts on people/environment connected to its operations or upstream/downstream value chain. Severity = scale + scope + irremediable character (likelihood added for potential impacts; for human rights, severity takes precedence over likelihood).
- **Financial materiality** — material when the matter triggers, or could reasonably be expected to trigger, material financial effects on financial position, performance, cash flows, access to finance, or cost of capital over short/medium/long term.
- A matter is material if either dimension is material; the two are inter-related and the assessment generally starts with impact.
- **Climate special rule (ESRS 1 paragraph 32):** if the undertaking concludes climate change is non-material, it must provide a **detailed forward-looking justification** including conditions that could lead climate to become material. For all other topics, a brief explanation suffices.
- **Implementation guidance:** EFRAG IG 1 (May 2024) operationalises Chapter 3 with a four-step assessment process and threshold-setting mechanics — see [[methodologies/double-materiality-assessment|Double Materiality Assessment]] and (→ [[sources/efrag-ig1-materiality-2024|EFRAG IG 1]]).

### Due diligence (Chapter 4)
ESRS does not impose conduct requirements but reflects the OECD Guidelines + UN Guiding Principles due-diligence steps in disclosure requirements:
- (a) Embed due diligence in governance/strategy/business model — ESRS 2 GOV-2, GOV-3, SBM-3
- (b) Engage with affected stakeholders — ESRS 2 GOV-2, SBM-2, IRO-1, MDR-P, topical SBM-2 equivalents
- (c) Identify and assess negative impacts — ESRS 2 IRO-1, SBM-3
- (d) Take action — ESRS 2 MDR-A, topical actions DRs
- (e) Track effectiveness — ESRS 2 MDR-M, MDR-T, topical metrics/targets

### Value chain (Chapter 5)
- The sustainability statement is for the same reporting undertaking as the financial statements (same consolidation perimeter).
- Reporting is **extended** beyond the financial perimeter to include material upstream and downstream value chain impacts, risks, and opportunities — but only for parts of the value chain where the matter is material.
- For associates and joint ventures (equity-method or proportional consolidation in financial statements), include material value-chain information consistent with other business relationships; impact metrics use the *full* impact data, not the share of equity held.
- **Sector averages and proxies are explicitly permitted** when the undertaking cannot collect direct value-chain information after reasonable efforts. This is the legal basis for spend-based and other proxy methods used in Scope 3 calculation (→ [[concepts/scope-3]]).
- Section 10.2 transitional provision: 3-year relief allowing exclusion of metric-level value-chain information not reasonably available, with disclosure of efforts.
- **Implementation guidance:** EFRAG IG 2 (May 2024) operationalises Chapter 5 and §10.2 with a financial-control-vs-operational-control decision tree, the Seren Group worked example, and a tiered data-collection approach — see [[methodologies/double-materiality-assessment|Double Materiality Assessment]] and (→ [[sources/efrag-ig2-value-chain-2024|EFRAG IG 2]]).

### Time horizons (Chapter 6)
- Reporting period = financial reporting period
- **Short-term** = the financial reporting period
- **Medium-term** = end of short-term up to 5 years
- **Long-term** = more than 5 years (with optional further breakdowns when relevant)
- Industry-specific overrides allowed under ESRS 2 BP-2 paragraph 9 (e.g., long-cycle industries with multi-decade asset lives).
- Topical ESRS may set different definitions for specific items, in which case the topical definition prevails.

### Preparation and presentation (Chapter 7)
- Comparative prior-period information mandatory for quantitative metrics (with first-year transitional relief)
- Disclose sources of estimation uncertainty
- Errors in prior periods: restate where impracticable
- Subsidiary exemption: subsidiary may omit if parent's consolidated sustainability report covers it
- Classified/sensitive information protections
- May voluntarily disclose opportunities

### Structure of sustainability statement (Chapter 8 + Appendix D)
The statement is organized as a **single section of the management report** with four parts:
1. **General information** — ESRS 2 disclosures (GOV/SBM/IRO/MT/MDR cross-cutting)
2. **Environmental information** — Article 8 of EU Taxonomy Regulation 2020/852 disclosures + ESRS E1, E2, E3, E4, E5
3. **Social information** — ESRS S1, S2, S3, S4
4. **Governance information** — ESRS G1

### Linkages and connectivity (Chapter 9)
- Information may be incorporated by reference from other parts of the management report or separate documents (with conditions)
- Connected information requirements link sustainability and financial statements — ESRS 2 SBM-3 must reconcile material risks with financial-statement narrative
- Connectivity matters for double materiality: financial-statement risk discussions and sustainability-statement IRO disclosures must be aligned

### Transitional provisions (Chapter 10) — see [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in and Transitional Provisions]]
- 10.1 Entity-specific disclosures — first-year relief
- 10.2 Value chain — 3-year relief
- 10.3 Comparative information — first-year relief
- 10.4 List of phased-in DRs (full table in Appendix C)

## Deadlines

ESRS 1 has no standalone deadline. Its application date follows the CSRD phased rollout — financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2024 for the earliest cohort. See [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]] for the staggered company-level deadlines.

## Penalties

No standalone penalties. Failure to apply ESRS 1 properly (e.g., a defective Double Materiality Assessment) renders the broader sustainability statement non-compliant under CSRD; penalties are then those set by Member State transposition under the CSRD framework.

## Calculation Requirements

ESRS 1 contains no quantitative disclosure requirements. It establishes the **definitions** that quantitative disclosures elsewhere depend on:

- Time-horizon thresholds (short ≤ FY, medium ≤ 5 years, long > 5 years) — used in scenario analysis (E1-1, E1-9), forward-looking metrics, target horizons (MDR-T)
- Value chain inclusion rule — defines the consolidation extension for Scope 3 (→ [[concepts/scope-3]]) and value-chain metrics in S2, S3
- Sector-average / proxy permission — legal basis for spend-based EEIO methods (→ [[methodologies/scope-3-cat1-spend-based]])
- Materiality threshold-setting requirement — quantitative or qualitative thresholds must be established and disclosed
