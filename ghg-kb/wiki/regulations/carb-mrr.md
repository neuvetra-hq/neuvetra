## Overview

The **Mandatory Reporting Regulation (MRR)** is California's facility-level greenhouse gas reporting rule, codified at Title 17, California Code of Regulations (CCR), sections 95100–95163. It was first enacted in 2009 under Health and Safety Code section 38530 (AB 32, the Global Warming Solutions Act). The 2018 amendment was approved by the Office of Administrative Law (OAL) on 2019-03-29 and took full effect for the 2019 data year.

MRR requires operators of industrial facilities, fuel suppliers, and electric power entities in California to monitor, calculate, and annually report GHG emissions to CARB. Its primary purpose is to produce a verified, facility-level emissions dataset that supports:
- California's GHG emissions inventory
- Compliance verification for the Cap-and-Trade Regulation (Title 17 CCR §95800 et seq.)
- Regulatory program development across California

MRR is a **Scope 1-focused, facility-level instrument**. It is complementary to but structurally different from SB 253 (CCDAA), which requires corporate-level Scope 1/2/3 disclosure under GHG Protocol. The two obligations can apply simultaneously to the same company. (→ [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253 — CCDAA]])

MRR incorporates by reference the US EPA GHG Mandatory Reporting Rule (40 CFR Part 98), substituting CARB authority for EPA authority throughout.

(→ [[sources/carb-mrr-2018|CARB MRR 2018 Amendment Source]])

## Who Must Comply

**Four categories of reporting entity:**

### 1. Facility operators

*Always required to report, regardless of emissions level:*
- Electricity generation units reporting CO2 mass emissions year-round under 40 CFR Part 75
- Cement production
- Lime manufacturing
- Nitric acid production
- Petroleum refineries
- Geologic sequestration of carbon dioxide
- Injection of carbon dioxide

*Required when stationary combustion + process emissions of CO2 + CH4 + N2O ≥ **10,000 MT CO2e/year**:*
- Stationary fuel combustion (including electricity generating units not subject to 40 CFR Part 75)
- Glass production
- Hydrogen production
- Iron and steel production
- Pulp and paper manufacturing
- Petroleum and natural gas systems
- Geothermal electricity generation
- Lead production
- Any facility in a US EPA 40 CFR Part 98 subpart category emitting >10,000 MT CO2e

### 2. Fuel and CO2 suppliers

Required when fuel delivered into California, if completely combusted, would release ≥ **10,000 MT CO2e**:
- Position holders at terminals and refiners delivering petroleum fuels and/or biomass-derived fuels
- Importers of transportation fuels outside the bulk transfer/terminal system
- All refiners producing liquefied petroleum gas (regardless of quantity)
- Operators of interstate pipelines delivering natural gas
- Importers of LPG, CNG, or LNG into California
- Local distribution companies and intrastate pipelines delivering natural gas
- Natural gas liquid fractionators (regardless of quantity)
- CO2 producers (regardless of quantity); CO2 importers/exporters ≥ 10,000 MT/year bulk imports/exports
- Facilities making LNG or CNG products from interstate pipeline gas

### 3. Electric power entities

Always required to report:
- Electricity importers and exporters
- Retail providers (including multi-jurisdictional retail providers)
- California Department of Water Resources (DWR)
- Western Area Power Administration (WAPA)
- Bonneville Power Administration (BPA)

### 4. Petroleum and natural gas systems

Required when stationary combustion emissions (including flaring) + process emissions ≥ **10,000 MT CO2e**, OR when total emissions (combustion + process + fugitive + vented) ≥ **25,000 MT CO2e**:
- Offshore and onshore petroleum and natural gas production
- Onshore natural gas processing plants
- Onshore natural gas transmission compression
- Underground natural gas storage
- LNG storage, import, and export facilities
- Natural gas distribution facilities

### Exclusions

Not required to report:
- Generating facilities solely powered by nuclear, hydroelectric, wind, or solar (unless on-site combustion ≥ 10,000 MT CO2e)
- Backup or emergency generators permitted as such by an air district
- Fire suppression systems and portable equipment
- Primary and secondary schools (NAICS 611110)
- Fugitive methane from municipal solid waste landfills (40 CFR Part 98, Subpart HH)
- Fugitive CH4 and N2O from livestock manure management systems (40 CFR Part 98, Subpart JJ)
- Agricultural irrigation pumps

## Reporting Requirements

**Annual emissions data report** must be submitted each year covering:
- All applicable GHG sources within the facility/supplier boundary
- GHG types: CO2, CH4, N2O, SF6, HFCs, PFCs, NF3, and other fluorinated greenhouse gases
- Biomass-derived fuel emissions reported separately from fossil fuel emissions
- Any designated de minimis emissions (≤3% of total CO2e, not exceeding 20,000 MT CO2e)

**Abbreviated reporting** is available for facilities meeting all three conditions: no cap-and-trade compliance obligation during the current compliance period; not subject to 40 CFR Part 98 as applicable here; and total emissions (all sources including fugitives and venting) below 25,000 MT CO2e. Abbreviated reports require fewer data elements and are not subject to third-party verification. Deadline: June 1.

**Third-party verification** is required when any of the following apply:
- Total reported emissions ≥ 25,000 MT CO2e (including CO2 from biomass and geothermal sources)
- The entity is an electricity importer or exporter
- The entity has or has had a cap-and-trade compliance obligation in any year of the current compliance period

Verification types:
- **Full verification** — comprehensive review; required at minimum every 3 years
- **Less intensive verification** — data checks and document review only; permitted in interim years between full verifications

Material misstatement threshold: errors >5% of reported covered emissions or covered product data.

Verification bodies must maintain a minimum of $4,000,000 USD in professional liability insurance.

**Cap-and-Trade linkage:** Facilities exceeding 25,000 MT CO2e of "covered emissions" incur a compliance obligation under California's Cap-and-Trade Regulation (Title 17 CCR §95812). MRR data is the verified foundation for cap-and-trade compliance. Entities that fail to submit or verify emissions data by applicable deadlines receive an "assigned emissions level" set by the Executive Officer.

## Deadlines

| Date | Obligation | Applicable Entity |
|---|---|---|
| April 10 (annual) | Full emissions data report | Facility operators and suppliers |
| June 1 (annual) | Abbreviated emissions data report | Facilities <25,000 MT CO2e, no C&T obligation |
| June 1 (annual) | Full emissions data report | Electric power entities |
| August 10 (annual) | Third-party verification statement | All entities required to verify |

All deadlines are for the preceding calendar year's data.

**Cessation of reporting:**
- Entities with cap-and-trade compliance obligations: may cease when covered emissions are below 25,000 MT CO2e for an entire subsequent compliance period.
- Entities without compliance obligations: may cease when total reported emissions are below 10,000 MT CO2e for 3 consecutive years.
- Permanently shut-down facilities: must report for the shutdown year and the first full year of non-operation, then may cease.

**Record retention:** 10 years after final report submission for entities that had a compliance obligation; 5 years for entities without.

## Penalties

Penalties are assessed under Health and Safety Code section 38580. The MRR defines violation triggers; dollar amounts are set by statute:

| Violation | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Late, incomplete, or inaccurate report | Each day or portion thereof = separate violation |
| Unreported emissions | Each metric ton of CO2e emitted but not reported = separate violation |
| Measurement/recordkeeping failure | Each failure to measure, collect, record, or preserve required data = separate violation |

ARB considers the reporting entity's size and complexity, pattern of violations, and other factors in H&S Code §42403(b) when determining penalty amounts. ARB will not initiate per-ton enforcement until after the applicable verification deadline.

Violations may also be enjoined under H&S Code §41513. The Executive Officer may revoke or modify any Executive Order issued under MRR as a sanction.

## Calculation Requirements

**Methodology base:** 40 CFR Part 98 (US EPA GHG Mandatory Reporting Rule), as published in the Federal Register on October 30, 2009 and subsequent amendments through April 25, 2011, plus California-specific modifications. Where the federal rule says "Administrator," read "Executive Officer of CARB." Where the federal rule says "EPA," read "ARB."

**GHGs covered:** CO2, CH4, N2O, SF6, HFCs, PFCs, NF3, and other fluorinated greenhouse gases as defined in §95102.

**GWP values used for CO2e conversion:**
- Data years 2011–2020: GWP values from 40 CFR Part 98, Table A-1 (published 2009-10-30 in Federal Register)
- Data years 2021 and onward: Updated GWP values from 40 CFR Part 98, Table A-1 (published 2014-12-11)

**Measurement accuracy:** All flow meters and measurement devices used for covered emissions calculations must be calibrated within ±5% accuracy and recalibrated at the more frequent of: manufacturer recommendation, applicable 40 CFR Part 98 subpart frequency, or once every 36 months.

**De minimis:** Up to 3% of total facility CO2e (not exceeding 20,000 MT CO2e) may be estimated using alternative methods, subject to verifier concurrence. De minimis emissions must still be reported.

> **Methodology conflict note:** SB 253 (CCDAA) mandates GHG Protocol methods for corporate Scope 1/2/3 disclosure. CARB MRR mandates EPA 40 CFR Part 98 methods for facility-level reporting. Both obligations can apply to the same company for the same physical emissions. The calculated totals may differ due to differing emission factors and calculation approaches. SB 253 explicitly permits MRR data to be used as input to corporate disclosure, but this does not eliminate the methodological distinction.
