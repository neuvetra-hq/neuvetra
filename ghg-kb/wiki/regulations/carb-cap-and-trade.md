## Overview

The **California Cap-and-Trade / Cap-and-Invest Regulation** (Title 17, California Code of Regulations, sections 95801–96022) is California's market-based compliance mechanism for GHG emissions reduction. It was authorized by AB 32 (HSC §38570) and first became operative for large industrial facilities and electricity generators in 2013. The regulation is currently effective as amended through 2019 (OAL-approved 2019-03-29); 2026 amendments are under active rulemaking.

CARB began rebranding the program as **"Cap-and-Invest"** approximately 2024–2025, emphasizing how auction proceeds are reinvested into climate programs through the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund. The regulatory text retains the original "Cap-and-Trade" designation.

**Core mechanism:** The regulation establishes a declining annual aggregate emissions cap. Covered entities must hold one compliance instrument (allowance or offset credit) for each metric ton of CO2e of covered emissions. CARB conducts quarterly allowance auctions through the California Integrated Emissions Tracking System and Tracking Portal (**CITSS**). Allowances may be allocated, banked, or transferred between registered entities.

The 25,000 MT CO2e threshold in MRR is the operational link between the two regulations — MRR-verified covered emissions at or above this threshold trigger a cap-and-trade compliance obligation. (→ [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]])

## Who Must Comply

A cap-and-trade compliance obligation arises when a facility's MRR-verified **covered emissions** reach or exceed **25,000 MT CO2e** in any year (§95812).

**Covered sectors include:**
- Large industrial facilities: cement, petroleum refining, hydrogen production, glass, lime, nitric acid, iron and steel, pulp and paper
- Electricity generating units
- Natural gas suppliers and local distribution companies
- Transportation fuel suppliers and petroleum product importers

**CITSS registration** is required for all covered entities. Voluntary market participants (offset project operators, secondary market buyers) may also register.

## Reporting Requirements

Cap-and-Trade compliance is built on top of MRR reporting. Covered entities must:
1. Submit verified annual emissions data through MRR by applicable deadlines (→ [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]])
2. Maintain sufficient compliance instruments in their CITSS account by compliance deadlines
3. Submit annual compliance reports detailing instrument holdings and surrender quantities

MRR data is the verified foundation for each entity's compliance obligation. Entities that fail to submit or verify MRR data by applicable deadlines receive an **assigned emissions level** set by CARB's Executive Officer.

## Deadlines

Cap-and-Trade operates on **three-year compliance periods** with annual interim obligations.

**Compliance periods (§95840):**
| Period | Years |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2013–2014 (transitional) |
| 2 | 2015–2017 |
| 3 | 2018–2020 |
| 4 | 2021–2023 |
| 5 | 2024–2026 |
| 6 | 2027–2029 |
| 7 | 2030–2032 |

**Recurring deadlines:**
| Obligation | Timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Annual interim allowance surrender (~30% of preceding-year covered emissions) | November 1 each year within a compliance period | §95856 |
| Triennial true-up (100% of compliance period covered emissions) | November 1 of the year following each period end | §95856 |
| Quarterly allowance auctions | February, May, August, November | Conducted via CITSS (§95910) |
| Untimely surrender obligation | Within 5 days of next auction or reserve sale | §95857 |
| 2026 regulatory amendments | In active rulemaking (SRIA underway) | Will supersede this version |

## Penalties

Violations of cap-and-trade compliance obligations are enforced under HSC §38580 (the AB 32 enforcement provision). Specific consequences for cap-and-trade violations include:
- Mandatory surrender of additional allowances as a punitive measure
- Civil monetary penalties under the Health and Safety Code
- Injunctive relief

Entities with an "assigned emissions level" (due to MRR non-compliance) face compliance obligations based on CARB's estimate rather than verified data.

## Calculation Requirements

**Covered emissions** (§95852): the subset of MRR-reported emissions subject to a compliance obligation, defined by entity type and fuel/product category. Not all MRR-reported emissions are "covered" for cap-and-trade purposes.

**Compliance instruments (§§95820, 95821):**
- **California GHG Allowances (CCAs):** Each allowance represents the right to emit 1 MT CO2e. Vintage-dated. Issued through quarterly auctions and free allocation (industrial assistance for trade-exposed sectors)
- **ARB Offset Credits and Registry Offset Credits:** Issued under CARB-approved Compliance Offset Protocols; subject to quantitative usage limit (LO)
- **Price Ceiling Units:** Sold at the price ceiling under §95915 if the auction settlement price reaches the ceiling

### Quantitative Offset Usage Limit (§95854)

AB 398 (2017) significantly tightened the share of compliance obligation that may be met with offsets:

| Compliance period | LO (offset usage limit) |
|---|---|
| Periods 1–3 (2013–2020) | 8% |
| Period 4 (2021–2023) | 4% |
| Period 5 (2024–2026) | **4% for data years 2024–2025; 6% for data year 2026** |
| Period 6+ (2027 onward) | 6% |

**AB 398 direct-environmental-benefit requirement:** at least half of offsets used for compliance must come from projects with direct environmental benefits in California (§95854(d)).

### Price-Control Mechanism (Subarticle 10)

Three layered instruments to manage allowance price volatility:

1. **Auction Reserve Price (price floor)** — bids below this price are not accepted at quarterly auctions; rises annually (§95911)
2. **Allowance Price Containment Reserve (APCR)** — two reserve tiers; allowances released for sale at fixed prices when settlement price approaches them (§95913)
3. **Price Ceiling** — hard maximum (§95915). When demanded, the Executive Officer issues Price Ceiling Units at the ceiling price; proceeds are spent to achieve at least an equivalent ton-for-ton GHG reduction that is real, additional, quantifiable, permanent, verifiable, and enforceable

**Voluntary Renewable Electricity (VRE) Program:** Allows electricity importers to use specified qualifying renewable energy to reduce their covered emissions obligation.

### Linkage (Subarticle 12)

Cap-and-Trade is linked to **Québec's cap-and-trade system** under the Western Climate Initiative (WCI). Allowances and offset credits issued by either jurisdiction are interchangeable for compliance. Subarticle 12 also provides a framework for retirement-only limited linkage and approval of additional external programs.
