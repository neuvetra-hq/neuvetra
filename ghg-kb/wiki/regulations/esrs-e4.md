## Overview

ESRS E4 (Biodiversity and Ecosystems) is the topical environmental standard governing biodiversity-related disclosures under the CSRD. It covers the **five direct drivers of biodiversity loss** identified by the IPBES Global Assessment: climate change, land/freshwater/sea use change, direct exploitation, invasive alien species, and pollution. ESRS E4 connects strongly to the **EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030**, the **Kunming–Montréal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF)**, and the **Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures (TNFD)**.

E4 introduces a **transition plan** disclosure (E4-1) modeled on the climate transition plan (E1-1) — making biodiversity the second sustainability matter (after climate) where strategic planning is mandated explicitly at the disclosure level. The standard makes deep land-use-change linkages relevant to land-sector GHG accounting (→ [[concepts/land-use-change-emissions]]).

(→ [[sources/esrs-set1-2023-2772|ESRS Set 1 — Commission Delegated Regulation 2023/2772]])

## Who Must Comply

CSRD reporters for whom **biodiversity and ecosystems are material** under the [[concepts/double-materiality|Double Materiality]] assessment. High-typical-materiality sectors: agriculture & food production, forestry, fisheries, mining, energy infrastructure, real estate (land development), pharmaceuticals (biopiracy/genetic resources), tourism in protected areas.

**Phase-in (size-conditional):** Undertakings ≤750 employees may omit *all* of ESRS E4 for the **first 2 years** of preparation (per [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in]] / Appendix C of ESRS 1).

## Reporting Requirements

### SBM-3 (E4-specific) — Material impacts, risks and opportunities and their interaction with strategy and business model
Layered on top of ESRS 2 SBM-3. Specifically requires identification of material assets/activities related to or impacting biodiversity-sensitive areas (Natura 2000, KBA, IUCN Red List ecosystems).

### IRO-1 (E4-specific) — Process to identify material biodiversity and ecosystems-related IROs
How the undertaking screens its operations and value chain for material biodiversity IROs across the five IPBES direct drivers; site-level screening referencing biodiversity-sensitive area definitions; primary forest, wetlands, and other priority biome screening.

### E4-1 — Transition plan and consideration of biodiversity and ecosystems in strategy and business model
Modeled on E1-1 (climate transition plan). Disclose:
- How strategy and business model are compatible with EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030 and Kunming-Montréal GBF (in particular the Global Goal A — halt and reverse biodiversity loss)
- Locked-in pressures from current operations and assets
- Resilience analysis of business model under climate-biodiversity scenarios
- Plans to reduce material negative impacts on biodiversity

### E4-2 — Policies related to biodiversity and ecosystems
Policies in the structure of ESRS 2 MDR-P. Includes deforestation-free supply chain policy, traceability commitments, no-go area policies, sustainable sourcing.

### E4-3 — Actions and resources related to biodiversity and ecosystems
Action plans in the structure of ESRS 2 MDR-A. Includes mitigation hierarchy actions (avoid, minimize, restore, offset), nature-based solutions, supply chain due diligence on conversion-free commodities (soy, palm oil, beef, timber).

### E4-4 — Targets related to biodiversity and ecosystems
Targets in the structure of ESRS 2 MDR-T. Common targets: zero deforestation by year X, % suppliers verified deforestation-free, hectares restored, no-net-loss in own operations.

### E4-5 — Impact metrics related to biodiversity and ecosystems change
**Quantitative metrics:**
- Land-use change emissions and land-use type changes (in hectares, by ecosystem)
- Number/area of sites in or adjacent to biodiversity-sensitive areas (Natura 2000, IBAs, KBAs)
- Pressures on biodiversity from the five direct drivers (where measurable)
- Where applicable: invasive alien species spread, soil sealing/degradation
- Outcome metrics where available: species observed, ecosystem condition indicators

The metrics are not as standardized as climate metrics; the standard defers significant methodological judgment to the undertaking subject to ESRS 2 MDR-M (methodology, assumptions, comparability).

### E4-6 — Anticipated financial effects from material biodiversity and ecosystem-related impacts, risks and opportunities
- Material risks (transition risks from biodiversity regulation, physical risks from ecosystem service loss, reputational risks)
- Material opportunities (nature-positive products and services, blue/green markets)
- **Subject to phase-in:** first-year omission permitted; first 3 years qualitative-only reporting permitted
- See [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in]]

## Deadlines

No standalone deadline. CSRD phased rollout applies — see [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]].

**Important phase-in:** Undertakings ≤750 employees may omit ALL of E4 for the first 2 years of preparation. This is the longest size-conditional E-pillar relief.

## Penalties

Set by Member State CSRD transposition.

## Calculation Requirements

ESRS E4 metrics are heterogeneous:
- Land-use change in **hectares** by ecosystem type (links to GHG land-use-change emissions methodology — see [[concepts/land-use-change-emissions]] and the GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard)
- Sites by **count** and **area (ha)** in biodiversity-sensitive locations
- Pressures may use mixed units (mass for nutrient runoff, volume for water abstraction)
- Outcome metrics are highly methodology-dependent

The standard does not mandate a single methodological framework but explicitly references TNFD, IPBES, and the **Mean Species Abundance (MSA)** family of indicators as suitable when material.

For undertakings reporting both E1 and E4, **land-use-change CO₂** must be coordinated: the same land-conversion event drives both an E1-6 Scope 1/3 GHG metric and an E4-5 land-use-change hectare metric. Avoid double-counting in narrative but report both quantitative metrics.
