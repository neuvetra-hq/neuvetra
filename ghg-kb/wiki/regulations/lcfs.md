## Overview

The **California Low Carbon Fuel Standard (LCFS)** is a market-based regulation, codified at Title 17 California Code of Regulations sections 95480–95503 (Subarticle 7), that requires the average carbon intensity (CI, expressed in gCO2e/MJ on a full fuel-cycle / well-to-wheels basis) of the California transportation fuel pool to decline on a defined annual schedule. The Final Regulation Order at issue here was approved by the Office of Administrative Law on **2025-06-27** and took effect **2025-07-01**, replacing the prior 2018-amended LCFS regulation. (→ [[sources/california-lcfs-2025|LCFS Regulation Source]])

LCFS is administered by [[organizations/carb|CARB]] under authority granted by [[regulations/ab32|AB 32 — California Global Warming Solutions Act of 2006]] (HSC §§38500 et seq.), and is the principal California program decarbonizing the transportation fuel pool. It operates in parallel to (and is distinct from) [[regulations/carb-cap-and-trade|Cap-and-Trade]] and [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]]: where MRR collects facility-level GHG inventories and Cap-and-Trade caps emissions across covered industrial sectors, LCFS attaches a compliance obligation to **the carbon intensity of each unit of transportation fuel sold, supplied, or offered for sale in California**.

The mechanism: each year, CARB sets an average CI benchmark for gasoline (Table 1), diesel (Table 2), and fossil jet fuel (Table 3, alternative jet fuel only). Fuels delivered into California with a CI **below** the applicable benchmark generate **credits**; fuels with a CI **above** the benchmark generate **deficits**. Credits and deficits are denominated in **metric tons of CO2 equivalent (MTCO2e)** and are tracked in CARB's LCFS Reporting Tool / Credit Bank and Transfer System (**LRT-CBTS**). Each fuel reporting entity must annually retire enough credits to offset its deficits.

The 2025-07-01 amendment increased benchmark stringency by 9 percentage points step-up — gasoline benchmark dropped from 87.01 gCO2e/MJ (2024) to **76.60 gCO2e/MJ** for fuel transactions on/after 2025-07-01, declining to **9.91 gCO2e/MJ by 2045**. Diesel benchmark drops from 87.89 (2024) to 81.70 (2025 H2) and reaches 10.57 by 2045. The amendment also introduced the **Automatic Acceleration Mechanism (AAM)** — a quarterly-evaluated rule (effective May 15, 2027 onward) that can advance benchmarks by one calendar year if the credit bank exceeds three times trailing-quarter deficits AND credit generation exceeds deficit generation, preventing benchmark over-supply.

LCFS is **not** the same as a cap-and-trade allowance system — there is no aggregate cap on transportation fuel emissions, no CARB-issued allowances, and no quarterly auction. Instead, all credits are generated by private parties producing or supplying low-CI fuels; CARB only issues credits via the §95486(a)(3) Buffer Account and the §95485(c)(3) Advanced Credits mechanism (capped at 30 million credits over a six-year window for residential EV charging during compliance shortfalls).

## Who Must Comply

LCFS applies to "any transportation fuel sold, supplied, or offered for sale in California" and to any person who is a **fuel reporting entity** for that fuel (§95482(a)). Compliance obligations attach to the entity designated as the **first fuel reporting entity** for each unit of fuel; that entity is also, by default, the credit or deficit generator.

**First fuel reporting entity (§95483):**

| Fuel | First Fuel Reporting Entity |
|---|---|
| Liquid fossil (CARBOB, gasoline, ULSD diesel, fossil jet) | Producer or importer of the fuel |
| Liquid alternative (denatured/anhydrous ethanol, biodiesel/FAME, renewable diesel, alternative jet fuel) | Producer or importer of the alternative fuel component |
| Bio-CNG / Bio-LNG / Bio-L-CNG | Producer or importer of the biomethane |
| Renewable propane | Producer or importer of the renewable propane |
| Fossil CNG / LNG / L-CNG / propane | Owner of the **fueling equipment** dispensing fuel to motor vehicles |
| Hydrogen | Owner of the fueling supply equipment ("hydrogen station owner"); for fuel-cell forklifts: forklift fleet owner |
| Electricity (residential EV charging) | Electrical Distribution Utility (EDU) or its designee for base credits |
| Electricity (non-residential / commercial / fleet / public chargers) | Charging-equipment owner / EV-Service-Provider as designated under §95483(c) |

Fuel-reporting-entity status (and therefore credit/deficit generator status) can be **contractually transferred** between transferor and recipient, subject to written-contract requirements and a three-calendar-quarter window (§95483(a)(2)–(3)).

**Fuels in scope (§95482(a)):** California reformulated gasoline (CaRFG), California ULSD diesel, fossil CNG/LNG/L-CNG, bio-CNG/LNG/L-CNG, electricity, hydrogen, fuel blends >10% ethanol, biomass-based diesel blends, denatured fuel ethanol (E100), neat biomass-based diesel (B100/R100), alternative jet fuel, propane, and any other liquid or non-liquid transportation fuel.

**Opt-in fuels (§95482(b)):** Electricity, bio-CNG, bio-LNG, bio-L-CNG, alternative jet fuel, and renewable propane are presumed compliant with the schedule through 2030-12-31; their providers may **elect** to opt into LCFS as opt-in fuel reporting entities under §95483.1 to generate credits.

**Exemptions (§95482(c)–(d)):**
- Non-biomass alternative fuels supplied at <420 million MJ/year aggregate (≈3.6 million gasoline-gallon equivalent) statewide
- Fossil jet fuel (no deficits — but alternative jet fuel can opt in for credits)
- Fuels used in military tactical vehicles and tactical support equipment
- Fuels used in locomotives not subject to title 17 §93117 and in ocean-going vessels (does not exempt at-berth shore power, harbor craft, recreational/commercial harbor craft)
- Fossil propane and CNG used in school buses purchased before 2020-01-01

**Hard ineligibility (§95482(f)–(i)):**
- Palm-oil-derived transportation fuels — assigned ULSD CI for any reported volumes
- Bio-CNG/LNG/L-CNG used in CNG vehicles from projects breaking ground after 2029-12-31 — ineligible after 2040-12-31, assigned ULSD CI thereafter
- From 2030-01-01: hydrogen vehicle fuel must be ≥80% renewable hydrogen and/or hydrogen-with-CCS; non-conforming volumes assigned ULSD CI with EER = 1
- From 2035-01-01: hydrogen produced from fossil-gas feedstock ineligible for LCFS credits unless biomethane attributes match 100% of production OR 100% paired with CCS
- Soy/canola/sunflower-derived biomass-based diesel: capped at 20% of company-wide BBD volume; excess assigned the diesel benchmark CI or the certified pathway CI, whichever is higher (effective 2028-01-01 for entities with pre-2025-07-01 CA-GREET3.0 BBD pathway certifications; 2025-07-01 effective for new entrants)

## Reporting Requirements

**Two reporting cycles operate in parallel (§95491):**

**Quarterly Fuel Transactions Reports** — submitted via LRT-CBTS for each fuel type and transaction type. The data window is 90 days post-quarter (45 days upload + 45 days reconciliation with business partners):

| Reporting Quarter | Data Coverage | Quarterly Report Due |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | January–March | June 30 |
| Q2 | April–June | September 30 |
| Q3 | July–September | December 31 |
| Q4 | October–December | March 31 (following year) |

**Annual Compliance Reports** — due **April 30** of the year following the compliance period. Each entity must demonstrate it has retired credits sufficient to offset its annual compliance obligation (deficits + carryover deficits − credits − transfers − etc., per §95485(b)(2)). Mandatory credit retirement at submission: if the entity holds credits AND has deficits, LRT-CBTS automatically retires credits to fully cover the obligation, or retires every credit in the account if total credits < total deficits.

**Information required per quarterly report (§95491(c)–(d)):** Organization FEIN, reporting period, Fuel Pathway Code (FPC), fuel amount, transaction type, transaction date, business partner FEIN (if applicable), aggregated transaction indicator, fuel application (LDV/MDV vs HDV), Production Company ID and Facility ID. Liquid fuel volumes must be **temperature-corrected to 60°F** using API Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards Chapter 11, ASTM D1250-08, or specified ethanol/biodiesel formulas. CARBOB/gasoline/diesel producers must report MCON (or other crude name designation), volume, and country/state of origin for each crude supplied to each refinery during the quarter.

**Transaction types (§95491(d)(1)(A)):** Production in California, Production for Import, Import, Purchased with Obligation, Purchased without Obligation, Sold with Obligation, Sold without Obligation, Export, Loss of Inventory, Gain of Inventory, Not Used for Transportation. "Production for Import" is reported by out-of-state producers electing to be the first fuel reporting entity for fuel imported into California.

**Submission mechanics:** Reports must be submitted electronically through LRT-CBTS with a digital signature meeting California Code of Regulations title 2 §§22000 et seq. Late submission is allowed for up to three business days post-deadline subject to (1) a written correction request and (2) automatic 25% credit deduction per business day late.

**Recordkeeping (§95491.1):** Fuel reporting entities must retain records supporting all reports for the longer of 10 years or until any pending CARB enforcement is resolved. Records must be made available to CARB and to verifiers within 14 days of request.

**Verification (§§95500–95503):** Annual Fuel Pathway Reports, Quarterly Fuel Transactions Reports, Crude Oil Quarterly and Annual Volumes Reports, Project Reports, and Low-Complexity/Low-Energy-Use Refinery Reports must be verified by a CARB-accredited verification body. Verifier accreditation is granted under §95502; conflict-of-interest requirements (§95503) follow patterns analogous to those used for the [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]]. Verifier issues a Positive, Qualified Positive, or Adverse Verification Statement; an Adverse Verification Statement results in credits being ineligible for issuance pending CARB review.

## Penalties

§95494 penalty regime, anchored in HSC §38580 and Chapter 1.5 of Part 5 of HSC Division 26:

| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Each day a required report is unsubmitted, incomplete, or inaccurate | Separate violation; per-day penalty under HSC §43031 (CARB discretion) |
| Each deficit not eliminated by year-end and not validly carried over per §95485 | Separate day of violation, capped at **$1,000 per deficit** (per MTCO2e) |
| Generating credits in violation of §95491.2(b)(2)(C) (measurement accuracy) | Credit invalidation + enforcement |
| Material misstatement in any report (≥0.01 gCO2e/MJ change in CI or ≥1 whole credit/deficit change) | Modification or deletion of Certified CI; credit invalidation; deficit recalculation; up to 60 days to cure resulting deficit (§95495(b)(4)) |
| Refusing to provide records or failing to produce within required time | Basis for invalidation under §95495(b)(1)(F) |
| CCS-credit-related leakage (sequestered CO2 released or leaked) | Credit invalidation under §95495(b)(1)(G); Buffer Account retirement available where responsible party no longer exists |

CARB has broad authority under §95495 to suspend, restrict, modify, or revoke LRT-CBTS accounts; modify or delete Certified CIs; restrict, suspend, or invalidate credits; or recalculate deficits. An Initial Determination triggers a 14-day comment window for affected parties; a Final Determination follows within 30 days.

**Five-year deferral mechanism (§95485(c)):** If insufficient credits are pledged to the Clearance Market in a given year, an entity may carry an Accumulated Deficit obligation for up to five years, **with interest**, provided it retired all credits in its account at year-end. Two consecutive years in the Credit Clearance Market triggers a mandatory five-year Compliance Plan submission to CARB by August 31.

## Calculation Requirements

**Core credit/deficit equation (§95486.1(a)(1)):**

```
Credits_i^XD / Deficits_i^XD (MT) = (CI_standard^XD − CI_report^XD) × E_displaced^XD × C
```

where:
- `CI_standard^XD` = the annual benchmark CI (gCO2e/MJ) for category XD ∈ {gasoline, diesel, jet}, from Tables 1, 2, or 3 in §95484
- `CI_report^XD` = `CI_i / EER^XD`, the EER-adjusted CI of the reported fuel
- `CI_i` = the certified CI of the fuel pathway from CA-GREET3.0 (Tier 1) or a custom Tier 2 pathway, including any indirect-land-use-change (ILUC) modifier from the AEZ-EF Model where applicable
- `EER^XD` = Energy Economy Ratio (Table 5) — captures the relative drivetrain efficiency of the alternative fuel vs. its fossil reference. Examples: gasoline-replacement BEV = 3.4; diesel-replacement BEV truck/bus = 5.0; H2/FCV = 2.5 (light/medium-duty), 1.9 (heavy-duty); electric heavy rail = 4.6; electric eOGV (ocean-going vessel shore power) = 2.6
- `E_displaced^XD = E_i × EER^XD` where `E_i` is fuel energy in MJ from Table 4 energy-density factors (e.g., CARBOB 119.53 MJ/gal, ULSD 134.47 MJ/gal, electricity 3.60 MJ/kWh, hydrogen 120.00 MJ/kg, denatured ethanol 81.51 MJ/gal). Energy densities use lower heating values per CA-GREET4.0
- `C = 1.0 × 10^-6 MT/gCO2e` (unit conversion)

**Petroleum producer/importer additional incremental deficit (§95489):**

```
Deficit_Base^XD = (CI_standard^XD − CI_baseline^XD) × E^XD × C

If CI_3yr_CA_Crude_avg > CI_baseline_CA_Crude_avg + 0.10 gCO2e/MJ:
  Deficit_Incremental^XD = (CI_baseline_CA_Crude − CI_3yr_CA_Crude) × E^XD × C
Else:
  Deficit_Incremental^XD = 0
```

The California Baseline Crude Average CI is 12.61 gCO2e/MJ for crude supplied during 2024 and subsequent years. The Three-year California Crude Average CI is calculated annually as a volume-weighted average of crude CIs from Table 9 (e.g., Algerian Saharan 17.16; Angolan Cabinda 12.24; Brazilian Buzios 7.08; Canadian Albian Heavy Synthetic 24.45 — full table covers 200+ crude streams keyed by Country and Crude Identifier). Crudes not in Table 9 receive the default value pending OPGEE-modelled addition.

**Compliance balance equation (§95485(b)(2)):**

```
Compliance Obligation = Deficits_Generated + Deficits_Carryover

Credit Balance = (Credits_Generated + Credits_Acquired + Credits_Released
                  + Credits_Carryover)
               − (Credits_Retired + Credits_Sold + Credits_OnHold
                  + Credits_Clearance + Credits_Adjustment)
```

**Methodology references incorporated by reference:**
- **CA-GREET3.0** — Tier 1 pathway calculator (will reference CA-GREET4.0 for energy densities)
- **AEZ-EF Model (Dec 31, 2014)** — Agro-Ecological Zone Emissions Factor model for ILUC of crop-derived biofuels
- **OPGEE model** — Oil Production Greenhouse Gas Emissions Estimator, used to calculate crude carbon intensities in Table 9
- **API Manual of Petroleum Measurement Standards Chapter 11 (May 2004)** — temperature correction
- **ASTM D1250-08 (Reapproved 2013)** — temperature correction

**Methodological boundary vs. corporate GHG inventories:** LCFS-reported CI values are **full fuel-cycle (well-to-wheels)** for transportation fuels — they include indirect land-use-change emissions for crop-based biofuels and full upstream emissions for crude oil. This is **distinct from** corporate Scope 1 / Scope 3 Cat 11 inventory boundaries used under [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253]] and the [[sources/ghg-protocol-corporate-standard|GHG Protocol]] — a fuel reporting entity must not directly substitute LCFS CI values into a corporate GHG inventory without methodological reconciliation. LCFS credits/deficits are **regulatory compliance instruments** rather than corporate emission accounting line items.

**Relationship to Cap-and-Trade:** LCFS credits cannot be substituted for [[regulations/carb-cap-and-trade|Cap-and-Trade]] allowances and vice versa (§95487(a)(2)(A)). The two regimes are independent — a refiner subject to Cap-and-Trade for its facility combustion emissions and to LCFS for the fuels it produces faces parallel, additive compliance obligations.
