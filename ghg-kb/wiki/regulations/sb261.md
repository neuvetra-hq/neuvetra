## Overview

> **Enforcement status — Ninth Circuit injunction:** On 2025-11-18, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit issued a temporary order enjoining enforcement of SB 261 pending resolution of an appeal challenging the statute on constitutional grounds. CARB has confirmed it will not enforce SB 261 until the injunction is lifted. The CARB fee regulation (Title 17 CCR §§96070–96077, adopted 2026-02-26) continues to apply and fees remain due — only enforcement of the substantive disclosure requirements is stayed. Wiki content below reflects the statute as written; compliance obligations are subject to the injunction's resolution. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-isor|ISOR fn. 2]]; [[sources/carb-sb253-261-notice|Notice fn. 1]])

California SB 261 was signed into law on 2023-10-07 as Chapter 383. It adds Health and Safety Code Section 38533, requiring large businesses doing business in California to prepare and publicly disclose a **climate-related financial risk report** biennially.

Unlike SB 253 — which mandates a full GHG emissions inventory (Scope 1, 2, and 3) — SB 261 requires disclosure of the *financial risks* arising from climate change: physical risks (wildfires, floods, extreme weather, sea level rise) and transition risks (policy, legal, technology, and market changes), plus the measures the company is taking to reduce and adapt to those risks. The required disclosure framework is the TCFD (Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures) or a recognized equivalent.

Together, SB 253 and SB 261 form California's two-track corporate climate disclosure regime: emissions data (SB 253) + financial risk narrative (SB 261). (→ [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253 — CCDAA]])

(→ [[sources/sb261-bill-text|SB 261 Bill Text Source]])

## Who Must Comply

**Covered entity** means any corporation, partnership, LLC, or other business entity that:
- Is formed under the laws of any U.S. state or under an act of Congress, AND
- Has **total annual revenues exceeding $500,000,000** (determined by prior fiscal year), AND
- **Does business in California**

There is no requirement to be headquartered or incorporated in California.

**Excluded:** Entities regulated by the California Department of Insurance are carved out entirely.

**Subsidiary definition (codified — Title 17 CCR §96072):** A subsidiary is a business in which another company (the parent or holding company) owns more than 50% of its voting stock. CARB identifies subsidiaries through commercial databases cross-referenced with CA Secretary of State and Franchise Tax Board data, with a self-reporting option for parent-subsidiary relationships.

**Subsidiary consolidation:** If a subsidiary qualifies as a covered entity but its parent also qualifies, the parent may file a consolidated report covering the subsidiary. Subsidiaries need not file separately in that case. (→ [[regulations/sb219|SB 219]])

**Exemptions (codified — Title 17 CCR §96071, adopted 2026-02-26):**
- Non-profit or charitable organizations that are tax-exempt under the Internal Revenue Code
- Entities regulated by the California Department of Insurance, or in the insurance business in any other state
- Federal, state, and local government entities, and companies majority-owned (>50%) by government entities
- Entities whose **only** California activity consists of wholesale electricity transactions
- Entities whose **only** California presence is teleworking employees

(→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation §96071]])

**Preliminary covered entity estimate:** CARB staff analysis (Dunn & Bradstreet + CA Secretary of State data) identified approximately 4,160 entities subject to SB 261 and 2,596 entities subject to SB 253. These are preliminary figures pending validation. (→ [[sources/carb-workshop-aug-2025|CARB Workshop Aug 2025]])

## Reporting Requirements

Covered entities must prepare a **climate-related financial risk report** disclosing:

1. **Climate-related financial risk** — material risk of harm to immediate and long-term financial outcomes due to physical and transition risks, including risks to: corporate operations, provision of goods and services, supply chains, employee health and safety, capital and financial investments, institutional investments, financial standing of loan recipients, shareholder value, consumer demand, and financial markets.

2. **Measures adopted** to reduce and adapt to the disclosed climate-related financial risks.

**Mandated framework:** Reports must follow one of:
- **TCFD Final Report of Recommendations (June 2017)** or any successor
- **IFRS S2** (ISSB Sustainability Disclosure Standards)
- Any law, regulation, or exchange listing requirement from a regulated exchange, national government, or other governmental entity, including US government regulations

Each report must include a **framework statement**: (a) which framework is being applied, and (b) which recommendations/disclosures have been compiled and which have not, with a short explanation of omissions and any plans for future disclosure.

If a covered entity cannot complete all required disclosures, it must provide disclosures to the best of its ability and explain gaps in detail.

**Third-party verification:** If the report includes GHG emissions claims or claims about voluntary GHG mitigation, CARB may consider those claims only if verified by a third-party independent verifier.

**Third-party reports:** Existing TCFD, IFRS S2, or equivalent reports prepared for other purposes can satisfy SB 261 if they meet HSC § 38533 requirements. The entity must post the report on its own website and register the public link in CARB's docket.

**Publication method:** The covered entity makes the report publicly available on its own website. CARB opened a public docket on 2025-12-01 for entities to submit links to their reports (not the reports themselves); the docket closes 2026-07-01. This central registry of links is how CARB tracks compliance, but the reports themselves remain hosted on company websites.

**Calendar vs. fiscal year:** The statute does not specify. For the first report, covered entities should use the most recent/best available data. (→ [[sources/carb-sb261-checklist|CARB Checklist Sep 2025]])

**TCFD four disclosure pillars — minimum CARB requirements:** CARB's September 2025 checklist specifies the following minimum content for each pillar. (→ [[sources/carb-sb261-checklist|CARB Checklist Sep 2025]])

1. **Governance** — Describe the organization's governance structure, if any, for identifying, assessing, and managing climate-related financial risks. Must include: board oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities (where the entity has a Board); description of management oversight.

2. **Strategy** — Describe actual and potential impacts of climate-related risks and opportunities on the company's operations, strategy, and financial planning, where material. Must include: risks and opportunities across short, medium, and long-term horizons; impact on operations, strategy, and financial planning; resilience of the organization's strategy under various climate scenarios. Qualitative scenario analysis is acceptable and encouraged where feasible — quantitative scenario analysis is not required.

3. **Risk Management** — Describe how the reporting entity identifies, assesses, and manages climate-related risks, and how those processes integrate into the organization's overall risk management.

4. **Metrics and Targets** — Disclose the metrics and targets used to assess and manage relevant climate-related risks and opportunities where material.

> **Note — Scope 1/2/3 emissions NOT required for initial SB 261 report:** CARB explicitly excluded emissions data from the minimum Metrics and Targets requirement for the initial reporting period. CARB's stated rationale: emissions data collection may not be feasible by the January 1, 2026 deadline and may duplicate SB 253 requirements. Covered entities following the full TCFD or IFRS S2 framework may voluntarily include emissions data; any GHG claims must be third-party verified before CARB will consider them. (→ [[sources/carb-sb261-checklist|CARB Checklist Sep 2025]])

## Deadlines

| Date | Obligation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **2026-01-01** | First climate-related financial risk report publicly available on company website | Biennial obligation — **enforcement suspended under Ninth Circuit injunction as of 2025-11-18** |
| **2026-01-01 (annually)** | Annual fee paid to CARB for administration and implementation | Annual, not biennial; fee regulation adopted Feb 2026 |
| **2028-01-01** | Second report due | Biennial cadence continues — subject to injunction resolution |

The reporting obligation is **biennial** (every two years). The administration fee is **annual**.

> **Note:** As of 2025-11-18, CARB is not enforcing the January 1, 2026 first-report deadline due to the Ninth Circuit temporary injunction. Approximately 120+ covered entities voluntarily submitted climate risk reports ahead of the January 1, 2026 deadline, per CARB's February 2026 count. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-press-release|Press Release]])

**Annual implementation fee (adopted):** Flat-rate fee per entity, calculated annually as Total Required Revenue ÷ number of covered entities. CPI-adjusted from FY 2027-28 onward. Fee notices issued by September 10 each year; payment due within 60 days. Pre-adoption estimates (~$1,403/year) were workshop projections; exact amounts set annually by CARB. Entities also subject to SB 253 receive a combined invoice. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation §§96073–96074]])

## Penalties

| Violation | Maximum Penalty |
|---|---|
| Failure to make report publicly available on website | **$50,000** per reporting year |
| Publishing an inadequate or insufficient report | **$50,000** per reporting year |

Penalties are imposed by CARB through administrative hearings under Title 17 CCR §60065.1–§60075.1. CARB must first adopt implementing regulations authorizing these penalties before enforcement begins.

*Note: Health and Safety Code §38580 (standard GHG enforcement provision) does not apply to SB 261 violations — the statute explicitly creates a standalone enforcement mechanism.*

Mitigating factors CARB must consider: (a) the entity's past and present compliance record, and (b) whether good faith measures to comply were taken and when.

## Calculation Requirements

SB 261 does not mandate a GHG emissions calculation. However, TCFD recommended disclosures include climate-related metrics and targets (e.g., Scope 1/2/3 emissions), so covered entities following the full TCFD framework in practice will often include emissions data. Any GHG claims included in the report must be third-party verified before CARB will consider them.
