## Overview

ESRS E3 (Water and Marine Resources) is the topical environmental standard governing water and marine-resource disclosures under the CSRD. It covers freshwater (withdrawal, consumption, discharge) and marine resources (extraction, use, impact on marine ecosystems). Water-quality discharge is split between this standard (where the matter relates to volumetric water resource pressure) and ESRS E2 (where the matter is pollutant content).

E3 has the lightest disclosure load of the environmental standards (only 5 DRs), reflecting that water is highly site-specific and that not all undertakings have material water dependencies or impacts. Water-stress location is often the deciding factor in materiality.

(→ [[sources/esrs-set1-2023-2772|ESRS Set 1 — Commission Delegated Regulation 2023/2772]])

## Who Must Comply

CSRD reporters for whom **water and marine resources are material** under the [[concepts/double-materiality|Double Materiality]] assessment. High-typical-materiality sectors: agriculture & food production, beverage manufacturing, textiles, semiconductor manufacturing, mining, pulp & paper, electric power generation (cooling water), and any operations sited in water-stressed areas.

For undertakings with no operations or value-chain dependence in water-stressed regions and no material discharge, water may legitimately be assessed as non-material; in that case all E3 disclosures may be omitted with a brief explanation.

## Reporting Requirements

### IRO-1 (E3-specific) — Process to identify material water and marine resources-related IROs
How the undertaking screens for material water IROs, with explicit reference to **areas at water risk, including areas of high water stress**. The site-by-site mapping (typically using WRI Aqueduct or WBCSD India Water Tool equivalents) is the typical methodological backbone.

### E3-1 — Policies related to water and marine resources
Policies in the structure of ESRS 2 MDR-P. Covers water use, treatment and discharge, marine ecosystems, alignment with frameworks such as the UN Sustainable Development Goal 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) where applicable.

### E3-2 — Actions and resources related to water and marine resources
Action plans in the structure of ESRS 2 MDR-A. Includes water-efficiency measures, recycling/reuse projects, watershed restoration, marine biodiversity protection.

### E3-3 — Targets related to water and marine resources
Targets in the structure of ESRS 2 MDR-T. Common target structures include water consumption reduction (absolute or intensity) in water-stressed areas, and watershed-level commitments (e.g., AWS certification, CEO Water Mandate).

### E3-4 — Water consumption
**Quantitative metrics:**
- **Total water consumption** (in m³) — water withdrawn minus water discharged
- **Total water consumption in areas at water risk, including areas of high water stress** (in m³) — disaggregated
- **Total water recycled and reused** (in m³)
- Total water stored (where material) and changes in water storage
- **Contextual information**: methodology used (e.g., WRI Aqueduct), water-stress definition source

Reported under MDR-M structure (methodology, data sources, comparability).

**Note on terminology:** *withdrawal* = water taken from any source; *consumption* = withdrawal minus discharge (the portion not returned to the source/watershed); *discharge* = water returned (with quality considerations covered under E2-4 if pollutant concentrations are material).

### E3-5 — Anticipated financial effects from material water and marine resources-related impacts, risks and opportunities
- Material risks (water-scarcity exposure, regulatory restrictions on withdrawal, drought-induced supply disruption)
- Material opportunities (water-efficient products, blue economy markets)
- **Subject to phase-in:** first-year omission permitted; first 3 years qualitative-only reporting permitted
- See [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in]]

No standalone deadline. CSRD phased rollout applies — see [[regulations/csrd|CSRD]].

Set by Member State CSRD transposition.

## Calculation Requirements

E3 disclosures are in **volumetric units** — cubic meters (m³) for water consumption, withdrawal, discharge, recycling. No GHG conversion applies.

**Water-stress assessment** is the methodological backbone. ESRS does not mandate a specific tool, but the WRI Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas, WWF Water Risk Filter, and WBCSD India Water Tool are the conventional choices. The chosen tool and definition of "high water stress" must be disclosed under E3-4 contextual information.

For multi-site undertakings, water consumption must be disaggregated by:
- Operations in areas of high water stress vs. operations not in stressed areas
- Significant geographic regions where material variation exists (consistent with ESRS 1 Chapter 3.7 disaggregation rules)
