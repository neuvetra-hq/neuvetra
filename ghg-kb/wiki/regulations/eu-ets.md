## Overview

The EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) is the world's first international emissions trading system, established by Directive 2003/87/EC and operational since 2005. It operates as a **cap-and-trade** mechanism: the EU sets a declining annual cap on total GHG emissions from covered sectors; operators must surrender one EU Allowance (EUA) for each tonne of CO₂ equivalent emitted in the prior year.

The EU ETS is now in **Phase 4 (2021–2030)**, with significantly accelerated cap reductions and broader sectoral coverage following **Directive (EU) 2023/959** (the "Fit for 55" ETS revision, OJ L 130, 16.5.2023; → [[sources/eu-directive-ets-revision-2023-959|Directive 2023/959 — Source File]]). The 2030 target is a **62% reduction in covered-sector emissions relative to 2005 levels** (Article 9 as amended).

The system now has two parallel trading regimes:

- **ETS1** — the original system covering stationary installations (Annex I activities), aviation, and from 2024 maritime transport. Free allocation for industrial sectors at risk of carbon leakage is being phased out as the **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** phases in (2026–2034).
- **ETS2** — a separate, parallel cap-and-trade system established by Directive 2023/959 (new Chapter IVa) covering the **release for consumption of fuels used for combustion in buildings, road transport, and additional sectors** not covered by ETS1. Compliance falls on **fuel suppliers (regulated entities)**, not end-users. Permits required from 2025; trading and surrender obligations from 2027 (with a possible one-year delay to 2028 under defined energy-price triggers).

**Relationship to CSRD/ESRS E1:** Companies subject to EU ETS must disclose the **percentage of their Scope 1 emissions covered by EU ETS allowances** under ESRS E1-6 (→ [[regulations/esrs-e1|ESRS E1]]). The two regimes are complementary: EU ETS is a compliance/payment obligation on GHG-intensive facilities and fuel suppliers; CSRD/ESRS is a disclosure obligation on large companies.

## Who Must Comply

### ETS1 — Stationary Installations, Aviation, Maritime

Coverage is mandatory for all qualifying operators.

#### Combustion Installations
- Rated thermal input exceeding **20 MW**
- Excludes hazardous and municipal waste incineration facilities (Commission to assess inclusion of municipal waste incineration from 2028 — Directive 2023/959 recital 98)
- New from Directive 2023/959: an installation that meets the capacity threshold for an Annex I activity is in scope **even if it emits no GHGs** (recital 10) — ensures a level playing field as installations decarbonise

#### Industry (Annex I, Directive 2003/87/EC, as amended by Directive 2023/959)

| Sector | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Mineral oil refineries | All |
| Coke ovens | All |
| Metal ore roasting/sintering | All |
| Pig iron or steel production | Capacity >2.5 tonnes/hour |
| Cement clinker (rotary kilns) | >500 tonnes/day |
| Lime production | >50 tonnes/day |
| Glass manufacturing | >20 tonnes/day melting capacity |
| Ceramic firing | >75 tonnes/day or kiln capacity >4 m³ |
| Pulp production | All |
| Paper and board manufacturing | >20 tonnes/day |
| Primary aluminium or alumina production | All (revised wording, Directive 2023/959) |
| Nitric/adipic/glyoxylic acid production | All |
| Bulk organic chemical production | All |
| Hydrogen (H₂) and synthesis-gas production | **>5 tonnes/day** (lowered from prior threshold by Directive 2023/959) |
| Carbon black production | >50 tonnes/day |
| Drying or calcination of gypsum / plasterboard | >20 tonnes/day |
| Transport of GHGs for geological storage | All transport modes — pipeline, ship, truck (extended by Directive 2023/959) |
| **Maritime transport (new)** | Ships ≥ 5,000 GT carrying cargo or passengers, per Regulation (EU) 2015/757; CO₂ from 2024, CH₄ + N₂O from 2026 |

#### Aviation
Flights within the European Economic Area (EEA), plus departing flights to Switzerland and the UK. The detailed aviation-specific revisions (free-allocation phase-out for aircraft operators, CORSIA integration, sustainable aviation fuel allowances) are governed by sister Directive **(EU) 2023/958**, not by Directive 2023/959 — a future ingest will create dedicated coverage.

#### Maritime (from 2024)

Per new Articles 3ga–3gg inserted by Directive 2023/959:

| Voyage segment | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Within an EU port | 100% |
| Between two EU ports | 100% |
| EU port ↔ non-EU port | 50% |

**Phase-in of surrender obligations (Article 3gb):**

| Reporting year | Surrender obligation |
|---|---|
| 2024 verified emissions | 40% |
| 2025 verified emissions | 70% |
| 2026 onward | 100% |

The shortfall in 2024 and 2025 is **cancelled** rather than auctioned. **Methane and nitrous oxide** become covered ETS gases for maritime from **1 January 2026**. **Offshore ships** included from **2027**.

The **shipping company** (the ISM Code "company" — usually the ship's owner or its appointed manager) is the regulated entity. Article 3gc requires Member States to ensure cost pass-through where the shipping company is contractually distinct from the entity that procures fuel or operates the ship (typically a charterer).

**Anti-evasion: container transhipment ports.** The Commission publishes a list of "neighbouring container transhipment ports" — non-EU ports with >65% transhipment share located within 300 nautical miles of an EU port. Calls at those ports are treated as EU port calls, preventing routing around the system.

#### Geographic Scope
All EU Member States + Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway (EEA). Northern Ireland for electricity generation.

#### Small Emitter Exemptions
Certain small installations may be excluded where Member States implement alternative regulatory measures of equivalent effect. **New from Directive 2023/959:** installations that combust **>95% zero-rated biomass** are excluded from ETS scope (recital 70) — the threshold balances regulatory burden against the need to keep MRV oversight on installations that retain fossil-fuel capacity.

### ETS2 — Buildings, Road Transport, and Additional Sectors

Established by **Chapter IVa (Articles 30a–30k)**, inserted by Directive 2023/959.

#### Activity Scope (Annex III, new)

ETS2 covers the **release for consumption of fuels used for combustion** in:

| IPCC source category | Sector |
|---|---|
| 1A1a ii / 1A1a iii | CHP / Heat plants supplying buildings or additional sectors |
| 1A3b (excluding agricultural vehicles on paved roads) | Road Transportation |
| 1A4a | Commercial / Institutional |
| 1A4b | Residential |
| 1A1 (excl. CHP/heat for buildings) | Energy Industries — **additional sectors** outside ETS1 |
| 1A2 | Manufacturing Industries and Construction — **additional sectors** outside ETS1 |

Excluded from ETS2: fuels used in ETS1 Annex I activities (avoiding double coverage); fuels with a zero emission factor; hazardous and municipal waste used as fuel.

#### Regulated Entity (Article 30b, recital 78)

The compliance obligation falls on **regulated entities**, defined by reference to **Council Directive (EU) 2020/262** (excise duty) — typically fuel distributors / wholesalers placing fuel onto the market. **Final consumers (households, drivers, building occupants) bear no direct ETS2 obligation.** They bear the carbon cost only via fuel prices.

A **GHG emissions permit** is required from 1 January 2025 even though trading does not begin until 2027.

## Reporting Requirements

### ETS1 — GHG Permit
Every covered installation must hold a **greenhouse gas emissions permit** issued by the national competent authority, which includes approval of a monitoring plan. Maritime shipping companies operate under an analogous regime via the administering authority assigned to them.

### ETS1 — Annual Verified Emissions Report
Operators must submit a verified emissions report covering:
- Total verified GHG emissions for the prior calendar year
- Activity data, emission factors, oxidation factors
- Uncertainty assessments
- Methodology used (calculation-based or measurement-based)

### ETS2 — Permit, Monitoring, and Annual Report (Article 30b, Article 30f, Annex IV Part C, Annex V Part C — all new)

Regulated entities must:
- Hold a GHG emissions permit from 1 January 2025
- Report **historical 2024 emissions** by 30 April 2025 (basis for the 2028+ cap rebasing)
- Submit a verified annual emissions report each year thereafter
- From 2027, hold and surrender allowances equal to the verified emissions from fuels released for consumption

Calculation method (Annex IV Part C, new): `Emissions = Fuel released for consumption × Emission factor`. Default IPCC 2006 factors are used unless fuel-specific factors from accredited laboratories are more accurate. Reporting and verification structurally mirror ETS1 but adapted to fuel-supplier reporting (see also [[sources/eu-ets-mrr-2018-2066|MRR]] and [[sources/eu-ets-avr-2018-2067|AVR]] amendments for ETS2-specific monitoring and verification rules).

### Monitoring Methodologies (ETS1)

All ETS1 monitoring is governed by **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/2066** (the "MRR" — Monitoring and Reporting Regulation), consolidated 27 May 2025 (→ [[sources/eu-ets-mrr-2018-2066|EU ETS MRR — Source File]]). The MRR mandates a tier-based methodology system anchored to a legally binding **monitoring plan** approved by the national competent authority for each installation/operator.

**Calculation-based (standard, Articles 24, 26–35):**
- `Emissions = Activity Data × Emission Factor × Oxidation Factor`
- Activity data from supply records or direct measurement
- Emission factors: activity-specific or approved defaults from MRR Annex III Table 1
- Biomass is zero-rated **only if** it meets sustainability + GHG savings criteria of RED II Article 29; otherwise its carbon counts (Article 38(5)). Directive 2023/959 codifies this rule at the directive level by amending Annex IV (Part A and Part B) to remove the unconditional "emission factor for biomass shall be zero" language.

**Calculation-based (mass balance, Article 25):**
- Used for complex chemical processes: `Emissions = (Carbon in inputs − Carbon in outputs − Carbon stored) × 3.664`
- Applies to refineries, integrated steel, bulk organics where standard methodology is impractical

**Measurement-based (CEMS, Articles 41–46):**
- Continuous Emissions Monitoring Systems
- Must be corroborated by supporting calculations
- Guidance in MRR/AVR Guidance Document No. 7

**Aviation small emitters:** Eurocontrol Small Emitter Tool (SET)

**Non-CO₂ aviation effects:** Mandatory monitoring from 1 January 2025 for routes within EEA and EEA→Switzerland/UK; voluntary for other routes 2025–2026 (MRR Annexes IIIa and IIIb).

### Verification (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/2067 — AVR)

The AVR (→ [[sources/eu-ets-avr-2018-2067|EU ETS AVR — Source File]]) governs how every ETS report is verified and who is qualified to verify it. Each annual emissions report must be verified by an **accredited verifier** before submission. Verification must conclude with **reasonable assurance** (a high but not absolute level of assurance) that the report is free from material misstatement.

**Materiality thresholds (AVR Article 23):**

| Report type | Materiality level |
|---|---|
| Category A / B installation emissions | 5% of total reported emissions |
| Category C installation emissions | 2% of total reported emissions |
| Aircraft operator ≤ 500 kt fossil CO₂/yr | 5% |
| Aircraft operator > 500 kt fossil CO₂/yr | 2% |
| Non-CO₂ aviation effects report | 5% of aggregated CO₂(e) reported |
| Baseline / new-entrant / annual activity level reports | 5% of the relevant data point |
| ETS2 regulated entity (fuel supplier) | 5% of reported emissions (2% above threshold size) |

**Verifier accreditation:** Verifiers must be accredited by a national accreditation body of a Member State under Regulation (EC) 765/2008 and the AVR. Accreditation grants mutual recognition across the EU. Verification activity scopes are defined by AVR Annex I.

### Tier System (MRR Article 26, Annexes II and V — ETS1 only)

The required minimum tier for each parameter (activity data, NCV, emission factor, oxidation/conversion factor, carbon content, biomass fraction) depends on **installation category** and **source-stream classification**:

| Installation category | Annual fossil CO₂ | Whole-installation uncertainty cap |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | ≤ 50,000 t | ≤ 7.5% |
| **B** | 50,001–500,000 t | ≤ 5.0% |
| **C** | > 500,000 t | ≤ 2.5% |

| Source-stream class | Tier flexibility |
|---|---|
| Major | Highest tier in Annex II; up to 2 levels lower for Cat A/B with technical-feasibility justification |
| Minor (≤ 5,000 t/yr or ≤ 10% of total, capped at 100,000 t) | Lower tier permitted with justification |
| De minimis (≤ 1,000 t/yr or ≤ 2% of total, capped at 20,000 t) | Conservative no-tier estimates allowed (incl. energy balance method) |

### Zero-Rating of Low-Carbon Fuels (MRR Article 3(23c), 38(5), 39a; codified in amended Annex IV by Directive 2023/959)

Carbon in fuels and materials is treated as emitted **unless zero-rated**. Zero-rating applies to:
- **Biomass** — meets RED II sustainability + GHG savings criteria (Article 29(2)–(7),(10) of Directive (EU) 2018/2001)
- **RFNBO** (renewable fuels of non-biological origin) and **RCF** (recycled carbon fuels) — meet RED II Article 29a GHG savings criteria
- **Synthetic low-carbon fuels** — ≥ 70% GHG reduction vs fossil comparator AND prior surrender of allowances for captured carbon used in production (Directive (EU) 2024/1788)

### Gases Covered

| Gas | Sector |
|---|---|
| CO₂ | Energy, industry, aviation, maritime (from 2024), ETS2 (from 2027) |
| CH₄ | Maritime (from 2026) |
| N₂O | Nitric, adipic, and glyoxylic acid production; maritime (from 2026) |
| PFCs | Aluminium production |

## Deadlines

### ETS1 — Annual MRV Cycle

| Obligation | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Submit verified annual emissions report to competent authority | **31 March** (following calendar year) |
| For maritime: emissions marked as verified in Union Registry | **1 April** |
| Free-allocation grant by competent authorities to operators | **30 June** (moved from 28 February by Directive 2023/959, recital 66) |
| Surrender allowances (EUAs) equal to prior-year verified emissions | **30 September** (moved from 30 April by Directive 2023/959, recital 66) |

The annual cycle: monitor throughout the year → verify by March 31 → free-allocation grant by June 30 → surrender by September 30.

### ETS2 — Phase-in Schedule

| Date | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **30 June 2024** | Member State transposition deadline for Chapter IVa (ETS2) |
| **1 January 2025** | Regulated entities (fuel suppliers) must hold a GHG emissions permit |
| **30 April 2025** | First historical-emissions report (covers 2024) |
| **1 January 2027** | ETS2 trading begins; first allowances issued |
| **31 May 2028** | First ETS2 surrender obligation (covering 2027 verified emissions) |
| **(Optional) 2028 instead of 2027** | One-year delay possible if Article 30k gas/oil price triggers are met |

ETS2 surrender deadlines are **deliberately set one month after** the equivalent ETS1 deadlines to mitigate double-counting issues at the boundary.

### Maritime Phase-in (within ETS1)

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **1 January 2024** | Maritime emissions monitoring begins under amended Regulation (EU) 2015/757 |
| **31 March 2025** | First verified maritime emissions report due (covering 2024) |
| **30 September 2025** | First maritime allowance surrender — 40% of 2024 verified emissions |
| **30 September 2026** | 70% of 2025 verified emissions surrendered |
| **1 January 2026** | CH₄ and N₂O become covered gases for maritime |
| **30 September 2027** | 100% of 2026 verified emissions surrendered (full compliance) |
| **1 January 2027** | Offshore ships included |

## Penalties

- **€100 per tonne** of excess emissions (failure to surrender sufficient allowances)
- Adjusted annually by EU inflation index (EICP/HICP)
- Paying the penalty **does NOT** discharge the surrender obligation — the shortfall must be made up in the following compliance period
- Names of non-compliant operators are **publicly disclosed**
- Unverified annual reports block all allowance transfers in the Union Registry until verification is completed
- Penalty regime applies to ETS2 regulated entities via Article 30e cross-reference to Article 16

## Calculation Requirements

### ETS1 — Cap and Allowance Allocation

**Phase 4 cap trajectory (Article 9 as amended by Directive 2023/959, Article 1(11)):**

| Period | Linear Reduction Factor |
|---|---|
| 2021–2023 | 2.2% per year |
| 2024–2027 | **4.3% per year** (accelerated by Directive 2023/959) |
| 2028 onward | **4.4% per year** |

**Additional Phase 4 rebasing (one-time cap adjustments, Directive 2023/959):**
- **−90 million allowances** in 2024
- **+78.4 million allowances** in 2024 to admit maritime transport
- **−27 million allowances** in 2026
- Additional small upward adjustments in 2026 (to admit CH₄/N₂O from maritime) and 2027 (offshore ships) — those allowances flow to the Innovation Fund

**2024 total cap:** 1,386,051,745 allowances (Commission publication of 6 September 2023 per Article 9 as amended)

**2030 target:** 62% reduction in ETS-covered emissions vs 2005.

**Allocation methods:**
- Primarily **auctioning** by Member States
- **Free allocation** retained for industrial sectors at risk of carbon leakage, but being phased down for CBAM-covered sectors (see CBAM Phase-Out below)
- **From 2026, free allocation is conditional** on:
  - Implementation of energy-efficiency / emissions-reduction techniques (recital 45) — applies in particular to large energy users
  - For the **20% installations with the highest emission intensity** under any product benchmark: a mandatory **climate-neutrality plan**
- Annual benchmark adjustment range increased from **0.2%–1.6% to 0.3%–2.5%** (recital 48)

### CBAM-Linked Free-Allocation Phase-Out

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, Regulation (EU) 2023/956) replaces free allocation as the carbon-leakage prevention mechanism for covered sectors. A **CBAM factor** progressively reduces free allowances for those sectors:

| Year | CBAM factor (% of free allocation that remains) |
|---|---|
| 2025 and earlier | 100% |
| 2026 | 97.5% |
| 2027 | 95% |
| 2028 | 90% |
| 2029 | 77.5% |
| 2030 | 51.5% |
| 2031 | 39% |
| 2032 | 26.5% |
| 2033 | 14% |
| 2034 onward | 0% (no free allocation for CBAM sectors) |

Free allowances no longer issued to CBAM sectors are transferred to the **Innovation Fund**. (→ [[sources/eu-directive-ets-revision-2023-959|Directive 2023/959]] recital 46)

### ETS2 — Cap and Auctioning

**Cap trajectory (Article 30c, new):**
- **2027 cap** derived from Effort Sharing Regulation 2024 limits (Article 4(2) of Regulation (EU) 2018/842), applying an **LRF of 5.10%** per year from a 2024 reference value
- **From 2028** the cap is rebased on the average of 2024–2026 verified emissions and declines at **LRF 5.38%** per year
- If 2024–2026 emissions overshoot the 2025 trajectory by >2%, the LRF is adjusted upward per Annex IIIa formula
- **Sectoral contribution targets:** 43% reduction by 2030 vs 2005 for buildings + road transport; 42% for additional sectors

**Auctioning (Article 30d, new):**
- **All allowances auctioned — no free allocation** (recital 81)
- 2027 auction volumes are **130% of the 2027 cap** (front-loading), with the additional 30% deducted from 2029–2031 auctions
- 600 million ETS2 allowances seeded into the Market Stability Reserve in 2027
- 150 million allowances + auction revenue (up to **€65 billion total** through 2032) channelled to the **Social Climate Fund** (Regulation (EU) 2023/955)

**Price-stability safeguards (ETS2-specific):**
- **Article 30h (€45 ceiling trigger):** automatic MSR releases if average allowance price exceeds €45 in initial years
- **Article 30k (energy-price delay):** automatic one-year delay of cap and surrender obligations if defined gas/oil wholesale-price thresholds are breached in the year before trading would begin

### Market Stability Reserve (MSR — amended by Article 2 of Directive 2023/959)

The MSR adjusts allowance supply to absorb cumulative surpluses. Directive 2023/959 amends Decision (EU) 2015/1814:
- **Aviation allowances and emissions are now included in the Total Number of Allowances in Circulation (TNAC)** calculation
- A **new automatic price-spike safeguard** releases allowances from the MSR when the average price meets defined thresholds — distinct from but parallel to the ETS2-specific Article 30h trigger
- Provisions extended to cover ETS2 (separate ETS2 MSR holding, Article 1a(3) as amended)

### Organizational Boundary

EU ETS applies at the **facility/installation level (ETS1)** or **regulated-entity level (ETS2 fuel suppliers)** using the **operational control approach** (→ [[methodologies/operational-control-approach|Operational Control Approach]]). The operator of the installation — whoever holds the GHG permit — is responsible for ETS1, regardless of financial ownership structure. For ETS2, the regulated entity is whoever places the fuel onto the market for consumption.

### Union Registry

All allowances, verified reports, and surrender records are maintained in the EU-wide Union Registry, the central ledger of the EU ETS. ETS2 allowances are tracked separately from ETS1 allowances (Article 30d(1)).

### Funds Receiving ETS Auction Revenue

Directive 2023/959 expanded the funds financed by ETS revenue:

- **Innovation Fund** (Article 10a(8)) — scope expanded to support buildings/road-transport decarbonisation, maritime decarbonisation (alternative fuels, electrification, wind propulsion, ice-class), aviation electrification, CCDs/CDs/fixed-premium contracts. +20 M allowances from free-allocation pool, +5 M from auctioning pool.
- **Modernisation Fund** (Article 10d) — additional **2.5% of Union-wide cap (2024–2030)** allocated to lower-income Member States (GDP/capita <75% of EU average). Fossil-fuel investments largely ineligible going forward.
- **Social Climate Fund** (Regulation (EU) 2023/955, parallel) — 50 M ETS1 allowances + 150 M ETS2 allowances + ETS2 auction revenue up to **€65 billion** through 2032. Targeted at vulnerable households and transport users.
