## Overview

**AB 32 — the California Global Warming Solutions Act of 2006** (Stats. 2006, Ch. 488) is the foundational enabling statute for California's greenhouse gas regulatory framework. Signed into law in September 2006 and codified at Health and Safety Code (HSC) Division 25.5, sections 38500–38599, it established California as the first U.S. state to adopt a comprehensive, economy-wide GHG emissions cap.

AB 32's primary mandate: California must reduce its GHG emissions to **1990 levels by 2020**, representing approximately a 15% reduction from business-as-usual projections at the time of enactment.

All of California's major GHG compliance programs flow from or are complementary to AB 32:
- The **Mandatory Reporting Regulation (MRR)** — mandated by HSC §38530 (→ [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]])
- The **Cap-and-Trade Regulation** — authorized by HSC §38570 (→ [[regulations/carb-cap-and-trade|Cap-and-Trade]])
- The **Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act (SB 253)** — enacted separately but builds on AB 32 infrastructure and CARB authority (→ [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253]])

AB 32 grants CARB the authority to monitor, regulate, and enforce GHG emission reductions across California's economy, including authority to adopt market-based compliance mechanisms such as cap-and-trade.

(→ [[sources/ab32-bill-text|AB 32 — Bill Text and Fact Sheet]])

## Who Must Comply

AB 32 creates two tiers of obligation:

**CARB (mandatory agency obligations):**
- Determine the 1990 statewide GHG emissions level (by 2008-01-01)
- Adopt mandatory GHG reporting regulations (by 2008-01-01)
- Develop and publish a Scoping Plan (by 2009-01-01; updated every 5 years)
- Adopt GHG emission limits and regulations operative by 2012-01-01
- Maintain reductions beyond the 2020 target

**Regulated entities (indirect — through implementing regulations):**
AB 32 does not directly impose compliance obligations on most businesses — it directs CARB to adopt regulations that do. The implementing regulations (MRR, Cap-and-Trade) determine which facilities and entities must comply.

AB 32 does create one direct business obligation:
- A **cost-of-implementation fee** (HSC §38597) on approximately 250 major GHG sources (refineries, power plants, industrial facilities). Revenues are deposited into the **Air Pollution Control Fund** to fund CARB's AB 32 implementation work. This is separate from cap-and-trade auction proceeds (which flow to the Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund).

## Reporting Requirements

AB 32 (HSC §38530) mandates CARB to:
- Monitor and report annually from the **largest emission sources first**
- Account for **all electricity consumed in California**, including imported power with transmission losses
- Maintain comprehensive emission records

The implementing regulation that fulfills this mandate is the CARB MRR (Title 17 CCR §95100–95163). All facility-level GHG reporting obligations for California businesses flow through MRR, not directly from AB 32 statute. (→ [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]])

## Penalties

**Enforcement authority** (HSC §38580): CARB monitors and enforces all rules and regulations adopted under AB 32.

Violations are "deemed to result in an emission of an air contaminant" for penalty purposes under pre-existing California air quality penalty law. Specific penalty dollar amounts are set by regulation and the Health and Safety Code air quality provisions, not AB 32 directly.

CARB may:
- Convert violations into "days in violation" calculations
- Seek injunctive relief under HSC §41513
- Issue administrative orders

## Calculation Requirements

AB 32 does not specify calculation methodologies — it delegates this to CARB regulation. Key methodological instruments adopted under AB 32 authority:

- **MRR** adopts 40 CFR Part 98 (US EPA methods) with California modifications for facility-level calculation (→ [[regulations/carb-mrr|CARB MRR]])
- **Cap-and-Trade** defines "covered emissions" and quantification methodologies for compliance instruments (→ [[regulations/carb-cap-and-trade|Cap-and-Trade]])
- **SB 253** mandates GHG Protocol methods for corporate-level disclosure (→ [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253]])

AB 32 (HSC §38562) requires that adopted regulations:
- Minimize costs while maximizing benefits
- Avoid disproportionate impacts on low-income communities
- Minimize **leakage** — GHG emissions shifting outside California in response to California-only regulations
- Complement air quality and toxic reduction efforts

**GHGs covered:** Carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, hydrofluorocarbons, perfluorocarbons, sulfur hexafluoride, and nitrogen trifluoride. Short-lived climate pollutants (including black carbon) are addressed in subsequent implementing regulations.

**"Market-based compliance mechanism" definition (HSC §38570):** Either (1) a declining annual aggregate emissions limitation system (cap-and-trade), or (2) GHG exchanges, banking, credits meeting specified environmental equivalency requirements. This statutory definition frames the legal character of the Cap-and-Trade Regulation.
