## Overview

California SB 219 amends both SB 253 and SB 261 — the two foundational California corporate climate disclosure laws — introducing timeline flexibility and structural clarifications. It is grouped with SB 253 and SB 261 by CARB as "the 200s" (Health and Safety Code §38532–38533).

SB 219 does not create new disclosure obligations of its own. It modifies how existing obligations under SB 253 and SB 261 are implemented.

(→ [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253 — CCDAA]]) (→ [[regulations/sb261|SB 261 — Climate-Related Financial Risk Disclosure]]) (→ [[sources/sb219-bill-text|SB 219 Enrolled Bill Text]])

## Who Must Comply

SB 219 does not independently create covered entity obligations. It applies to all entities subject to SB 253 and/or SB 261.

## Reporting Requirements

SB 219 makes four amendments to the SB 253/261 framework:

**1. CARB-set Scope 3 schedule (amends SB 253)**
The original statute required Scope 3 disclosure "no later than 180 days after" Scope 1 and 2 disclosures. SB 219 removed this fixed relationship and replaced it with "a schedule specified by the state board." This is the direct legal basis for the 2027 Scope 3 start date established in the CARB Initial Regulation. (→ [[sources/sb219-bill-text|SB 219 Bill Text]])

**2. Parent-level consolidation (amends SB 253 and SB 261)**
A parent company may file a single consolidated climate disclosure report satisfying the obligations of qualifying subsidiary companies. Subsidiaries covered by a parent's consolidated report are not required to file separately. This applies to both the GHG emissions inventory (SB 253) and the climate risk report (SB 261). (→ [[sources/sb219-bill-text|SB 219 Bill Text]])

**3. Fee authority moved from statute to regulation**
Both original statutes required entities to pay an annual fee "upon filing the disclosure." SB 219 removed this language from both statutes, not to eliminate fees, but to place fee authority in CARB's rulemaking process. The Initial Regulation (Title 17 CCR §§96073–96074) is the resulting mechanism. (→ [[sources/carb-sb253-261-adopted-regulation|Adopted Regulation]])

**4. Contracting authority made discretionary**
Both statutes originally required CARB to contract with an emissions reporting organization. SB 219 changed "shall" to "may" for both programs, giving CARB flexibility to perform functions directly rather than through a third-party organization.

## Deadlines

SB 219 does not set its own reporting deadlines. It defers to the deadlines established under SB 253 and SB 261.

## Penalties

SB 219 does not establish its own penalty structure. Penalties remain as defined in SB 253 (up to $500,000/year) and SB 261 (up to $50,000/year).

## Calculation Requirements

None specific to SB 219. See [[regulations/sb253-ccdaa|SB 253]] for GHG Protocol calculation requirements.
