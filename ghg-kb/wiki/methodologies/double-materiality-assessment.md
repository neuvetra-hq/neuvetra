## Overview

The **Double Materiality Assessment (DMA)** is the gateway process for every CSRD reporter: it determines which sustainability matters are material and therefore which ESRS disclosure requirements apply. ESRS 1 Chapter 3 codifies the *concept* of double materiality (two perspectives — impact and financial); ESRS 1 Chapter 5 codifies the *value chain extension* (assessment must reach beyond the financial-consolidation perimeter). EFRAG's two implementation guidance documents — **IG 1 (Materiality Assessment)** and **IG 2 (Value Chain)** — operationalise these chapters into an executable process. They are designed to be applied **jointly**: the materiality assessment of IG 1 is incomplete without the value-chain mechanics of IG 2.

This methodology page collects the EFRAG-recommended process, the impact and financial threshold-setting mechanics, the value-chain boundary rules, and the data-collection tiering. It is a practitioner page; for the conceptual framing of double materiality see [[concepts/double-materiality|Double Materiality]]. For phase-in interactions see [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in]].

The DMA is not itself a GHG calculation — it sits **upstream** of all GHG accounting. The output of the DMA determines whether ESRS E1 (Climate Change) applies in full, in part, or via the climate-specific non-materiality forward-looking justification (ESRS 1 ¶32). Once material, GHG quantification proceeds under E1-6 using the GHG Protocol family of standards.

## When To Use

Run the DMA:

- **Before** the first ESRS sustainability statement, to determine the in-scope DRs.
- **At each annual report cycle**, to confirm whether material IROs have changed (per IG 1 FAQ 7, the assessment should be updated at least annually and whenever business-model, value-chain, or external-context changes are significant).
- **When entering CSRD scope** for the first time (e.g. crossing thresholds, listing on an EU-regulated market, or a parent-level consolidation change).
- **When a major event** (acquisition, divestment, new geography, regulatory shock, supplier disruption) plausibly shifts the IRO landscape.

The DMA is mandatory for every CSRD reporter — there is no materiality test that can switch off ESRS 1 or ESRS 2; only topical standards (E1–G1) are activated or deactivated by the DMA outcome.

## Step-by-Step

The four steps below follow EFRAG IG 1 §3 (paragraphs 63–99). Steps A–C apply to **own operations and the value chain together** (per ESRS 1 ¶63 and IG 2 §2). Step D feeds the disclosure templates of ESRS 2.

### Step A — Understand the context

(IG 1 §3.1, ¶64–73)

Inputs: business model, products and services, geographies, regulatory landscape, peer benchmarking, prior-period sustainability statement.

Tasks:

1. Map the **business model** — products, services, segments, processes, key inputs and outputs.
2. Map the **value chain** — direct (tier-1) suppliers, tier-N upstream actors, distribution channels, customers, end-of-life paths. Identify geographies, sectors, and ownership / contractual relationships in each segment.
3. Identify **affected stakeholders** in own operations and value chain: workers, communities, suppliers, customers, end-users, environment as silent stakeholder.
4. Inventory the **regulatory landscape** relevant to the undertaking and its value chain (EU + non-EU, sector-specific).
5. Catalogue **prior IROs** identified in any previous sustainability or risk-management exercise (NFRD, GRI, TCFD, internal ERM, supplier audits).

Deliverable: a context dossier feeding Step B's identification work.

### Step B — Identify the long list of IROs

(IG 1 §3.2, ¶74–82, FAQ 8)

Tasks:

1. Use **ESRS 1 Application Requirement 16** (the topical / sub-topic / sub-sub-topic table) as the *starting* reference list. AR 16 lists the sustainability matters covered by ESRS topical standards.
2. Identify **entity-specific matters** not covered by AR 16 but material to the undertaking (FAQ 8: AR 16 is a starting point, not a closed list).
3. For each topic, identify **actual and potential IROs** across own operations and value chain — both negative and positive impacts; both risks and opportunities.
4. For value-chain IROs, focus on **hotspots** — areas of severe actual or potential impacts on people / environment, and relationships critical to dependencies (per IG 2 ¶32 — leverage does NOT determine materiality, only data-collection ability).

Deliverable: a long-list of IROs by topic, with each IRO tagged by location (own ops / upstream / downstream), time horizon, and stakeholder affected.

### Step C — Assess materiality and set thresholds

(IG 1 §3.3, §3.6, §3.7)

This step has two parallel sub-assessments — impact and financial — plus consolidation of outcomes.

#### C.1 — Impact materiality (IG 1 §3.6, ¶113–159)

Severity is the determinant for negative impacts. Severity is assessed **from the perspective of the affected people / environment**, not the undertaking. Three factors:

| Factor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Scale** | How grave the impact is — depth of harm, infringement of basic rights / freedoms / dignity |
| **Scope** | How widespread the impact is — number of people / extent of environment affected |
| **Irremediable character** | Whether the impact can be remediated, restored, or compensated (irreversible impacts weigh more heavily) |

Decision rules:

- For **actual** negative impacts: severity alone determines materiality.
- For **potential** negative impacts: severity AND likelihood.
- For **potential negative human-rights** impacts: **severity takes precedence over likelihood** (IG 1 ¶45, restating ESRS 1 ¶45).
- For **positive** impacts: scale and scope (+ likelihood if potential).

Threshold-setting:

- Thresholds may be **qualitative or quantitative** (no single ESRS-mandated value).
- Thresholds must be **disclosed** under ESRS 2 IRO-1 and applied **consistently** across the assessment.
- IG 1 Figures 4–5 illustrate severity-likelihood matrices; thresholds may be set by combining severity bands (low / moderate / high / very high) with likelihood bands (rare / possible / likely / very likely).

#### C.2 — Financial materiality (IG 1 §3.7, ¶126–137)

A matter is financially material if it triggers — or could reasonably be expected to trigger — material financial effects on:

- Development, financial position, financial performance, cash flows
- Access to finance, cost of capital

Over short / medium / long-term horizons (per ESRS 1 Chapter 6 horizons: short = reporting period, medium = up to 5 years, long = beyond 5 years).

Threshold-setting:

- **Quantitative thresholds**: absolute monetary amounts (e.g. EUR 5M effect) or relative thresholds (% of revenue, total assets, net income, equity, free cash flow).
- **Qualitative factors**: dependencies on natural / human / social resources, reputational risks affecting cost of capital, regulatory exposure, strategic / business-model implications.
- Effects **not yet on the balance sheet** count — IG 1 ¶133–135 makes this explicit. A reputational risk that has not yet materialised but plausibly could move cost of capital is financially material.
- Likelihood × magnitude logic applies; for high-magnitude / low-likelihood scenarios, a separate qualitative assessment may be more useful than a probability-weighted EUR figure.

#### C.3 — Consolidate and consider interaction (IG 1 §3.3.3, ¶99–100)

- A matter is **material if either dimension is material** (impact OR financial).
- The two perspectives **interact** — an impact that today has no financial effect may become financially material as regulation, stakeholder expectations, or stranded-asset dynamics evolve.
- **Material IROs from actions** must be considered (ESRS 1 ¶52–53): a decarbonisation plan that closes a product line may create material workforce risks; intersectional impacts must be disclosed.

### Step D — Report

(IG 1 §3.4, ¶85–91)

The DMA outcome feeds three ESRS 2 Disclosure Requirements:

| DR | What it carries from the DMA |
|---|---|
| **ESRS 2 IRO-1** | Description of the materiality-assessment process: methodology, threshold-setting, stakeholder engagement, scenario use, scope (own ops vs value chain), frequency |
| **ESRS 2 IRO-2** | List of all DRs covered by the sustainability statement, including phased-in / omitted / entity-specific items; for ESRS E1, the ESRS 1 ¶32 climate-non-materiality forward-looking justification (when applicable) sits here |
| **ESRS 2 SBM-3** | Material IROs by topic, time horizons, value-chain location, anticipated financial effects (subject to first-year and 3-year qualitative-only relief — see [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in]]) |

## Data Requirements

### Stakeholder engagement (IG 1 §3.5, FAQ 15–17)

ESRS does **not** mandate a specific engagement method — but the undertaking **must disclose whether and how engagement occurred** (IRO-1).

| Stakeholder type | Role | Engagement examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Affected stakeholders** (workers, communities, suppliers, customers, environment-as-silent-stakeholder) | Inform **impact** materiality | HR pulse surveys, supplier audits, community consultations, NGO partnerships, grievance channels |
| **Users of sustainability statements** (investors, lenders, business partners, ratings agencies) | Inform **financial** materiality | Investor day Q&A, lender covenants, ESG-rating dialogues, customer ESG questionnaires |

Engagement may piggy-back on existing business processes; greenfield engagement is not required. **Silent stakeholders** (the environment, future generations) are represented through proxies — scientific evidence, expert NGOs, regulatory frameworks (IG 1 FAQ 17).

### Value-chain data — tiered approach (IG 2 §3, FAQ 8–9, ¶152–162)

Apply this order of preference for value-chain metric data:

| Tier | Source | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Direct data** | Major tier-1 suppliers / customers — questionnaires, contractual clauses, audits, real-time integrations | Material relationships where the undertaking has leverage and the data is feasible to collect |
| **2 — Sector / regional / country averages** | Sector benchmarks, regional risk databases (e.g. WRI Aqueduct for water, ITUC labour-rights index, country-level GHG factor databases) | Tier-1 data unavailable after reasonable effort, OR for tier-2+ actors where direct collection is impractical |
| **3 — Product-level proxies / estimates** | Lifecycle databases, EEIO factors (e.g. EPA Supply Chain GHG EF), spend-based methods | Sector data too coarse, or for very granular product-level disclosures |

**"Reasonable effort" is proportional** (IG 2 ¶160): the undertaking is not expected to quantify impacts of thousands of tier-3 suppliers, but tier-1 major suppliers and customers must be engaged. The undertaking discloses basis, accuracy, and planned improvements (ESRS 2 ¶10).

**Worked example (IG 2 ¶161):** a beverage company with material Cat 11 (Use of Sold Products) emissions from customer-owned cooling fridges may **estimate** electricity consumption rather than measure each unit, provided the basis is disclosed.

### Reporting boundary (IG 2 §2.3, ¶34–57)

Two distinct boundary concepts apply:

| Concept | Definition | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reporting undertaking** | Same parent + subsidiary group as the financial statements (full **financial-control** consolidation) | Entire ESRS sustainability statement — the floor of own-operations reporting |
| **Operational control** (environmental-only) | Sites or entities the undertaking has authority to direct operating activities at (regardless of equity / financial control) | ESRS E1 / E2 / E4 — **separate** disclosure of 100% Scope 1, pollution, biodiversity from operationally-controlled assets (E1 ¶50(b)). Does **NOT** apply to social standards (S1–S4) |
| **Value chain extension** | Material IROs in business relationships **outside** the consolidation perimeter (upstream + downstream) | Topical disclosures where matter is material in the value chain (e.g. Scope 3 in E1, value-chain workers in S2, communities in S3) |

For the financial-statement consolidation perimeter ↔ Scope 3 mechanics (especially **Cat 15 Investments** for associates / JVs), see [[concepts/scope-3|Scope 3]] and [[regulations/esrs-e1|ESRS E1]].

## Worked Example

(Adapted from IG 2 Seren Group case study, ¶141–150)

**Seren Group** is a hypothetical EU manufacturing parent with the following structure:

| Entity | Ownership | Financial-statement treatment | Operational-control? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent (Seren Group) | 100% | Full consolidation | Yes |
| Subsidiary A (textile mill) | 100% | Full consolidation | Yes |
| Subsidiary B (logistics co) | 65% | Full consolidation | Yes |
| Joint operation C (dye plant) | 50% (operated by Seren) | Proportional consolidation per IFRS 11 | Yes |
| Joint venture D (retail JV) | 50% (operated by partner) | Equity method | No |
| Associate E (fabric supplier) | 25% | Equity method | No |

**Reporting boundary for ESRS sustainability statement:** Parent + A + B + 50% of C (financial-control consolidation perimeter from the financial statements).

**ESRS E1 ¶50(a) — Scope 1/2/3 metrics:** report consolidated Scope 1 / 2 / 3 from Parent + A + B + 50% of C.

**ESRS E1 ¶50(b) — separate operational-control disclosure:** **additionally** disclose 100% of Scope 1 / 2 from Parent + A + B + C (full operational-control footprint, including the 50% of C not consolidated and any operationally-directed associate assets). This is a *separate line item*, not netted against E1 ¶50(a).

**ESRS E1 ¶51 — Scope 3 Cat 15 (Investments):** emissions from D and E (where they are *not* in operational control and are equity-method investees) are reported as Cat 15 Investments. PCAF attribution methodology applies (→ [[methodologies/scope-3-cat15-financed-emissions|Scope 3 Cat 15]]).

**Value-chain (upstream / downstream) disclosures:** material IROs in tier-1 fabric suppliers, dye chemical suppliers, downstream apparel brands using Seren's textiles, end-of-life garment treatment — reported per ESRS 2 SBM-3 by location, with hotspot identification (e.g. cotton-growing water stress, dye chemical labour conditions).

## Limitations

- **EFRAG IGs are non-authoritative.** Where IG 1 / IG 2 appears to contradict ESRS 1, ESRS 2, or any topical standard, **ESRS take precedence** (IG 1 disclaimer p. 1). Use the IGs as a *useful default*, not as a binding rule.
- **No prescribed quantitative thresholds.** Threshold-setting requires judgment, peer benchmarking, and stakeholder dialogue. Different undertakings in the same sector may legitimately set different thresholds — but each must disclose and justify under IRO-1.
- **Value-chain data is partial.** The 3-year transitional provision (ESRS 1 §10.2) acknowledges that complete value-chain data is rarely available in year 1; preparers must disclose efforts and limitations rather than achieving false precision.
- **Sector-specific standards not yet adopted.** When EFRAG / Commission adopt sector-specific ESRS, sub-topics may shift from entity-specific to standardised — preparers should re-run Step B against the new AR 16-equivalent.
- **DMA does not extend to LSME suppliers beyond what LSME standards require.** The LSME cap (IG 2 ¶94) limits compliance push-down — preparers cannot mandate LSMEs in their value chain to provide data the LSME standards would not require.
- **DMA outcomes interact with phase-in.** A topic assessed material may still benefit from size-conditional, first-year, or 3-year qualitative-only reliefs. See [[concepts/esrs-phase-in|ESRS Phase-in]].
