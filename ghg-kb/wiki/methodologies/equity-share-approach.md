## Overview

The equity share approach is one of three methods for setting an organizational boundary under the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard. Under this approach, a company **consolidates GHG emissions in proportion to its equity share** in each operation — regardless of whether it has operational or financial control.

If a company has a 30% equity stake in a joint venture, it accounts for 30% of that venture's emissions. If it wholly owns a subsidiary, it accounts for 100%.

(→ [[sources/ghg-protocol-corporate-standard|GHG Protocol Corporate Standard, Chapter 3]])

## When To Use

- When the primary goal is to understand **financial exposure** to GHG risk proportional to ownership
- When the company holds minority interests in multiple joint ventures and wants a proportionate view
- When required by a specific reporting program that mandates equity share consolidation
- As an alternative to control-based approaches when the company does not operate its assets directly

The revised edition allows companies to report under a **single approach** — equity share, financial control, or operational control — rather than requiring both equity and control (as the first edition did).

## Step-by-Step

1. **Identify all operations** within the organizational boundary — wholly owned subsidiaries, joint ventures, associated companies, leased assets.

2. **Determine equity percentage** for each operation (ownership stake, not necessarily equal to voting rights).

3. **Obtain total emissions** for each operation (Scope 1 and 2 at minimum).

4. **Multiply** each operation's total emissions by the company's equity share percentage.

5. **Sum** the proportional emissions across all operations to get the consolidated inventory total.

6. **Apply consistently** across all reporting periods. Changes in equity stakes require recalculation of the base year if significant.

## Data Requirements

- Legal/corporate structure documentation (ownership percentages for each entity)
- Emissions data for each entity (Scope 1 and 2)
- Documentation of any equity changes during the reporting period
- Disclosure of minority-owned operations included or excluded

## Worked Example

Company A owns 100% of Plant X, 50% of JV Y, and 25% of Facility Z.

| Operation | Total Emissions (tCO₂e) | Equity % | Company A's Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant X | 10,000 | 100% | 10,000 |
| JV Y | 8,000 | 50% | 4,000 |
| Facility Z | 6,000 | 25% | 1,500 |
| **Total** | | | **15,500** |

## Limitations

- **No double-counting guarantee for the atmosphere:** The same physical emissions at JV Y are split between all equity partners — none accounts for 100%, so aggregate corporate accounting across the economy may leave gaps.
- **Data access challenge:** Minority shareholders may not have access to detailed emissions data from operations they don't control.
- **Less intuitive for operational management:** Managers responsible for operating decisions typically control 100% of a facility — the proportional view may not align with where reduction levers sit.
- **Removed minimum threshold:** The revised edition removed the minimum equity threshold — companies should include even small equity interests where emissions are significant.
